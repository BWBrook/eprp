# Independent research-question consultation: the Ediacaran–Cambrian world

**Prepared for Barry Brook · 21 October 2026**

## Scope, judgement and reading conventions

This consultation uses public literature and public research archives. No repository, working notes, personal documents or previous research proposals of yours were searched or consulted.

I recommend starting with **Question 1, oxygen exchange around Ediacaran animal forests**, and **Question 2, the independence of Cambrian extinction chronologies**. They offer complementary returns: a mechanistic experiment that existing fossils can constrain, and an audit that can establish which historical claims the available evidence can actually support. The first makes a substantive case for substantial simulation; the second should begin with a small, carefully documented analysis.

I retain **six questions**. Additional ideas on small shelly fossil reconstruction, repaired-injury estimates of predation and finer Sinsk functional recovery did not clear both the distinction-from-prior-work and verified-data-start gates. The shortlist is therefore selective across your interval; it is not a comprehensive research agenda. Question 3 is the strongest inexpensive alternative if you want a shorter route to a decisive result. I rank the two deep dives by their potential scientific payoff and complementarity, rather than by novelty probability alone.

The questions below concern specific gaps, not broad claims that oxygen, preservation or ecological interactions have been overlooked. “Novel” means that the proposed discriminating test, at the stated scope, appears substantially unanswered. A matching study was **not found in my search**; that is not a claim that nothing exists. Probabilities are subjective assessments, rounded to five percentage points, with substantial uncertainty. “Answerable” includes a useful, quantitatively demonstrated limit on identification, but does not include an unconstrained simulation presented as historical explanation. Where those outcomes differ materially, I give separate probabilities.

**Reading labels:** **full text** means the main article was consulted beyond its abstract, including relevant methods and results; supplementary material is identified separately when inspected. **Abstract only** also covers cases where isolated additional excerpts were available but the whole article was not examined. No citation is presented as verified solely from recollection. Full titles and DOI links appear in the reference register. Archive verification distinguishes downloaded contents from a visible file listing or an author's availability statement.

Dates in Ma are approximate orientation labels. I do not assume that the White Sea–Nama turnover, the regional Kotlinian crisis and the end-Ediacaran losses are one event, or that competing Cambrian numerical timescales are interchangeable.

Questions **1–4 and 6** use data or methods released from 2023 onward.

### 1. Can losing oxygen consumers leave their neighbours short of oxygen?

**Question.** Does the published conclusion that canopy mixing prevents substantial resource depletion hold in dense, heterogeneous Ediacaran communities, and can removing a structural taxon reduce oxygen supply to surviving low-tier organisms despite lowering respiratory demand?

**Why it matters.** This tests the domain of an established physical result and a possible mechanism of secondary ecological loss. A negative result would validate efficient mixing across newer geometries and eliminate an attractive oxygen-mediated cascade mechanism.

**Closest prior work.** Ghisalberti et al. (2014), [10.1016/j.cub.2013.12.017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2013.12.017), **full main text; supplement not read**, already model uptake and argue that mixing prevents appreciable community depletion: this is the null to test. Gutarra et al. (2024), [10.1016/j.cub.2024.04.059](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2024.04.059), **full text**, resolve Avalon community flow without the proposed removal experiment. Their 2025 extension, [10.1093/pnasnexus/pgaf346](https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgaf346), **full text**, reveals nearly stagnant dense-Funisia interiors, a specific challenge to generalizing the earlier mixing result, but does not solve that oxygen test.

**Decisive test.** Reproduce the earlier mixing–uptake calculation; then compare dense and clustered geometries, inert canopies around identical recipient sinks, morphologies at equal potential demand, and living-taxon removal. Simulation is decisive for controlled counterfactual communities. Living uptake alone is not a new contribution.

**Evidence.** Zenodo [10.5281/zenodo.11001479](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.11001479) and [10.5281/zenodo.17035671](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.17035671): community files downloaded; one actual 2024 STEP geometry checked; large archives inventoried. These include fossil-informed *simulated* communities. Main identification problem: unknown living anatomy, physiology and flow, rather than unavailable gross geometry.

**Cheapest first step.** Within a week, reproduce the 2014 depletion bound and substitute exchange estimates for the dense 2025 setting. Stop if conservative physiological bounds still imply negligible depletion and recipient effects.

**Probabilities.** Novel **65%** for this narrow heterogeneity/removal test; answerable within two years **75%** for bounded mechanisms, approximately **25%** for the actual historical sign or an extinction cause.

### 2. How much Cambrian synchrony is measured, and how much is imposed?

**Question.** Which claims about the order and interregional synchrony of end-Marjuman trilobite loss, SPICE onset and redox change remain identified after removing correlation constraints derived from those same events?

**Why it matters.** A common chronology can inadvertently supply the temporal agreement later invoked to explain an extinction. A reassuring null would establish that conclusions survive this audit; loss of resolution would not establish diachroneity.

**Closest prior work.** Farrell et al. (2025), [10.1130/B37919.1](https://doi.org/10.1130/B37919.1), **full text; supplementary methods inspected**, already estimate local lag and compare age models; the new test concerns dependence between evidence and alignment. Schiffbauer et al. (2017), [10.1126/sciadv.1602158](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.1602158), **full text**, establish facies-dependent event expression, not this dependence audit. Pulsipher et al. (2021), [10.1016/j.earscirev.2020.103442](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.earscirev.2020.103442), **full text**, compile and analyse SPICE records, but do not independently calibrate every record's clock.

**Decisive test.** Map each age constraint to its original observation; compare published, weakened-correlation and local-depth-order analyses. Keep isotope onset distinct from redox onset. The core provenance and local-order audit needs neither simulation nor heavy computation. Only if independently anchored regional records justify a global model, simulate known offsets to calibrate its uncertainty claims.

**Evidence.** Farrell's public supplement, [10.1130/GSAB.S.28207634](https://doi.org/10.1130/GSAB.S.28207634): PDF, R code, model-input workbook and Smithfield isotope workbook downloaded. The complete underlying CONOP occurrence compilation was **not** found publicly in those files. SPICEraq's article and archive description were inspected; its raw workbook download was not verified. Principal identification problem: dependent dating and correlation, followed by incomplete fossil detection.

**Cheapest first step.** Reproduce one local onset calculation and inventory all age inputs. Within five days establish whether the desired conclusion has independently informative constraints.

**Probabilities.** Novel **75%**; useful audit answerable within two years **85%**. Resolving worldwide biological synchrony to substantially better than one million years using existing data: approximately **40%**.

### 3. Does the apparent improvement in animal movement survive equal image quality?

**Question.** Does the reported late-Ediacaran increase in horizontal-trail smoothness persist under a common native-resolution, trace-width and preservation observation model?

**Why it matters.** A surviving geometric shift would strengthen evidence for changing movement; its disappearance would weaken this argument for improved motor coordination or sensory performance. Even robust geometry would not identify physiology uniquely.

**Closest prior work.** Wang et al. (2025), [10.1098/rspb.2025.0889](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2025.0889), **full text and supplements**, report the temporal shift and test repeat digitisation and curve-length convergence, but do not validate recovery against native image resolution. Wang and Chen (2026), [10.1016/j.palaeo.2026.113973](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.palaeo.2026.113973), **full text**, investigate locomotion, sensory range and environmental effects, without that measurement calibration. Environmental modulation itself is therefore not the proposed novelty.

**Decisive test.** Blindly re-digitise original images and known high-resolution curves after controlled blur, rasterisation and downsampling. Compare ages only where native pixels-per-trace-width, normalised path length, sediment texture and preservation overlap; validate across withheld publications and formations. Ask whether unchanged latent movement can reproduce the temporal shift through this independently calibrated observation process. Upsampling alone proves neither biology nor artefact. A workstation suffices.

**Evidence.** Wang's 2025 compilation covers 172 fossil occurrences from 38 units. Figshare [10.6084/m9.figshare.c.7881988](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.c.7881988): specimen/source spreadsheet and methods document downloaded and inspected. Zenodo [10.5281/zenodo.15079899](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.15079899): metadata lists code and two example coordinate files; a complete original-image/coordinate corpus is not supplied there, and individual code downloads failed. Main identification problem: age, taphonomy and source-image quality may covary.

**Cheapest first step.** In three to five days, degrade and re-digitise 20–30 known curves over the actual image-quality range. Deprioritise the artefact explanation if calibrated errors are too small to explain a consequential fraction of the reported shift.

**Probabilities.** Novel **80%**; answerable within two years **70%**, conditional on recovering sufficient original images with overlapping observation quality.

### 4. Could changing currents imitate evidence for stolon reproduction?

**Question.** Can waterborne recruitment under time-variable currents reproduce the isotropic, size-structured clustering attributed to stoloniferous reproduction on Avalon bedding surfaces while remaining consistent with independent evidence of physical connections?

**Why it matters.** This challenges the identifiability of a reproductive mechanism from spatial pattern. A successful alternative would narrow reproductive and diversification claims; failure would strengthen the stolon interpretation. Waterborne dispersal is not synonymous with sex: buds and fragments can disperse.

**Closest prior work.** Mitchell and Manica (2026), [10.1038/s41559-026-03094-2](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41559-026-03094-2), **full text**, connect clustering, resource competition and diversification, without explicitly reconstructing recruitment under changing currents. Mitchell et al. (2015), [10.1038/nature14646](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature14646), **abstract only**, establish the size-clustering argument. Delahooke et al. (2024), [10.1098/rsos.231601](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.231601), **abstract only**, already combine flow and spatial modelling for aligned fronds; this precludes claiming the general approach is new. Liu and Dunn (2020), [10.1016/j.cub.2020.01.052](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2020.01.052), **abstract only**, describe physical connections, an independent constraint rather than a population-wide dispersal estimate.

**Decisive test.** Compare marked point-process generators with identical abundance, growth and observation windows, including mixtures of stolon extension and propagule recruitment under steady, reversing and rotating currents. Predict withheld size–distance–orientation statistics. Physical connections constrain permissible clonal contributions; they are not evidence to explain away. Simulation measures classification error between plausible mechanisms.

**Evidence.** The public [MitchellManica2026 archive](https://github.com/egmitchell/MitchellManica2026) and README were inspected. Bed B data are explicitly withheld under geoconservation conditions; no access is assumed. Main identification problem: lifetime currents and recruitment episodes are unobserved; burial orientation need not represent either.

**Cheapest first step.** In two days, combine perpendicular recruitment pulses, apply the published isotropy statistic and determine whether it falsely diagnoses a stolon process.

**Probabilities.** Novel **70%**; answerable within two years **75%** for mechanism discrimination/calibration, approximately **40%** for assigning historical reproductive proportions.

### 5. Did ecosystem engineers exclude organisms, or their fossil evidence?

**Question.** Does the association between late-Ediacaran bioturbation and separation of Ediacara-type from Cambrian-type taxa survive controls for differential detection, reporting effort and the scale used to define a community?

**Why it matters.** Bioturbation can change habitat and preservation simultaneously. Their contributions must be separated before negative co-occurrence supports biological replacement. A residual effect would strengthen that evidence without proving competition; its absence would weaken this particular argument, not demonstrate that the organisms survived.

**Closest prior work.** Craffey et al. (2024), [10.1098/rspb.2024.2029](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2024.2029), **full text**, already test broad lithological and biomineralization effects: the gap is differential detection *within* those categories and aggregation/reporting sensitivity. Boag et al. (2016), [10.1017/pab.2016.20](https://doi.org/10.1017/pab.2016.20), **abstract only**, test assemblage concepts and broad preservational alternatives, not this conditional effect. Buatois et al. (2014), [10.1038/ncomms4544](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms4544), **abstract only**, document persistent matground conditions without characteristic body fossils; this is important contrary evidence against explaining all disappearance by preservation.

**Decisive test.** Recode documented absences versus unreported observations, compare matched beds/collections, and vary spatial and stratigraphic aggregation. Estimate how strong differential detection must be to remove the association. If independent detection information is absent, report bounds rather than an apparently identified occupancy model. No large simulation is required.

**Evidence.** Craffey's Dryad [10.5061/dryad.69p8cz9cc](https://doi.org/10.5061/dryad.69p8cz9cc): README and named occurrence, environmental and code files verified; raw-download access was not confirmed. Published source references provide the audit trail. Main problem: preservation and reporting act on both putative cause and response.

**Cheapest first step.** Audit twenty original records and test whether engineer-present and engineer-absent samples overlap in region, age and preservational conditions. Stop if meaningful matched comparisons do not exist.

**Probabilities.** Novel **70%**; answerable within two years **65%**. A bound on the ecological interpretation is more realistic than a unique estimate of historical exclusion.

### 6. Could burrows connect oxygen refuges without oxygenating the sediment overall?

**Question.** Could trace-constrained Ediacaran–Terreneuvian burrow geometries create persistent, connected oxygenated sediment patches large enough for infauna while mean oxygen penetration remained shallow?

**Why it matters.** Expanded infaunal habitat might not require widespread sediment oxygenation. A negative result would restrict oxygen benefits to burrow occupants or require supplies unsupported by the evidence.

**Closest prior work.** Cribb et al. (2023), [10.1111/gbi.12550](https://doi.org/10.1111/gbi.12550), **full text**, model average oxygen penetration and explicitly acknowledge local halos, without quantifying their connectivity. Meysman et al. (2010), [10.1357/002224010793721406](https://doi.org/10.1357/002224010793721406), **abstract only; model setup also inspected**, derive local oxygen halos, not Cambrian habitat connectivity. Gougeon et al. (2024 online/2025 volume), [10.1016/j.dib.2024.111258](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dib.2024.111258), **full text**, supply fossil dimensions and facies, not oxygen fields. Howman et al. (2024), [10.1038/s41597-024-03557-w](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-024-03557-w), **full text**, supply modern geometries, not Cambrian ventilation rates.

**Decisive test.** Hold total oxygen input, excavated volume and sediment oxygen demand fixed while varying branching, spacing and depth within fossil bounds. Solve reactive transport and measure connected sediment above alternative oxygen thresholds, with passage widths sufficient for finite-sized bodies and persistence exceeding transit times. Exclude occupied lumens. Compare equal-volume disconnected geometries. Three-dimensional simulation is decisive because averaging removes connectivity.

**Evidence.** Chapel Island Mendeley [10.17632/g2g74cj4x7.2](https://doi.org/10.17632/g2g74cj4x7.2): two PDF logs listed, one downloaded; no complete photo archive verified. Howman's Dataverse [10.7910/DVN/3DU1VW](https://doi.org/10.7910/DVN/3DU1VW): 38 CT volumes listed; metadata, README and 35-replicate morphology table downloaded, volumes not downloaded. Main identification problem: geometry does not establish ventilation, simultaneous occupancy or physiological tolerance.

**Cheapest first step.** Apply cylindrical diffusion and oxygen-budget bounds to fossil widths and spacing. Stop if even generous bounded supply cannot create traversable connected habitat, before building a three-dimensional model.

**Probabilities.** Novel **70%**; answerable within two years **55%** as a constrained feasibility bound. Actual historical colonisation would remain unproven.

## Part B — Deep dives on the two strongest candidates

### B1. Testing efficient oxygen mixing in dense animal forests

#### The claim worth testing

The useful question is whether an established result about efficient canopy mixing survives the dense and heterogeneous community geometries now available, and what that implies for survivors after a structural taxon disappears. A canopy can exchange oxygen with overlying water, consume imported oxygen and shelter poorly flushed pockets. Its removal changes transport and demand together. Neither the direction of the net effect nor its historical importance should be assumed.

The prior result is stronger than a generic claim that organisms deflect currents. Ghisalberti et al. (2014) [R3] modelled surface uptake and argued that vertical mixing prevents uptake or benthic release from maintaining substantial concentration gradients within their Avalon communities. Height remained advantageous through access to velocity and thinner boundary layers. I read the full main text; the supplement containing additional calculations was not read. Recovering and reproducing that calculation is a first-week requirement. Simply adding living consumption to a canopy model would not establish novelty.

Gutarra et al. (2024, 2025) [R1–R2] provide the specific opportunity: detailed geometries and sharply different flow regimes, including dense Funisia with little interior throughflow despite turbulence above the canopy. Does that turbulence exchange water with the recipients, or mostly pass over them? The proposed contribution is a test of the earlier result's domain of validity, followed by structural-taxon removal under controlled demand. Butterfield also explicitly interprets canopy effects in terms of gas exchange, DOI [10.1017/S0016756820000734](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0016756820000734) [R24; **abstract only** under this report's convention: selected sections inspected, not full text]. Oxygen transfer itself is an established idea. The particular dense-canopy/removal test was **not found in my search**.

This is a good use of your computational capacity because the crucial comparison is counterfactual. The same community cannot be observed with and without a structurally important rangeomorph, with biomass conserved, under identical currents. An explicitly bounded simulation can make that comparison. It cannot recover an extinct animal's respiration by computational force.

#### Competing hypotheses and discriminating predictions

**H1: transport benefit dominates.** A canopy increases oxygen delivery to standard low-tier recipients across the justified physiological domain. Under matched respiratory demand, communities classified as strong mixers also perform well in recipient uptake. Removing a large structural organism can worsen oxygen supply despite reducing total consumption.

**H2: consumption or sheltering dominates.** Relative to the same recipients on the reference bed without the canopy, added canopy demand or suppressed exchange reduces uptake; canopy removal improves recipient supply. A living canopy performing worse than its identical inert counterpart is not a discriminator: with fixed flow and nonnegative added sinks, that respiratory cost is expected under H1 as well. High spatial variation in vertical velocity need not predict the net effect.

**H3: the interaction changes sign.** Sparse or well-flushed canopies facilitate neighbours; sufficiently dense, slowly ventilated or strongly respiring communities compete for oxygen. Morphology changes the location of that transition. This predicts a reproducible boundary in parameter space, not merely a collection of contrasting visualisations.

**H0: the ecological effect is small or not robustly identifiable.** Differences are below a declared practical threshold, or reasonable anatomy, flow and uptake assumptions reverse the answer. This is a valid endpoint. It would establish that available geometry supports an effect on flow more confidently than an effect on oxygen-dependent habitat.

These hypotheses follow a preliminary test: does appreciable interior depletion occur at all under independently bounded demand? Efficient bulk mixing can coexist with local boundary-layer limitation, so concentration homogeneity and recipient uptake require separate diagnostics. H3 must not become the guaranteed answer produced by varying respiration from zero to infinity. The admissible parameter domain must be justified independently of the desired result. A transition outside that domain is not evidence for historical competition.

#### Define the response before constructing the model

Use a fixed set of small recipient surfaces near the bed as the primary assay. They represent a standard oxygen demand placed at identical locations across counterfactual treatments. Their dimensions and uptake law stay unchanged. They need not be assigned a disputed taxonomic identity.

Measure their oxygen uptake, the fraction meeting prescribed fractions of potential demand, and the lower tail of the uptake distribution. A community mean can hide a small, consistently deprived neighbourhood. Report oxygen concentration beside these surfaces separately from flux into them. Low concentration can accompany vigorous uptake; high ambient concentration does not guarantee effective transport across a boundary layer.

For a recipient with realised uptake \(J_r\) and a specified potential demand \(J_{r,\max}\), a useful response is \(f_r=J_r/J_{r,\max}\). Define the neighbour effect as

\[
\Delta f_r=f_r(\text{living canopy})-f_r(\text{reference}).
\]

Summarise its distribution across recipient locations rather than only its average. A secondary response is the spatial volume above a range of oxygen concentrations. Those concentration thresholds are scenario assumptions, not reconstructed Ediacaran tolerance measurements. A third response is total community oxygen consumption, needed for mass balance and interpretation.

This choice deliberately avoids treating all additional oxygen uptake as a public benefit. An engineer might acquire more oxygen itself while diminishing supply to its neighbours. Conversely, reduced downstream oxygen concentration might simply show that a well-ventilated community consumed more.

#### Data needed, and what is actually accessible

Begin with the Avalon D, E and Lower Mistaken Point community reconstructions. The 2024 Zenodo archive [D1] was checked beyond its landing page: the community ZIP was downloaded, its coordinate records inspected, and an actual STEP geometry recovered from the large archive. It contains positions, sizes, orientations and taxon labels for simulated communities informed by fossil observations. These are usable starting geometries, but repeated virtual communities are not repeated ancient ecosystems.

The corresponding White Sea archive [D2] supplies contrasting community configurations. Its small community ZIP was downloaded; the large geometry/results archive was inventoried rather than retrieved in full. Bring a dense Funisia case into the pilot because it motivates the proposed departure from the earlier mixing result. Use clustered Funisia and reserved realizations for subsequent prediction. The published configurations constrain plausible geometry; they do not constitute observations of ancient oxygen or independent validation of every reconstruction choice.

Portable geometry is available; some original simulation files use commercial software. A validated alternative solver can implement the new experiment, provided it reproduces appropriate baseline flow behaviour. Do not make exact software replication a hidden prerequisite for a different physical model.

The central missing observations are metabolic tissue volume, effective exchange area, respiration, flexibility and lifetime current conditions. Modern analogues can establish orders of magnitude and limiting cases. They cannot make a rangeomorph into a modern coral or sponge. Mitchell and Dhungana's 2025 metabolic-theory application [R25; **abstract only**] offers existing scenario assumptions, not measured extinct respiration. A literature table should attach a rationale and uncertainty interval to each physiological assumption. If no defensible bounds can be assembled, the historical ecological question fails its initial feasibility test.

#### Three counterfactuals that must remain separate

| Contrast | Held fixed | Changed | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Transport alone | Recipient locations and demand, sediment demand, inflow oxygen and flow | Add an inert canopy | Does structure improve delivery? |
| Morphology at matched demand | Total potential canopy demand, recipients, forcing and sediment | Shape, spacing or identity of structural elements | Which architecture best supports neighbours for the same potential demand? |
| Net effect of living neighbours | Recipients, forcing and sediment | Add/remove both canopy structure and its respiration | Does the living canopy facilitate or compete with recipients? |

“Matched demand” means matched *potential* oxygen consumption under non-limiting conditions. Forcing actual realised consumption to remain identical can conceal oxygen limitation. Match effective reactive area or tissue volume in separate sensitivity treatments; those are different biological constraints and need not produce the same answer.

For taxon removal, compare deletion with replacement by a different shape at matched potential demand. Deletion answers the net consequence of losing that organism. Replacement isolates its structural role. If deletion reduces community consumption but nevertheless worsens recipient supply, that is an especially informative facilitation result. It does not establish that an ancient extinction cascade occurred.

Hold inlet forcing common within each paired comparison, but recompute velocity whenever geometry or taxon removal changes the flow domain. Across comparisons vary current strength and direction, oxygen supply, sediment demand, canopy density, gross shape, effective exchange area and uptake kinetics. Begin with a few interpretable contrasts, not a fully factorial universe of poorly constrained parameters.

#### Physical model and boundaries

A suitable starting equation is

\[
\frac{\partial C}{\partial t}+\nabla\!\cdot(\mathbf{u}C)
=\nabla\!\cdot\!\left[\left(D_m+\frac{\nu_t}{Sc_t}\right)\nabla C\right]-R_w(C),
\]

where \(C\) is dissolved oxygen, \(\mathbf{u}\) is water velocity, \(D_m\) is molecular diffusivity, \(\nu_t\) is turbulent eddy viscosity, \(Sc_t\) is a turbulent Schmidt number, and \(R_w\) is water-column demand. Tissue and sediment surfaces receive consuming boundary fluxes, for example

\[
J(C)=J_{\max}\frac{C}{K+C}.
\]

Treat this uptake law as a model family, and compare its low-concentration linear and high-concentration saturated limits. Define normal directions explicitly and verify that total influx minus outflux equals consumption plus storage. Negative concentrations are numerical failure, not extreme ecological stress.

A dimensionless screening ratio is

\[
\mathrm{Da}_{Q}=\frac{Q_{\max}}{C_\infty UHW},
\]

with potential demand \(Q_{\max}\), upstream concentration \(C_\infty\), reference speed \(U\), and an explicitly defined upstream cross-section \(HW\). It measures patch demand relative to incoming advective oxygen. It is not an estimate of ancient metabolism. Local transfer limitation can matter even when this whole-patch ratio is small.

Also compare an interior exchange time, obtained from passive-tracer washout, with an oxygen-depletion time \(t_d=C_\infty V/Q_{\max}\) for the same water volume \(V\). Exchange much faster than depletion supports the 2014 approximation. Comparable times justify a resolved reactive calculation. Measure washout in interior subregions: a fast domain-average turnover can conceal isolated pockets. This diagnostic connects the new geometry to the existing result without assuming that turbulence above a canopy ventilates all of it.

Resolve an appropriate Reynolds and Péclet regime; vary turbulent scalar transport rather than assuming a single closure gives the answer. Reuse flow fields only across scalar solves with unchanged geometry and negligible flow feedback. The main experiment assumes passive bodies. Butterfield proposes ciliary ventilation [R24], so include a bounded active-flow sensitivity before historical interpretation; if unconstrained pumping determines the sign, retain only the passive conditional result. High-resolution branching need not be resolved everywhere: distinguish drag geometry from effective respiratory area, then refine selected cases where that distinction matters.

The upper boundary is a major source of possible self-deception. A fixed oxygen concentration just above the canopy can behave like an unlimited reservoir. Test taller domains, justified open-water exchange and alternative upper boundaries. Similarly, placing a fresh inlet before each tiny patch suppresses cumulative depletion. Longer or repeated patches should establish whether the inferred benefit persists downstream.

Begin with steady conditions, then test a small set of unsteady, reversing and low-flow episodes. A habitat can be acceptable on average but unusable during prolonged interruptions. Keep those scenarios separate from speculation about exact Ediacaran storm frequencies.

#### A staged experimental design

**Pilot, first week.** Recover and reproduce the 2014 mixing-versus-uptake bound before building the extension. Pair an Avalon geometry with a dense 2025 Funisia geometry, document physiological bounds and estimate interior exchange. If the earlier negligible-depletion result survives conservative assumptions in the dense case, accept it. Proceed only if a consequential recipient effect or plausible failure of the approximation remains. Reproduce one baseline velocity field, and check domain-height and oxygen-boundary sensitivity.

**First substantive comparison, months one to three.** Run the three contrasts above on a small set of canopy shapes and demand levels. Include a bare-bed case, simple obstacles with known limiting behaviour, and more than one reconstructed arrangement per surface. Preserve paired parameter draws so treatment differences are not confounded with different environmental samples.

**Expansion only if justified, months four to twelve.** Sample the region where a consequential effect or sign transition occurs. Use adaptive sampling to estimate that boundary and its uncertainty. Reserve additional community realisations and clustered White Sea configurations for prediction. Do not choose the best-performing reconstruction after inspecting those results.

**Consolidation, remaining year.** Resolve the few numerical or anatomical choices that could reverse the conclusion. A dye or oxygen-exchange experiment around printed inert geometry could validate transport if a collaborator has a suitable flume. It is an optional validation route, not evidence of extinct respiration and not a requirement disguised as existing data.

Your 1,344 CPU cores suit independent geometry and parameter cases. A few hundred informative flow solutions, followed by cheaper reactive-transport solves, are a more defensible starting point than millions of ecological simulations. The A100 is optional; it is useful only if the chosen solver or an independently validated surrogate benefits from it. Runtime estimates should follow the pilot's mesh and convergence measurements.

#### How this study could fool itself

The most serious error would be reporting “oxygenation” without saying whether it means concentration, uptake, exchange or habitat suitability. These can move in opposite directions. The three counterfactuals prevent one ambiguity but do not remove all physiological uncertainty. Non-photosynthetic bodies redistribute and consume supplied oxygen; turbulence is not an oxygen source.

Another error would be declaring the 2014 result overturned after changing several assumptions simultaneously. Reproduce its limiting case, then change spatial heterogeneity, density and demand separately. A discrepancy caused by altered boundary conditions or an incompatible scalar-transport closure is not evidence that dense ancient communities depleted their habitat.

A second risk is fitting the unknown physiology to obtain an attractive tipping point. Register the physiological domain before the main ensemble, show results across it, and separate tightly fossil-constrained parameters from weakly constrained ones. Do not interpret arbitrary prior-weighted averages as historical probabilities.

A third is false replication. Virtual communities drawn from one reconstructed bed mostly measure uncertainty about that bed. Statistical confidence must not scale as though each virtual community were an independent palaeoecological observation.

A fourth is replacing missing anatomy with convenience. A smooth envelope may reproduce bulk drag while badly representing exchange area. Conversely, adding intricate branches without understanding living tissue could produce impressive but unjustified uptake. Compare effective-area assumptions and surface versus volume scaling explicitly.

Finally, biomass, canopy height and substrate demand can covary. A treatment that changes all three may be biologically interesting, but cannot isolate morphology. Report sensitivity of the structural effect separately from a plausible whole-community scenario.

#### Stopping rules that accept the null

Prespecify a smallest effect worth further work: for example, a ten-percentage-point change in recipients' realised fraction of potential demand. This is a research decision, not an established physiological threshold. Report the continuous effect as well, so the conclusion is not hostage to that cutoff.

Stop expansion if the earlier efficient-mixing result extends to the dense geometries and recipient effects remain below that level across the defensible domain. That would validate a useful approximation. Also stop if reasonable reconstructions reverse the sign without any way to discriminate between them, or if strong effects require unsupported respiration or pumping. Conclude that the geometry does not presently yield a robust oxygen-mediated ecological prediction.

If all well-supported scenarios favour facilitation, accept that result. Do not keep adding speculative stress until competition appears. If the sign boundary lies within independently credible conditions, report a conditional mechanism and the missing observations needed to locate an ancient community on it. If the boundary lies everywhere because the parameter domain is effectively unconstrained, report non-identification rather than a general ecological law.

The strongest feasible paper would show when community architecture changes oxygen supply to neighbours, how that differs from simple mixing proxies, and which conclusions survive anatomy and boundary uncertainty. Extending that result to the White Sea–Nama extinction would require separate temporal and physiological evidence. It should not be a success criterion for this first study.

### B2. Does the timing evidence for Cambrian extinction survive an audit of its dependencies?

#### The scientific target

The question is: **which claims about the ordering and interregional synchrony of end-Marjuman trilobite loss, SPICE onset and associated redox change remain identifiable when correlations derived from those events are withheld from the test?** This could produce a consequential result even if every published numerical estimate survives. It would establish which conclusions have independent support, which are conditional on a correlation model, and which exceed the resolving power of the observations.

There is a specific reason to undertake this now. Farrell et al. (2025; **DOI: 10.1130/B37919.1**, [R4]) provide updated age modelling and public computational inputs. Their supplementary CONOP construction treats several biomere boundaries as synchronous fixed events. That is a defensible choice for building a conditional composite chronology. It cannot independently demonstrate the synchrony of those same boundaries. Their within-section SPICE–extinction comparison is a different claim: observed stratigraphic order does not arise merely from aligning distant sections.

The project must preserve that distinction. Schiffbauer et al. (2017; **DOI: 10.1126/sciadv.1602158**, [R5]) already demonstrate facies-dependent and discordant expressions of SPICE. Pulsipher et al. (2021; **DOI: 10.1016/j.earscirev.2020.103442**, [R6]) already quantify substantial variation across a large compilation. The proposed contribution is an explicit dependency audit, followed by tests of whether realistic inference procedures can distinguish independently observed agreement from agreement supplied by their assumptions.

#### Competing hypotheses and their predictions

**H1: independent temporal agreement.** Extinction, carbon-cycle change and the relevant redox transitions have a reproducible temporal relationship. Removing target-derived correlations broadens uncertainty but preserves the supported order or bounded lag. Independently calibrated sections predict the timing of withheld records. Synthetic tests show that the procedure would reject agreement if appreciable offsets were present.

**H2: agreement depends materially on imposed correlations.** Precise temporal agreement appears chiefly when the target extinction boundaries, isotope shapes or their derivatives help align records. Once these links are removed or relaxed, evidence becomes substantially weaker. Synthetic asynchronous records can produce apparently synchronous estimates under the original alignment assumptions. This would challenge the precision of the historical inference; it would not establish that the real events were asynchronous.

**H3: global forcing with regionally delayed expression.** A broadly shared disturbance produces different recorded onsets because carbonate chemistry, sedimentation, habitat change or preservation differ between settings. Local event order may remain well resolved while interregional onset differences correlate with facies. This hypothesis permits global forcing without demanding identical isotope or fossil signals everywhere. A geographically repeated carbon excursion alone does not distinguish H1 from H3. Unconstrained adjustable response delays cannot identify a global forcing date; in that case only recorded-response timing and compatibility are estimable.

**H4: insufficient independent information.** Several histories fit the available observations: a common pulse, regional offsets, or differing durations. Honest uncertainty remains broad under all reasonable models. This is a substantive outcome. It means that a particular synchrony or causal-order claim is presently unresolved, and identifies the additional observations needed to resolve it.

These hypotheses concern different layers of inference. The analysis should not force them into one winner-takes-all model comparison. H1 might describe local ordering at one section while H4 describes global synchrony.

#### Define the quantities before constructing a chronology

Use age before present, \(A\), in millions of years, so older events have larger values. Let \(A_{\mathrm{loss},j}\) denote the regional fossil disappearance or turnover estimate; interpreting it as biological extinction requires additional assumptions about migration and preservation. For section \(j\), define

\[
\Delta_j=A_{\mathrm{carbon},j}-A_{\mathrm{loss},j}.
\]

A positive value means the recorded carbon-isotope onset preceded the estimated loss transition. Define an analogous lag for each redox proxy. Do not substitute the carbon onset for a redox onset: they are different observations with potentially different response times.

Three estimands must remain separate. **Local order** asks which feature lies lower in an undisturbed succession. **Local elapsed time** converts their separation into a duration. **Interregional synchrony** asks whether independently estimated regional loss ages agree. Local order can survive weak absolute calibration; a numerical duration usually needs more assumptions. A globally synchronized composite can estimate durations conditional on synchrony while providing no independent test of synchrony itself.

For regional loss ages, estimate the spread

\[
W=\max_j A_{\mathrm{loss},j}-\min_j A_{\mathrm{loss},j}.
\]

Calculate \(W\) from joint age draws preserving shared calibration and correlation uncertainty, not by independently combining marginal intervals. Report which sampled regions this covers. It is not automatically the spread of extinction times across the planet. Distinguish biological extinction, local disappearance and the last specimen collected. A named biozone boundary is not interchangeable with these quantities.

#### Data: an executable starting point and its limits

The **Farrell supplementary archive, DOI: 10.1130/GSAB.S.28207634 [D3]**, was downloaded and inspected. It includes an explanatory PDF, R scripts, a spreadsheet of age-model inputs and a spreadsheet of Smithfield Canyon carbon-isotope observations. These support reproduction and an audit of derived constraints. They do not provide an unrestricted, complete underlying occurrence compilation: the supplementary documentation identifies partly unpublished material [R4; D3].

The geographical starting set is deliberately tiered:

| Record | Role in the design | Restriction |
|---|---|---|
| Smithfield Canyon, Utah | Reproduce the published local carbon-onset analysis and assess local lag sensitivity | Numerical durations remain conditional on age and accumulation models |
| McPherson Canyon, Idaho | Examine the transfer of an additional zircon constraint | A detrital zircon maximum age is not a direct depositional date |
| Ogof-ddû and Bryn-llin-fawr, Wales | Trace the contribution of tuff dates and their correlation pathways | Tuffs are younger than SPICE, not local brackets around it; transfer requires correlation |
| Andrarum-3, Sweden | Evaluate an additional radiometric and geochemical record | Date precision and exact sampled interval must be checked before treating it as an independent timing test |
| Missouri cores TE-1, LS-1, HM-1, 1EE and 12EE | Compare local order across a facies transect | A shared basin and correlation framework limit independence |

The first three entries are documented in [R4; D3]. Rooney et al. (2022; **DOI: 10.1130/G49833.1**, [R7]) supply the relevant Re–Os study for Andrarum-3; I read its abstract, and have not verified access to its complete numerical supplement. The Missouri main paper is accessible, but its supplementary data files were not downloaded during this consultation [R5]. SPICEraq supplies a useful global sampling frame; its supplementary spreadsheet was identified but not downloaded [R6]. These are explicit acquisition gates, not assumed ready-to-analyse inputs.

#### Build a provenance graph before fitting anything

For each observation record its sample identifier, section, depth, analytical method, source publication, revision history and uncertainty. Add separate nodes for correlations and derived ages. Connect every inferred age to the observations and assumptions that produced it. The graph is an auditable record of information reuse.

A date measured on an ash remains a direct age observation at its own location. Its assigned position in another section is a separate uncertain correlation. A biozone age inherited from that transfer must not reappear as another independent age observation. Similarly, a revised analysis of the same material does not create an additional geological replicate. Shared laboratory calibration uncertainty belongs in covariance terms rather than independent error bars.

Classify correlation links by their function: target-extinction links, target-isotope links, other fossil succession, lithological evidence and radioisotopic observations. Classification should be agreed before examining which deletions weaken a preferred conclusion. Ideally, have a second analyst classify a sample of links independently. Disagreements expose ambiguous evidence pathways that need explicit alternative models.

#### Concrete model comparison: hold observations fixed, vary assumptions

First reproduce the published local workflow with its supplied inputs and document any discrepancies. This is a baseline, not an attempt to improve the model while reproducing it. Keep raw isotope values, sample depths, fossil identifications, collection counts, measured analytical errors and observed lithological boundaries fixed across the main comparisons.

Then fit a sequence of increasingly independent analyses:

1. **Published conditional model:** retain the supplied assumptions, making their provenance explicit.
2. **Extinction-withheld model:** remove target-biomere synchrony constraints when evaluating extinction synchrony. Retain the actual occurrences and their order.
3. **Isotope-withheld model:** remove matching of SPICE features when testing SPICE timing. Retain local measured isotope series as outcomes.
4. **Jointly withheld model:** estimate timing from the remaining independent observations, with explicit uncertainty on any other correlations.
5. **Local-order model:** use within-section ordering and observation uncertainty without requiring a common numerical clock.

An inaccessible raw compilation changes what can be done. If a supplied age input already summarizes a composite constructed under fixed boundaries, its dependence cannot be removed by editing its label. Omit that derived constraint in the ablation, or reconstruct it from genuinely public underlying observations. Describe the result as a public-input sensitivity audit when that is the achievable scope.

Use monotone age–depth functions with variable accumulation and geologically motivated hiatuses. With height increasing upwards, age must decrease. For a detrital zircon age \(A_z\) and depositional age \(A_d\), the physical constraint is

\[
A_d\leq A_z,\qquad L=A_z-A_d\geq0.
\]

Here \(A_z\) is a latent true crystallization age with an analytical likelihood, not a measured central value used as a hard cutoff. Retain analytical and shared systematic uncertainty. Vary plausible lag distributions; do not convert a maximum depositional age into an exact date. Test correlation uncertainty and hiatus assumptions alongside tiepoint ablations, keeping their effects distinguishable.

#### Estimate events without defining the desired result

Estimate isotope onsets using a preregistered change-point or background-departure procedure with uncertainty. Check whether several reasonable definitions alter the result. Apply the same rule consistently, while permitting facies-specific baselines. Do not choose a different onset in each section because it matches the expected extinction horizon.

For fossils, use observed ranges and collection opportunities to estimate disappearance intervals. Where repeat sampling supports it, model detection probability and variation in preservation. Where sampling information is inadequate, retain a defensible bracket rather than fitting an elaborate extinction-time model whose precision comes from its prior. Show results using both the recorded turnover boundary and a sampling-adjusted regional disappearance estimate. The latter still cannot by itself distinguish extinction from habitat tracking or migration.

A redox series needs its own observation model. Distinguish a local sedimentary signal from an inferred global reservoir signal, and propagate any response-time uncertainty. If the available records cannot locate oxygen stress at the habitats occupied by the affected taxa, the study can assess temporal compatibility but cannot establish a killing mechanism.

#### When simulation earns its cost

The provenance audit, constraint ablations and local-order comparison can answer the core question without simulation. Proceed to simulation only if independently anchored regional records justify an interregional timing model. At that stage it is decisive for evaluating claimed resolution: the fossil record cannot supply a known true date against which to calibrate the complete reconstruction. Generate synthetic records using observed sample spacing, collection sparsity, age constraints and facies structure. Impose known synchrony or offsets of 0, 0.25, 0.5, 1 and 2 Myr. These are diagnostic design values, not inferred natural intervals.

Vary hiatuses, preservation gradients, accumulated dating uncertainty and crystallization-to-deposition lags. Include shared forcing with different local response delays. Feed the resulting records through each complete pipeline, including event detection, correlation and age estimation. Measure interval coverage, false declarations of equivalence, power to detect offsets and the frequency with which a lag's sign is recovered correctly.

Make the simulations demanding in both directions. A method should recognize asynchronous records without falsely rejecting true synchrony. Avoid an unfair comparison in which an intentionally rigid baseline faces an unrealistically perfect replacement. Begin with a small factorial design, identify the conditions that matter, and add replicates where Monte Carlo uncertainty could change a decision.

Parallel CPU runs are appropriate because independent synthetic histories and age models can be distributed across nodes. The A100 adds little to the essential test. More computation cannot compensate for missing independent chronology; its role is to quantify that limitation reliably.

#### How the study could fool itself

The most serious risk is mistaking **precision lost after withholding information** for proof that the original conclusion was wrong. Correlation assumptions can be reasonable and scientifically useful. The result must state how much support depends on them, not treat every dependence as an error.

A second risk is pseudoreplication. Many measurements from one core improve a local curve but do not supply many independent temporal anchors. Nearby sections can share environmental processes, correlations and calibration errors. Evaluate predictive performance by withholding whole regions or independent evidence blocks, not random rows.

Third, choosing only records with obvious SPICE excursions conditions the sample on recognizing the target. Include age-compatible sections with weak or absent excursions where their chronology and preservation can be assessed. Otherwise, the analysis can overstate geographical consistency by construction.

Fourth, fossil disappearances can follow habitat migration or preservation loss. A precisely dated local disappearance does not become a global extinction simply because the numerical estimate is precise. Conversely, an isotope offset can arise from local recording effects without disproving a shared forcing event. Present these possibilities as alternatives that require evidence, not automatic explanations.

Finally, selective sensitivity analysis can manufacture a desired outcome. Register the model set, event definitions and exclusion rules before the full analysis. Preserve results that support the published interpretation. Any newly added model should answer a documented diagnostic failure, not merely move an estimate towards asynchrony.

#### Stopping rules, a useful null and a two-year route

**First five working days:** reproduce the accessible Smithfield calculation, inspect the input graph, and catalogue independent constraints. If reproduction fails, identify the concrete missing dependency before expanding. If target-derived links have negligible influence on the relevant claim, accept that reassuring result.

**By the end of the first month:** determine whether at least two regions contain independently anchored, event-bearing records capable of resolving the proposed tolerance. Two sites joined by the target event do not pass. If the gate fails, stop the global synchrony analysis. Complete the local robustness audit and an explicit account of what new age constraints would have the greatest value.

Predeclare **0.5 Myr** as the initial practical equivalence tolerance, while reporting sensitivity to alternative tolerances. For a local lag, claim equivalence only when a calibrated 95% uncertainty interval lies entirely within \([-0.5,+0.5]\) Myr. An interval that merely includes zero is not evidence of equivalence. For interregional spread \(W\), require its 95% upper uncertainty bound to be below 0.5 Myr. The tolerance expresses a specified scientific resolution; it does not prove biological simultaneity or rapid causal coupling.

Months 2–4 would complete the public-input reconstruction and event definitions. If the regional-data gate passes, months 5–9 would calibrate the timing model with simulation, followed by regional comparisons and withheld predictions in months 10–15. Otherwise, publish the bounded audit without that expansion. The remaining period allows specialist review, reproducible packaging and publication. Author contact or new sampling must not quietly become necessary to deliver the promised existing-data result.

If independence is the limiting factor, finish with a prospective information analysis. Add hypothetical dates at specified horizons, with explicitly assumed analytical precision, and calculate which would most reduce uncertainty about the competing histories. Compare an additional date within an already well-constrained section against one in a poorly connected region. This makes the negative result actionable without pretending that the hypothetical dates exist. Its output is a ranked sampling design, and its ranking should be tested against alternative accumulation and correlation assumptions before recommending field or laboratory investment.

Accept three outcomes without further model hunting: robust agreement survives the audit; an independently supported offset exceeds the tolerance; or the available evidence does not distinguish them. The last outcome is particularly valuable if it replaces a vague uncertainty disclaimer with an explicit map of identifiable claims and the observations that would resolve the rest.

## Part C — The consultant's view

### Where confidence most exceeds resolution

My concern is strongest at the conversion of a defensible observation into a global causal story. “These assemblages differ”, “this proxy changed”, and “a particular crisis reorganized this shelf” can be well supported while the proposed worldwide timing and mechanism remain weakly identified. This is a judgement about particular inferences, not a claim that specialists share one uncritical consensus.

The late Ediacaran is the clearest example. Evans et al. identify substantial White Sea–Nama losses after examining broad geographical, environmental and preservational alternatives; Buatois et al. document persistence of matground conditions beyond the characteristic biota [R8, R18]. These observations make a wholly preservational disappearance difficult to defend. They do not uniquely establish a short global pulse, its duration relative to background turnover, or a single killing mechanism. Anatomical selectivity can support an oxygen hypothesis without measuring the oxygen exposure of the lost animals. The White Sea–Nama transition, Kotlinian regional records and terminal-Ediacaran losses should therefore enter an analysis as potentially distinct episodes, with correlations to be justified.

In the later Cambrian, the weak point is often the independence of the clock. Local isotope–fossil relationships and their dependence on setting are documented [R5–R6]. The extra step to global synchrony requires independent temporal information. Reusing correlated boundaries can produce precision conditional on an alignment without supplying evidence for the alignment itself. Part B proposes an audit, not a presumption that published chronologies fail.

“Ecological function” also invites more confidence than the measured quantity warrants. Murphy et al.'s Sinsk study reports functional-diversity decline extending beyond taxonomic recovery, while explicitly acknowledging broad taxonomic trait assignments and the absence of abundance information [R19]. That is valuable evidence about occupied trait space. It does not directly measure filtration, predation, carbon processing or community production. A small population can retain a trait category while contributing little flux; a species-poor population can dominate a flux. This distinction matters when connecting fossil diversity to ecosystem consequences.

### Debates that need a different question

“Biotic replacement or environmental catastrophe?” is too coarse when engineering changes both habitat and preservation, and environmental change modifies the strength of interactions. A testable version asks whether losses follow a specified engineering transition within comparable facies and preservation, beyond losses expected from independent environmental change. Securely placing that transition after the losses would reject it as their local trigger, conditional on adequate dating and detection. Mere absence of observed overlap would not: engineers can escape detection or leave persistent habitat changes. A residual association does not establish competition. Craffey et al.'s lithological tests provide a starting point [R16].

“Did oxygen cause the Cambrian radiation?” combines several outcomes and scales. Replace it with a constrained physiological prediction: would the measured or bounded exposure permit an identified body plan or behaviour, and does that prediction survive plausible metabolic rates? Distinguish bottom-water conditions, a canopy interior and irrigated sediment. An oxygen model with arbitrary demand and threshold parameters can accommodate almost any sequence. That is a statement about flexibility, not an explanation. Questions 1 and 6 demand bounds and specify when to stop.

“Was the explosion sudden?” remains ambiguous until the response variable is named. First fossil occurrence, taxonomic richness, anatomical disparity, behavioural repertoire and ecological dominance need different observation models. Select one, state the temporal resolution at which a change would count as abrupt, and test whether preserved data can distinguish it from gradual change. A smoothness statistic can constrain trajectory geometry without uniquely identifying sensory or neural innovation [R9–R10].

The same discipline applies to reproductive mode. Physical connections, limited dispersal, isotropic clustering and genetic sexuality are different propositions. The recent spatial studies create quantitative tests [R12–R15]; they do not make those propositions interchangeable. Ask which alternative generative processes survive independent connection evidence, rather than choosing a biological label from one spatial summary.

### The most under-asked question

**Why did apparently successful ecological innovations so often fail to become persistent community states?** First appearances attract attention because they are visible milestones. Persistence is a separate evolutionary problem: a capability can exist, spread locally, flourish after disturbance and then lose ecological prominence.

Temporary orthid brachiopod blooms around a late-Cambrian trilobite crisis provide a concrete foothold; Westrop and colleagues already recognize their transience [R23]. The underdeveloped comparison is between temporary and enduring ecological transitions under matched environmental opportunity. Did incumbent recovery close the opportunity, did environmental suitability disappear, or was the apparent bloom a depositional concentration?

I would start with abundance, facies and persistence through recovery, before demographic simulation. The first gate is whether repeated crises supply comparable observations; the public specimen-level evidence I could verify does not yet justify a strong seventh card. A null in which environmental tracking explains the temporary states would be informative. Evidence for persistence depending on the identity of recovering incumbents would justify a carefully constrained model of historical contingency. For your programme, separating the origin of an innovation from the conditions allowing it to persist could yield more than another model of its first appearance.
