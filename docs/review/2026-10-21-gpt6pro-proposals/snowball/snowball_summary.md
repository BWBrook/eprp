## The proposed topic

### **Could adaptation to Snowball Earth make the thaw more lethal?**

The central question is:

> **Can physiological evolution that helps a lineage survive prolonged glaciation reduce its subsequent survival enough to outweigh that earlier benefit?**

The proposed simulations cover approximately **730–650 Ma**, focusing on early eukaryotic phototrophs through the Sturtian and its immediate aftermath.

The project would connect evolving physiology with changing refugia, dispersal, resource competition, dormant propagules and stochastic population loss. Its main outcome is the persistence of the **same founding lineages across the complete environmental episode**.

The crucial experiment allows physiological evolution during the glacial interval in one treatment and delays it until the terminal thaw in another. **Both treatments can evolve during thaw and recovery.** This tests the consequences of earlier adaptation while allowing subsequent evolutionary rescue.

The proposal also separates three outcomes that could otherwise be confused: evolution preserves more lineages throughout; evolution preserves more during glaciation but some of those additional survivors subsequently disappear; or later losses actually outweigh the earlier survival benefit. Standardized ancestral-versus-evolved physiological assays then test whether an inherited performance cost explains the outcome.
