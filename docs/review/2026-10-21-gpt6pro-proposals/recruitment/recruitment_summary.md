## Proposed topic

**Can recruitment failure drive a mass extinction without its expected selectivity?**

The central question is whether a collapse in larval food supply can contribute substantially to marine species extinction while leaving little difference in extinction between species with feeding and nonfeeding larvae.

The broader scientific target is the relationship between **the mechanism that injures organisms and the selectivity eventually recorded among fossil taxa**. Adult survival, maternal provisioning, delayed maturation, recruitment compensation and spatial persistence all intervene between those two levels.

The proposed experiment looks for three conditions together:

1. **Substantial species extinction** relative to a viable no-event baseline.
2. **A small extinction difference between developmental modes**, initially measured with perfect knowledge of simulated species.
3. **A substantial reduction in extinction when ordinary larval food is restored**, while the rest of the crisis continues.

Each simulation family includes the complete crisis, larval food restoration, postlarval food restoration, restoration of both, and a no-event baseline. Those comparisons distinguish a consequential larval pathway from cases where other stages dominate—or where either of two food shortages is independently sufficient to cause extinction.

- **Life-cycle biology:** a consequential larval mechanism produces little selectivity even among perfectly observed species.
