# Programme remit and initial roadmap

## Purpose

Advance quantitative palaeobiology by finding scientifically useful questions
that can be answered from existing observations, published knowledge or
theoretical and simulation work. EPRP develops the evidence base, reusable
resources and critical reasoning that let downstream projects start well.

The geological past can inform hypotheses about present biodiversity loss;
modern ecology can suggest mechanisms and observation models for interpreting
the fossil record. Transfer between them must be argued at an explicit scale.
Similarity in a plotted pattern alone is not evidence of a shared mechanism.

Barry brings scientific taste, expertise and strategic judgment. Codex and
Claude contribute ideation, criticism, source work, implementation and review.
Rotate proposer and critic roles. An unresolved disagreement should have a
stated consequence and a discriminating check, rather than repeated advocacy.

Barry's 7 October follow-up gives **deep time** particular priority and asks
for substantial research, ideation and dataset preparation before selecting a
project. His wording used “area B” for deep time; the explicit subject takes
precedence over the A/B labels in Claude's initial framing. There is no reason
to concentrate on Barry's previous study systems. The aim is a consequential
scientific question newly tractable with available evidence and methods, not
an incremental application chosen because the data or software are convenient.

## Scope

Within the current remit:

- Literature maps, primary-source summaries, methodological comparisons and
  discovery of related code/data repositories.
- Database and API reconnaissance, data dictionaries, coverage and bias audits,
  reproducible shared extracts, and documented harmonisation.
- Hypothesis generation, rival explanations, simulation concepts, feasibility
  probes and proposals for downstream research.
- Programme priorities, task coordination, decisions and informative failures.

Examples of useful orientation axes, **not a selected research agenda**:

- Extinction selectivity and the conditions under which past vulnerability
  patterns can inform modern ecological expectations.
- Recovery, ecological reorganisation and the difference between taxonomic
  recovery and restoration of ecological structure or function.
- Diversification, geographic range and spatial turnover across environmental
  change, including questions whose value does not depend on a modern analogy.
- Recoverability of ecological signals under preservation, sampling,
  chronology and taxonomic uncertainty; what simulations could identify when
  available observations cannot discriminate mechanisms.

New field campaigns and primary study execution are outside this initial
foundation tranche. EPRP can later maintain shared tools or datasets; the
downstream project owns the study-specific design, analyses and claims.

## Initial route

1. **Foundation (complete).** Shared operating instructions, a source
   register with verification labels, a compact idea format, a status/ownership
   record and a concrete first correspondence exchange. Completion means these
   are inspectable and consistent, with no implied scientific findings.
2. **Research and ideation (current phase).** Explore multiple deep-time
   questions through repeated rounds of literature, data capability mapping
   and theoretical criticism. Let questions guide detailed data audits while
   source discoveries reveal questions we had not considered. Use simulations
   when they are the cheapest informative test and can be grounded in plausible
   observation processes. Record search limits and contrary evidence. Retire
   the initial 6–10-paper bound and do not require an early shortlist; reporting
   checkpoints should expose new understanding and gaps, not force selection.
3. **Question register (continuous; Barry, 9 October).** Maintain
   [ideas/register.md](../ideas/register.md): add sketches, check prior work and
   feasibility, rank, and park or reject with reasons. Expand with new questions
   every round. Each matured entry gives closest prior work, the unresolved
   contribution, feasible evidence, a rival explanation, a discriminating test
   and a reason to abandon it. Both agents critique; preserve surviving
   disagreement in the entry.
4. **Handoff.** Entries marked `ready for handoff` are posed for other
   Claude/Codex teams. When a team takes one up, record the EPRP commit and
   evidence links; the primary investigation lives with that team. Barry may
   also direct a question into a downstream project.

Report coherent rounds of orientation while keeping this phase open: what
major explanations compete, what observations could discriminate them, what
prior work has already settled, and what datasets or methods change feasibility.
An entry becomes `ready for handoff` when it survives those challenges, not
when a paper-count target is reached. EPRP never converges on a single target. Catalogue size and agent agreement
are not success measures. Process documentation is otherwise frozen during
this phase; correct consequential inaccuracies without adding more scaffolding.

## Conditions for a downstream project

A proposal ready for Barry's decision should specify:

- Question, scientific value and the quantity or contrast to be inferred.
- Inference scale, observational units and the information actually available.
- Closest prior work and a bounded, source-checked novelty claim.
- Feasible data/access route or a justified theoretical/simulation design.
- Observation-process risks, competing explanations and decisive comparisons.
- An informative null/contrary outcome, abandonment criteria and proportionate
  resource needs.

The handoff identifies the EPRP commit and source/data snapshots, transformations,
remaining uncertainties, owner and downstream repository. Readiness is a
scientific judgment; filling every template field does not by itself establish it.
