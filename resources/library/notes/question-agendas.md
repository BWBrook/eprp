# Published question lists and research agendas

Barry asked on 25 October 2026 (exact): "could you do a deeper dive into the
specialist literature of the past decade or so, looking for similar 'big
questions in quantitative palaeontology' type works, that might be useful for
our ideation? Perhaps the 2025 Paleobiology paper covers this off
comprehensively, but maybe there are other brainstormed/workshopped ideas
already out there: ideation fruit waiting for us to pick and work through!"

The 2025 paper ([note](big-questions-2025.md)) is not the whole field.
Several kinds of source set out research questions explicitly:

- community horizon scans and priority-question exercises;
- discipline "grand challenge" essays;
- *Paleobiology*'s 50th-anniversary issue;
- workshop and national-academy reports;
- reviews that close with an "Outstanding Questions" box or a "Future
  Issues" list.

This note catalogues those sources and extracts the questions they pose,
verbatim. It ends with a shortlist of questions EPRP could take up.

**28 October acquisition update.** Three formerly inaccessible works are now in
the private library: Patiño et al. 2017 via the University of La Laguna,
Kiessling et al. 2019 via the Oxford Research Archive, and Sutherland et al.
2013 via the Warwick Research Archive. The latter is an open accepted

## What the hunt found

- **392 sources catalogued**: 369 OpenAlex works, which form the `agendas` tier, and 23 grey documents. 216 of the works are held as open text.
- **1,224 verified items from 148 sources**: 790 questions and 434 research challenges. The wording of 938 can sit in the repository under their licences (see Files).
- **By theme** (a row can carry two): BD 322, CPB 317, CPP 243, AIO 229, SEP 228, AFR 179, ED 132, BST 132, PTS 102, LIO 77, FI 74. The pool leans towards drivers, conservation and climate. That reflects the conservation-heavy horizon-scanning tradition as much as the field.

**Overlap with the registers is low.** Only 18 rows ask substantially the same thing as a register entry: Q-019 (4); Q-024 (3); Q-005, Q-036 and Q-040 (2 each); and Q-006, Q-014, Q-027, Q-030 and B-015 (1 each). The extractors were told not to stretch matches. Most published questions are programme statements ("How can we…?"), while register entries are narrower and testable. So the pool is more useful as a source of neighbouring questions and of positioning than as a duplicate check. Low overlap is not evidence of novelty; that still needs each entry's prior-work check.

## Shortlist: questions EPRP's assets could take on

These are chosen by the library session for fit with what EPRP holds: PBDB, Macrostrat, the register deposits, the library, and simulation. Each is quoted from its source, followed by the nearest register entries.

**Extinction and recovery**

1. "Does polyphyly buffer ecological functions from extinction?" and "To what degree does polyphyletic occupation of ecological functions structure rebounds?" (Jablonski & Edie 2025, *Paleobiology* 51:83). Ecospace guilds crossed with clade membership in PBDB give a direct test across the Big Five. Neighbours: Q-009, Q-022, Q-043.
2. "Are changes to taxonomic, functional, morphological, and phylogenetic currencies correlated across extinction events?" (Jablonski & Edie 2025). Neighbours: Q-022, Q-042.
3. "Are mass extinctions temporally scale-independent?" (PaleoSynthesis PRIME workshop, 2025). Neighbours: Q-011, Q-041.
4. "Are extinction cascades more, or less, extensive when driven by primary extinctions occurring simultaneously at multiple trophic levels?" (Payne et al. 2023, *Cambridge Prisms: Extinction*). A food-web simulation question. Neighbours: Q-037, Q-048.
5. "Are short, catastrophic intervals of biodiversity loss typically nested within longer events through geological time?" (Rasmussen et al. 2023, TREE). Neighbours: Q-005, Q-014, B-007.
6. "Do the processes involved in response to ecological disturbances also apply to response to mass extinctions, or is there a threshold beyond which the processes differ?" (NRC 2005). Neighbours: Q-002, Q-011.

**Climate and extinction**

7. "How well is the rank order of modern climate sensitivities among clades and ecosystems reflected in differential extinction rates in the past?" (Kiessling et al. 2024, *Paleobiology* 51:97). Modern thermal-tolerance compilations against PBDB extinction rates by clade. Neighbours: Q-028, Q-015, Q-001.
8. "A future quest will thus be to contrast hyperthermal extinction drivers against non-hyperthermal drivers." (Kiessling et al. 2024). Neighbours: Q-003, Q-019, Q-049.
9. "How do tolerance, adaptive evolution, and migration interact in specific circumstances to reduce or amplify risk of extinction?" (Nogués-Bravo et al. 2018, TREE). Neighbours: Q-015, Q-049, Q-050.

**Macroevolution and scale**

10. "Are there circumstances under which ecological processes quantified as 'strong' on short timescales will be erased over macroevolutionary timescales?" (Liow & Quental 2025, *Paleobiology* 51:71). A natural simulation target. Neighbours: B-014, Q-039.
11. "Does diversity saturate in space and/or time?" and "Does speciation create niches or does empty niche space drive speciation?" (McGill et al. 2019, *Global Ecology and Biogeography*). Neighbours: Q-010, Q-044, Q-033.
12. "How does relative abundance persist or change through geological time and across the tree of life?" (McGill et al. 2019). PBDB abundance data make this testable. Neighbours: B-015.
13. "Why does the rate of evolution appear to accelerate close to present time?" (Rolland et al. 2023, *Nature Ecology & Evolution*). Neighbours: Q-045, Q-006.
14. "Is low evolvability associated with species extinction?" and "Is high evolvability associated with radiation?" (Love et al. 2021, *Paleobiology*). Neighbours: Q-050, Q-012.

**Communities and biogeography**

15. "Is there any secular trend in the tendency of communities to show bimodal modulated patterns of stasis and change as opposed to more continuous turnover?" and "Is Disturbance Enough, or Is Biology Needed?" (Brett et al. 2025, *Paleobiology*). Neighbours: Q-036, B-015.
16. "To what degree is spatial ecological variation comparable to temporal ecological variation?" (NRC 2005). This is the space-for-time question, testable with occurrence data. Neighbours: B-005.
17. "What drives the spatial expansion and contraction of a species over its duration?" (Seddon et al. 2014, question 19). Neighbours: Q-016, Q-020.
18. Brodie & Mannion (TREE 38:15, 2023) note that no current hypothesis for the latitudinal diversity gradient explains temperate diversity peaks in cold early–middle Permian intervals, or tropical-peaked marine gradients in the hot Middle Triassic. Neighbours: Q-027.
19. "Is larval or adult life mode more important for driving wide geographic dispersal in trilobites?" (PaleoSynthesis TRiBE workshop, 2024). It bears on the life-history mechanism that the main agents kept as Q-028 support. Neighbours: Q-028, B-012.

**Blue-sky**

20. "What might evidence of a 'failed Gaia' look like?" and "Do diverse populations allow greater resilience to perturbation?" (Lenton et al. 2018, TREE). Neighbours: Q-030.
21. "Why didn't plants evolve C4 or Crassulacean acid metabolism photosynthesis under the generally low CO2 conditions of much of the late Paleozoic?" (Matthaeus et al. 2023, *Annual Review of Earth and Planetary Sciences*).

The full table can be filtered by theme to find neighbours of any register entry. For example, rows tagged ED give the extinction-dynamics pool, and rows citing a register ID in `eprp_nearest` give the near-duplicates.

## How the sources were found

- **Citation chains.** These were works citing the established
  priority-setting papers that mention fossils, palaeontology,
  macroevolution or deep time: Sutherland et al. 2009 and 2011 (methods)
  and 2013, Seddon et al. 2014, Parsons et al. 2014, Patiño et al. 2017,
  Kiessling et al. 2019 and Smith, Dowding et al. 2025. This step found 177
  works. The Big Questions paper's own agenda-like references were added.
- **The library's own index.** Titles, abstracts and held full texts were
  searched for agenda phrasing ("big questions", "outstanding questions",
  "grand challenge", "research agenda" and similar).
- **Journal pools:**
  - palaeontological reviews in *Trends in Ecology & Evolution* since 2014,
    whose "Outstanding Questions" boxes are a standard feature;
  - the *Annual Review of Ecology, Evolution, and Systematics* and the
    *Annual Review of Earth and Planetary Sciences*, with their Summary
    Points and Future Issues;
  - all of *Cambridge Prisms: Extinction*;
  - the 13 papers of *Paleobiology* 51(1), the 50th-anniversary issue.
- **Two scouts** (Claude Sonnet 5.5 subagents):
  - a keyword search of the peer-reviewed literature through Crossref and
    OpenAlex, which gave 126 works, each DOI verified;
  - a grey-literature search of national-academy reports, funder workshops,
    society initiatives and networks, which gave 24 documents.

All OpenAlex works form the library tier `agendas` (`seeds/agendas.tsv`). It
was resolved and fetched like the other tiers, from open copies only.

## How the questions were extracted and checked

1. **Digest.** `library.py`'s store texts were cut into a digest for each
   work: the text under forward-looking headings (Outstanding Questions,
   Future Issues, Future Directions, Outlook, Conclusions, Challenges), every
   sentence ending in a question mark, and the closing section. Only open
   copies (`text/`) were used. TDM copies were never read.
2. **Extraction.** Claude Sonnet 5.5 subagents took the open questions and
   research challenges that the authors themselves pose. They excluded
   rhetorical questions, each paper's own study questions, and cited titles.
   Rows are verbatim; only hyphenation, whitespace and list numbering were
   normalised. Each row is tagged with one or two of the eleven Big
   Questions themes (AFR, SEP, PTS, BST, BD, AIO, ED, CPP, CPB, FI, LIO) and,
   where it asks substantially the same thing, with an EPRP register ID.
3. **Independent check.** Every extracted row was checked mechanically
   against its source digest, after case and punctuation folding. A row
   passes if it occurs there, or occurs with a page header cut out of the
   middle. Rows that failed were discarded.
4. **Grey literature.** The scout's quotations were re-fetched from the
   original pages and PDFs and matched. A first check against the scout's own
   copies found that some wording had drifted. After re-fetching, 170
   of 174 matched exactly, matched in order across two-column PDF layouts, or
   differed only by OCR slips. The other 4 were dropped. The 26
   purely solid-Earth questions (earthquakes, mantle and the like) were
   dropped as outside the remit.

**Licences.** Question wording appears in the repository only where the
source's licence allows reuse:
- Creative Commons licences;
- US federal (NSF) documents, which are in the public domain;
- the CC BY ICDP science plan.

For other sources (national-academy reports, society brochures, workshop web
pages, unlicensed open copies), the row is kept but `text` is blank. The
full table, wording included, is at
`deep-server:/data/paleodata/library/agendas/questions-full.tsv`.
