## Biological question and three different objects

Could useful adult ammonoid designs remain difficult to reach because changes
to an entire growth programme compromise an earlier stage? Start with
planispiral Devonian–Jurassic shells with measurable cross-sections; a complex
Late Cretaceous heteromorph can inform physical methods but is not automatically
a comparable validation lineage.

1. A **shell shape** describes an organism at a given size/stage.
2. An **ontogenetic trajectory** describes one individual's successive shapes.
3. An **evolutionary route** describes changes between complete growth
   programmes across generations.

Repeated adult forms, recurrent ontogenies and a physically viable developing
individual do not alone identify the third object. The scientific consequence
would be a change in how convergence and reversal are interpreted: performance
at adulthood may omit a consequential restriction on access to that performance.

## The first comparison: change the requirement, preserve the generator

Represent a complete growth programme by controls theta and a shell generator
G(theta, size). Let u describe uncertain shell/tissue/chamber reconstruction.
Every comparison uses the same external conditions, geometric exclusions,
absolute-size assumptions, u ensemble and candidate endpoint programmes.

| Model | Requirement applied | What a difference can establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A: adult performance | Evaluate the terminal adult's physical demands | Reference approximation based on adult performance |
| J: all-stage performance | Evaluate the same demands across the observed/modelled size range | The added contribution of juvenile physical demands **within G** |
| J variants | Retain all-stage demands while varying independently stated developmental coupling or permitted programme changes | Sensitivity to the hypothesised evolutionary/developmental representation |

Both endpoints in the main route contrast should be acceptable under J;
otherwise an impossible juvenile endpoint supplies a trivial result. Compare
routes between them under A and J. Adding all-stage requirements necessarily
cannot enlarge the viable set: that set inclusion is a mathematical consequence,
not the scientific finding. The question is whether the reduction creates a
consequential and robust difference in the tested route or access statistic.

Keep the generator fixed for A versus J. Changing generators at the same time
would mix a representation effect with juvenile requirements. Then repeat the
comparison across plausible generators. Fossil shape covariance is not a direct
measurement of genetic coupling, a mutation distribution or permissible step
size. This contrast initially isolates physical stage effects; identifying a
particular developmental mechanism requires additional evidence.

## Physical requirements and threshold discipline

Begin with accretion geometry, non-intersection and hydrostatic quantities,
including the cameral-fluid range compatible with a chosen buoyancy target.
Do not silently require neutral buoyancy for every possible ecology. Define
the ecological performance target and applicable stages before judging shells.
Relative-size indices alone do not specify shell thickness, septa, body-chamber
proportions, material density, soft tissues or absolute scale.

Peterman's methods show why these inputs matter. They also describe high
stability as a trade-off: it resists displacement while making active changes
of orientation harder. Neither maximum stability nor a Nautilus-like orientation
is therefore a universal viability optimum. A physical failure of a specified
mode of life is narrower than proof that the organism could not survive.

Separate hard geometric/physical exclusions from uncertain performance
preferences. Examine continuous performance margins before dichotomising them.
Thresholds and their plausible ranges need independent physical/ecological
justification; they cannot be selected to create a barrier. Robustness means
robustness over the independently defended range, not every imaginable cutoff.
If the result changes within that range, report the regimes of change rather
than choosing the favourable one. Early post-hatching viscous regimes and later
inertial regimes may require different physical descriptions.

## Accessibility, topology and evolutionary claims

For an initial conditional theory, record the declared programme changes,
observation scale and route statistic. Possibilities include the worst physical
margin along the best candidate route or crossing frequency under an explicit
transition process. Specify their units/normalisation before comparison. A
numerical effect threshold cannot yet be justified from the incomplete physical inputs
and unresolved transition evidence; the design is not ready for a prospective
discrimination experiment.

A sparse grid with no connecting edges proves neither that the continuous
feasible set is disconnected nor that evolution could never cross it. A
nearest-neighbour graph depends on metric, grid density and allowed steps.
True topological connectedness is preserved by a continuous invertible change
of coordinates whose inverse is continuous; a metric graph need not be. Moving
to a different generator family can change the actual feasible set.

Even a disconnected continuous viable set need not imply impossible evolution:
a transition process permitting sufficiently large direct heritable changes
can connect viable endpoints without individuals occupying intermediate
programmes. If step-size support, rates or population scale are unsupported,
report model-relative accessibility, not a geological waiting time or actual
probability of innovation. Conditional costs/frequencies are the primary target.
Strict disconnection would require a separate mathematical argument in a stated
family and would remain conditional on that family and physical bounds.

## Fossil comparison and observation process

The [data-support audit](../data/ammonoid-growth-support/README.md) now records
verified source bytes, observed growth coverage and preserved metadata conflicts.
Taxonomic affiliation and geological stage do not by themselves identify an
ancestor, sister pair or direction of transition. The number of independent
eligible historical transitions is presently **unknown**, not zero and not 177.
There are zero explicit evolutionary relationships in the supplied workbook;
external phylogenetic evidence would be required to construct them.

An eligible historical comparison needs linked juvenile/adult growth, sufficient
physical geometry, independently supported relationships/ordering and explicit
alternatives. Preserve shell dissolution, compaction, section-plane errors,
lost inner whorls/body chambers, transport, maturity and dimorphism uncertainty.
Diameter is a size coordinate, not a dated age trajectory. Check whether published
series reuse specimens or primary measurements before reserving held-out
lineages/sources. Fitting growth controls to a fossil and then reproducing the
same curve is calibration, not an independent test of an evolutionary route.

If support eventually permits, a useful prediction would be a juvenile-related
restriction on transitions between comparably performing adult endpoints,
tested on reserved lineages and carried through the observation model. Its
comparison must include ordinary stage-specific ecology, body-size scaling,
ancestral-state uncertainty and clade sorting. Absence of a shell design is
insufficient; negative transition evidence needs a defined opportunity and
detection process. No such held-out comparison is yet specified from actual
eligible events.
