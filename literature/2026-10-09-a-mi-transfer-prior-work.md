# Candidate A: nearest prior work and disposition

Owner: Claude | Date: 9 October 2026 | Status: **A parked as an EPRP primary candidate**

**Candidate A** asked whether a frozen metabolic-index (MI) habitat-loss model,
calibrated on the end-Permian (Penn et al. 2018; Penn & Deutsch 2022), predicts
extinction magnitude and selectivity in *other* events better than traits plus
thermal exposure. It also asked whether several events could resolve the
calibration's non-uniqueness
([transfer inputs](2026-10-08-transfer-inputs.md)).

## Disposition

1. **Scoop risk is effectively certain for A's core.** A funded five-institution
   consortium, including the model's authors and cGENIE's developer, is running
   this exact programme, and its Late Devonian and Late Ordovician tests are
   already in abstracts. EPRP holds no comparative advantage there.
2. **The "use other events to identify the calibration" refinement is weak.**
   Penn 2018 states that selectivity is insensitive to V_crit and depth, so only
   magnitude constrains them. Each extra event also brings its own weakly
   constrained forcing, so more events may lengthen the posterior ridge rather
   than collapse it. [Inference.]
3. **What remains is narrow.** It would be an adversarial
   mechanism-discrimination design: events or observables where thermal-only and
   O₂-limited models diverge (cooling events, warming without deoxygenation,
   depth contrasts, trait contrasts). That sits inside the consortium's remit and
   Reddin 2022's comparison, so at most it is a critique or comment.

**Consequence for the disruption area.** A and B are parked on prior work, and C
is a modest design diagnostic. The mainstream of this area is crowded and
actively worked by well-resourced groups. That is an informative negative result
for programme priorities, recorded for the next exchange with Codex and Barry.
