## 1. The closest designs

| E2 element | Closest capture–recapture design | Source (access) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stage bins, each with several detections of a species | **Robust design**: several secondary samples inside each primary period. Detection within a period is estimated separately from survival between periods | Pollock 1982, 10.2307/3808568 (meta); Kendall & Bjorkland 2001 abstract: the robust design "involving multiple sampling occasions per period of interest" |
| Deaths and births *within* bins | **Open robust design**: relaxes closure within periods ("closed to additions and deletions across samples within a period") | Schwarz & Stobo 1997, 10.2307/2533106 (meta); Kendall & Bjorkland 2001, 10.1111/j.0006-341x.2001.01113.x (abs) |
| Unsampled species latent; conditioning only on a non-empty record; origination (λ) | **Unconditional, temporal-symmetry models** (recruitment and seniority), not CJS's conditioning on first capture | Pradel 1996, 10.2307/2532908 (meta); Liow & Nichols 2010 (per Codex) |
| E2-mix: heterogeneity in preservation and hazard | **Finite mixtures** in survival and capture for open models, which condition on first capture | Pledger, Pollock & Norris 2003, 10.1111/j.0006-341x.2003.00092.x (abs) |

**What carries over (inference).**
- In the robust design, repeated detections inside a period identify the
  within-period detection probability.
- That is the step binary CJS lacks for its last occasion.
- I recall, without re-reading, that the robust design is valued for making
  estimable parameters that CJS confounds, including the last ones.
- E2's per-bin counts play the role of repeated within-period detections.
  Codex's final-count example and Claude's moment-ratio sketch are both
  instances of this logic.

**What does not carry over directly.**
- **Detection mechanics.** E2's detections are a Poisson process over
  continuous time, with exposure ending at an unknown death time inside the
  bin. Robust designs use discrete secondary occasions and binary captures.
- **Within-bin openness.** The open robust design models arrival and
  departure for availability, not death that ends exposure, so the mapping
  needs care.
- **Births and unsampled species.** Budding births and globally conditioned,
  never-detected species fit Pradel-type unconditional models better than
  conditional ones.
- **Mixtures.** Pledger's mixtures condition on first capture, and finite
  mixtures in open models can be weakly identified. That is recalled as a
  general caution, not checked here.

## 2. Conclusion on E2's structural question

E2's terminal-bin question is very plausibly answered **in principle** by
robust-design logic: repeated detections within a period separate detection
from survival. The specific continuous-exposure, budding, globally
conditioned version may lack a published theorem, but proving it would be an
application, not a new scientific question.

**Proposal: close E2's structural question as answered in principle**, with
the moment-ratio sketch as illustration. Do not commission a proof.

## 3. Does the data support E2? A first measurement

[data/q006-e2-support/clustering.py](../data/q006-e2-support/clustering.py)
uses the pinned PBDB Changhsingian marine species-level extract from Q-042.
Results in [clustering.tsv](../data/q006-e2-support/clustering.tsv):

| Quantity | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Species, occurrences | 2,159; 11,031 |
| Share of species with ≥ 2 occurrences in the stage | 0.587 |
| Share with ≥ 2 distinct references | **0.354** |
| Share with ≥ 3 distinct references | 0.168 |
| Repeat occurrences (beyond each species' first) | 8,872 |
| Repeats that add a reference not already counted for that species | **1,957 (22%)** |

**Inference.**
- Within-stage counts in PBDB are **strongly clustered by publication**:
  78% of repeat occurrences come from a reference already counted for that
  species.
- They are not independent Poisson detections.
- Clustering inflates E[K(K−1)]/E[K]² above the Poisson-mixture value.
  Under the moment-ratio reading of section 7 that excess would be mistaken
  for a higher extinction hazard (R increases with μΔ).
- A usable E2 therefore needs one of two things:
  - an explicit clustering or overdispersion model, for example counting
    by reference or collection cluster and modelling within-cluster
    multiplicity;
  - or counting detections at the reference level, which leaves about 35%
    of species with any repeat in the stage.
- That is a concrete limit on precision and an observation-model
  requirement. It is not evidence about identifiability in E2's idealised
  form.
- One stage of one extract was measured. Other stages and clades may
  differ.

## 4. The residual consequential target for Q-006

With E1 answered, the generic claim occupied by capture–recapture, and E2's
structure answered in principle, what remains is:
1. **E2-mix**: preservation heterogeneity correlated with hazard. Under
   Pledger-type mixtures, how is the event contrast affected when
   selectivity on range changes at the event? This links Q-006 to Q-038,
   where the Monarrez Pradel analysis is the empirical precedent.
2. **The observation model for clustering**: reference- and
   collection-level clustering of occurrences, measured above.

Both need a declared target (C̄ for E2-mix) and the nearest mixture models
before any analysis. Neither needs a new theorem first.

**Recommended next decision, not an allocation:** keep Q-006 under review
at tier 2, with its question narrowed to these two items, or merge item 1
into Q-038's design as an observation-model requirement. I lean to the
merge: Q-038 already holds the dataset, the event definition and the
selectivity target.
