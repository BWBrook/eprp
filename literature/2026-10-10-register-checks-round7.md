# Register checks, round 7: Q-037, older sketches, and the Raup–Sepkoski legacy

Owner: Claude | Date: 10 October 2026 | Supports [the register](../ideas/register.md)

**Method.**
- Barry asked in Claude chat on 10 October for the remaining checks before the
  next Codex round. He also posed a provocation, quoted exactly: "Raup and
  Sepkoski did a lot of foundational work 30-40 years ago. Is any of that worth
  revisiting, in light of modern knowledge/methods, or has it been done to
  death already? Everything from the fields of bullets, to did bolides cause
  all (almost) major extinction events, to what the diversification and
  extinction curves really look like when we apply our most rigorous

- Six bounded Sonnet scouts checked prior work, stopping after two empty
  rounds. Claude checked decisive abstracts directly in Crossref.
- Depth labels: meta = Crossref only; abs = abstract; sect = sections; full =
  full text; full\* = full text read through a summarising fetch tool.

  Meishan.
- The modern contrast is a size signal, not a trophic one.

  present? Within-event order needs a Signor–Lipps null.

## The Raup–Sepkoski legacy: what is done, what is live, where it sits

Barry's question was whether this foundational work is worth revisiting, or
done to death. Two scouts covered eight classic ideas, with about 15–25
records each and 3–5 read at section or full depth. Claude checked the
decisive abstracts. Short answer: three ideas are settled or nearly so, two
are live with no modern re-test found, and three survive as sharper estimands.
After Codex's review (76/1380), "never done" claims are read as "not found in a
bounded search". Q-038 was reformulated from a variance partition into a
question of held-out predictability.

| Classic idea | Original | Status now | Register |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bad genes or bad luck (field of bullets, selectivity) | Raup 1991 book; Raup 1994 PNAS (10.1073/pnas.91.15.6758): selectivity "present but minor" | **Live; not found posed as an estimand in a bounded search.** The ingredients exist: selectivity regimes (Jablonski 1986, 10.1126/science.231.4734.129); range selectivity weakened or eliminated in mass extinctions (Monarrez et al. 2023, 10.1098/rsos.230795); non-transferable selectivity (Foster et al. 2023); frailty plus luck (Tikhonov et al. 2026, 10.64898/2026.07.21.739780; its 57.3% is a share of simulated deaths, not a variance share). No variance partition found | **Q-038 (new; reformulated after Codex as held-out predictability, tier 2)**; links Q-012, Q-022 |
| Periodicity of extinction | Raup & Sepkoski 1984 PNAS (10.1073/pnas.81.3.801) | **Disputed; no re-test with propagated timescale uncertainty found.** Feng & Bailer-Jones 2013 (Bayesian, against) versus Melott & Bambach 2014 (27 Myr, p ≈ 0.02, fixed windows); Erlykin et al. 2018 (fragile) | **Q-041 (new, tier 2)** |
| Species-level magnitude by reverse rarefaction | Raup 1979 Science (10.1126/science.206.4415.217): "as many as 96 percent" | **Revised once; no simulation validation of the inversion found.** Stanley 2016 (about 81%); McKinney 1995 (random-within-genus bias); direct regional data give 62% (Wang et al. 2014) | **Q-042 (new, tier 2)**; shares machinery with Q-013 |
| Diversity curve under bias correction | Raup 1976 (10.1017/s0094837300004929); Sepkoski's compendium curve | **Live dispute about the estimand.** Close et al. 2020 (spatial structure, little long-term rise) versus Cermeño et al. 2022 (hotspot-driven rise); Alroy et al. 2008; Fan et al. 2020 | **Q-040 (new, tier 2)**; overlaps Codex's Q-010 |
| Three evolutionary faunas and coupled logistic | Sepkoski 1981 (10.1017/s0094837300003778), 1984 (10.1017/s0094837300008186) | **Structure re-tested; dynamics not.** Four mega-assemblages (Rojas et al. 2021, 10.1038/s42003-021-01805-y); diversity dependence near-universal in aggregate (Foote 2023; Rineau et al. 2022) | **Q-039 (new, tier 2)** |
| Impacts as the main cause (kill curve) | Raup 1991 (10.1017/s0094837300010332), 1992 (10.1017/s0094837300012227) | **Coincidence tests done and negative beyond the K–Pg** (Green et al. 2022, 10.1073/pnas.2120441119; Arens & West 2008). The strong kill-curve form, with an impact flux corrected for completeness, is untested but narrow. The impact-flux record itself is disputed (Mazrouei et al. 2019; Hergarten et al. 2019) | Sub-question added to **Q-011** |
| The Big Five as a distinct class | Raup & Sepkoski 1982 (10.1126/science.215.4539.1501) | **Largely settled against.** Magnitudes form a continuum (Wang 2003; Alroy 2008); "the Big Five turn out not to be statistically distinct from background rates of extinction" (Marshall 2023, 10.1017/ext.2022.4); graptoloid rate magnitudes are continuous and unimodal (Crampton et al. 2026) | Recorded in **Q-011** |
| Random clades (MBL models) | Raup, Gould, Schopf & Simberloff 1973 (10.1086/627905); Gould et al. 1977 (10.1017/s009483730000508x) | **Largely answered.** Real and random clades are similar (Gould et al. 1977). Clade asymmetries are "mostly indistinguishable" from constant birth–death in 27 radiations (Quintero et al. 2025, 10.1101/2025.03.20.644316); age–rate scaling is a sampling artefact (Reijenga & Close 2025). A test against a sampling-aware null on PBDB orders would probably confirm the null. Low value | No new entry; relevant to Q-026 |

**Unresolved tension worth logging.**
- Quintero et al. 2025 find species-level lineages becoming more vulnerable
  with age.
- Tikhonov et al. 2026 find age-dependent genus survival fully explained by
  survivorship bias, with no change in lineage properties.

These differ in scale and method, and both bear on Q-012.

**Corrections from the scouts.**
- "Fair game" as a Raup category could not be verified. The 1991 book has a
  section on "wanton extinction"; "field of bullets" is confirmed through
  secondary sources.
- Arens & West 2008 found that neither LIPs nor impacts alone raised
  extinction frequency, but their co-occurrence did. The earlier Q-011 summary
  ("uncorrelated with LIP or crater size alone") stands as worded.
