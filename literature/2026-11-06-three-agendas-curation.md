## López-García & Moreira: a formal eight-item agenda

The [public PMC author manuscript](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4640172/)
permits direct inspection of the formal question box and explanatory sections.
Its eight grouped themes, in order, concern ancestral eukaryotic complexity;
mitochondrial ancestry and metabolism; host ancestry; host cellular capabilities;
metabolic partnerships; acquisition timing; nuclear origins and selective drivers;
and membrane transformation. The box contains 19 question marks. The restricted
XML independently gives 19 within its box, whereas the full extracted text
yielded 29 machine candidates.

A later XML selector check confirmed eight native box paragraphs,
`par0095`–`par0130`, matching those eight groups in order. Their interrogative
counts are 2, 3, 4, 2, 1, 3, 3 and 1.

The main text separately develops four mechanistic challenges: metabolic
partnership, mitochondrial timing, nuclear evolution and membrane evolution.
These overlap the box and are not four additional priorities. The substantive
tension is between improving ancestry estimates and explaining viable mechanisms:
the authors argue that better phylogenetic placement alone does not settle the
transition. This is a 2015 agenda; its unresolved-status claims require a fresh
literature check before use as contemporary gaps. No exact question wording is
reproduced here.

## Abdelhady et al.: a technology review, not a numbered agenda

The [publisher's publicly indexed abstract, introduction and section excerpts](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1464343X2300328X)
support a review of imaging, molecular evidence, computational analysis and
high-resolution shell records. Its bibliometric component covers 2000–2022
Scopus records restricted to Earth Sciences. The limitations excerpt acknowledges
that keyword prominence can exaggerate emerging techniques relative to routine
ones; frequency is not scientific importance.

Restricted-side XML inspection finds 21 section elements, no question box, and
six interrogatives confined to two prose paragraphs: three in the introduction
and three in a future/limitations section. Those are candidate prompts, not an
author-designated list. The introduction's examples concern origins, innovation
and early animal diversification. Accordingly, curate this source as a methods
and limitations review with illustrative scientific motivations. The prospect
of faster automated classification does not itself demonstrate biological
discrimination, taxonomic transferability or removal of sampling bias. Direct
publisher-page retrieval returned 403; the publicly indexed rendering supplied
only the sections stated above, not full-text access.
