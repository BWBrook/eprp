## The residual being checked

Q-012 asks two things:
- Does a relative's survival (sister, or ancestor) predict a held-out
  lineage's survival, under rival models matched on marginal survival?
- Does inherited susceptibility then explain the apparent effect of age on
  extinction?

The rival models are intrinsic ageing, unshared frailty (heterogeneity that
is not inherited), and inherited frailty.

**Inference.** Relatedness structure in extinction is estimated in-sample
(Smits, Tamre & Parsons, Roy et al.). Tikhonov et al. supply the
non-inherited frailty null and explicitly name the lineage-resolved
comparison as not done. **No held-out test of relatives' survival under
models matched on marginal survival was found.** Scoop risk remains in this
active field.

## A new data resource

Lamyman, Fordham & Pearson 2026, "Phylogeny of the Cenozoic planktonic
foraminifera", J Syst Palaeontol 10.1080/14772019.2026.2669255 (abstract,
via the subagent). It updates and extends the Aze et al. 2011 lineage tree:
it adds 136 morphospecies and 29 genera, removes 15, and is calibrated to
GTS2020. It should replace Aze 2011 as Q-012's first tree, with Triton for
occurrences.

## The design gate, next (not modelling)

1. **Support.** Count the usable relative pairs in Lamyman's tree with
   Triton occurrences:
   - sister pairs, and ancestor–descendant pairs;
   - both durations observed;
   - a prediction landmark that precedes the outcome.
2. **Identification.** Shared exposure is the main rival to inherited
   susceptibility. Sisters often share environment and geography, so a
   relative's survival can predict a lineage's survival without any
   inherited risk. The design needs a contrast that separates the two, for
   example relative pairs that diverged in habitat or region, or a
   covariate for co-occurrence.
3. **Rival models matched on marginal survival:**
   - intrinsic ageing (a Weibull-type hazard);
   - non-inherited frailty (Tikhonov-type);
   - inherited frailty (correlated along the tree).
   All three must be fitted to the same marginal survival. Then check by
   simulation, at the actual number of pairs, whether held-out prediction
   of relatives' survival separates inherited from non-inherited frailty.
4. **Outcome-neutral rule.**
   - A gain in held-out prediction above a skill threshold set in advance
     supports inheritance.
   - An interval below the threshold is an informative negative: no
     detectable inheritance beyond shared exposure.
   - Too few pairs, or a simulated discrimination too weak, is inadequate
     support, and the question is parked.

**Observation model to declare:**
- Triton sampling and detection;
- the morphospecies concept (Lamyman's lineage approach addresses
  pseudoextinction);
- age uncertainty in GTS2020 calibrations.

It fits Barry's deep-time weighting: the question is pure deep time, and
any modern bearing comes after.
