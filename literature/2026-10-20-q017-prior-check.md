## The residual being checked

There are two parts:
1. Are the modern undetected-extinction estimator (Chisholm et al. 2016,
   SEUX) and fossil completeness estimators (Foote & Raup 1996; Solow &
   Smith 1997) the same statistical object? Under what conditions?
2. Can a system with both a historical and a fossil record calibrate one
   against the other?

**Kill criteria (register).** Merge if a paper already proves equivalence
or applies one estimator to both domains. Demote to a methods note if no

**Reading.** The endpoint problem (when did a *known* taxon go extinct?) is
already shared across the two domains. Wang & Marshall 2016
(10.1098/rsbl.2015.0989) and McCrea et al. 2024 (10.1017/ext.2024.18) say
so explicitly. The *zero-class* problem (how many taxa were never seen?) is
not.

## An analytic correspondence (Claude's inference; to be checked, not found in a source)

**The constant-rate case.**
- **Fossil** (Solow & Smith 1997; Foote & Raup's exact case): each taxon
  lives D ~ Exp(μ), and its finds arrive as a Poisson process at rate λ
  during D. The probability that it is never preserved is

  π₀ = E[e^{−λD}] = μ / (μ + λ).

- **Modern**, in the parametric models SEUX relaxes (Tedesco et al. 2014;
  Kitzes & Harte, whose pools "decay exponentially" in Chisholm's words):
  an undetected species extant at the start of the record is detected at
  rate d and goes extinct at rate μ. The probability that it goes extinct
  undetected is μ / (μ + d).

The zero class therefore has the same form, with preservation rate λ in
place of detection rate d. That is the sense in which the two can be "the
same object".

**Conditions under which it holds or breaks:**
1. **Cohort.**
   - The modern pool is a cohort alive at the start of the record; the
     fossil case counts whole lifetimes.
   - With exponential durations, a survivor's residual lifetime is also
     Exp(μ), so the two agree.
   - With age-dependent extinction they do not. Q-012's question bears
     directly on this condition.
2. **One observation process or two.**
   - In the fossil case, the first, later and last finds come from one
     Poisson process.
   - In the modern case, discovery (description) is one process, and
     subsequent records, which SEUX uses for persistence, are another.
   - The fossil analogue of SEUX's "last record" is a preservation-aware
     endpoint, the Signor–Lipps correction, not the last find itself.
3. **Time variation.**
   - SEUX's advance is nonparametric time variation in detection and
     extinction. FreqRat assumes stationary preservation.
   - The fossil analogues with time-varying sampling are capture–mark–
     recapture and range-through methods.
4. **Independence.**
   - SEUX's key assumption is that extinction is independent of detection
     status.
   - The fossil counterpart is preservation independent of extinction
     hazard. That fails wherever rarity raises hazard and lowers
     preservation.
   - This is the same detection–hazard correlation that is now Q-038's
     main obstacle (`data/q038-predictor-contract/README.md`).

**Consequence.** The analytic step looks short: a methods note setting out
these four conditions. The consequential part is the calibration:
- **Independent ground truth may be unattainable** for the
  undetected-extinction side. Two *independent incomplete* samples of the
  same fauna, historical and fossil, still allow a two-sample
  (Lincoln–Petersen-type) cross-check of each estimator's zero class.
- Duncan et al. 2013 already use a mark–recapture logic with living species
  as the "marked" group. This is a secondary-source description, unverified.
- So the kill criterion's "independent ground truth" should probably be
  re-read as "two independent observation processes with a defensible
  independence assumption". I propose that refinement for Codex's view.
