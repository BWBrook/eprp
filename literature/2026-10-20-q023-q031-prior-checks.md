## Q-023: are crises origination failures? Residual open; develop

**The residual.** Recompute Bambach, Knoll & Wang's (2004) partition of
diversity loss into origination deficit and extinction excess for every
major crisis, using sampling-standardised estimators: three-timer,
gap-filler, capture–mark–recapture or PyRate.

**What the sources say:**
- **Bambach et al. 2004.** Paleobiology, 10.1666/0094-8373(2004)030<0522:oeamdo>2.0.co;2.
  The abstract was checked by Claude. Data: the Sepkoski compendium,
  unstandardised. Only the end-Ordovician, end-Permian and end-Cretaceous
  are extinction outliers within their stratigraphic neighbourhoods. The
  scout reports the abstract as attributing the late Frasnian and
  end-Triassic losses mainly to low origination.
- **Alroy 2008.** PNAS, 10.1073/pnas.0802597105, PMC2556405.
  - Read second-hand: the scout read it through a fetch summariser. Claude's
    own full-text retrieval failed, so treat this as unverified.
  - In the new PBDB data (three-timer rates, roughly 11-Myr bins), the
    end-Triassic is "a legitimate mass extinction". For the Late Devonian
    it cites Bambach, with no new partition, and notes that its
    importance "already has been called into question".
  - **Register correction:** "Alroy 2008 keeps the Frasnian result" was an
    inference. Alroy reports no Frasnian partition, and 11-Myr bins cannot
    resolve one.
- **No all-event standardised recomputation was found.**
  - divDyn (Kocsis et al. 2019, 10.1111/2041-210x.13161) gives subsampled
    rates across the Phanerozoic. Its abstract says "only three mass
    extinctions stand out clearly", but it does not partition.
  - Foote 2000 gives an era-scale relative-role reversal, not an
    event-by-event partition.
- **Event-specific standardised work:**
  - *End-Guadalupian:* origination-driven in marine invertebrates (Clapham
    et al. 2009, 10.1666/08033.1, abstract checked). Extinction-driven in
    Karoo tetrapods (Day et al. 2018, 10.1017/pab.2018.17).
  - *End-Triassic:* extinction peaks, with some origination decline
    (Kiessling et al. 2007; Kocsis et al. 2014).
  - *Late Devonian:* the origination-failure claim rests on compendium data
    and clade phylogenies (Stigall 2010, 2012; Lamsdell & Selden 2016). The
    only standardised Frasnian–Famennian signal found is an extinction
    spike, in a 2011 conference abstract (Kiessling et al., no DOI).
  - *End-Ordovician:* graptolite capture–mark–recapture shows elevated
    extinction and no origination (Mitchell et al. 2025).
  - *Toarcian and Jurassic/Cretaceous:* none found.
- **Methods.**
  - Origination is estimated less faithfully than extinction (Foote 2003,
    10.1086/345841, abstract checked).
  - Range-based rates are distorted by edge effects, the Signor–Lipps
    effect and the Pull of the Recent (Alroy 2014, 10.1666/13036).
  - So Bambach's partition rests on the weaker half of the rate pair,
    estimated from range data.

**Disposition: develop.**
- This is a bounded recomputation with a clear decision value. Each of
  Bambach's two origination-failure verdicts either survives standardised
  estimators or does not.
- **Next:** a design note.
  - **Partition:** the diversity change at each crisis interval split into
    an origination deficit and an extinction excess, each relative to the
    interval's own stratigraphic neighbourhood (Bambach's definition),
    computed from standardised rates.
  - **Data:** stage-level PBDB marine genera (divDyn's binning).
  - **Rates:** three-timer and gap-filler, with uncertainty across
    subsampling trials.
  - **Diagnostics:** an explicit check of origination edge and smearing
    effects at each boundary.
  - **Cost:** the pilot needs only open PBDB data and an existing R
    package.
- **Kill:** park if standardised origination rates are too unstable at
  crisis boundaries to sign the partition. Report that as a
  methodological negative.

## Q-031: is shrinking under warming predictable? Partly occupied; develop the narrowed version

**The kill check (Nätscher, De Baets & Kiessling 2026, PNAS
10.1073/pnas.2505564123).**
- **The published SI could not be read:** PNAS returns 403, and the paper
  is not in PMC.
- **Every readable version lacks both a within- versus among-lineage
  partition and a comparison with the temperature–size rule:**
  - the bioRxiv supplement (10.1101/2025.07.25.666730);
  - the Zenodo deposit (10.5281/zenodo.15096088): README, data and R
    script;
  - the author's FAU thesis chapter.
- The published reference list does not cite Forster et al. 2012 or a
  Price-equation source.
- **Kill criterion: very probably clear.** To close it, someone with
  institutional access needs to search the PNAS SI for "lineage",
  "turnover", "Price" and "temperature–size".

**A finding from Claude's re-run of their deposited analysis:**
- The deposited `combi_samp` table has 5,655 rows. Their Fig. 3 regression
  uses 4,976 rows with finite lnRR, and **4,544 of them are background rows
  coded warming = 0**.
- That reproduces the published slope: −0.018 lnRR per °C (SE 0.0019,
  R² 0.018).
- Restricted to the 432 crisis rows, the slope is −0.025 (SE 0.0085,
  R² 0.020, p = 0.003).
- So the published slope partly contrasts background with crisis. The
  within-crisis dose response is negative too, but rests on five warming
  values (4, 5, 6 and 10 °C) assigned one per event.
- This concerns the deposited script; the published SI was not seen.

**Other work:**
- **Single-event partitions disagree.**
  - Within-lineage change dominates in Permian–Triassic foraminifera (Rego
    et al. 2012), Toarcian belemnites (Rita et al. 2019) and PETM mammals
    (Rankin et al. 2015).
  - Turnover dominates in Toarcian bivalves (Piazza et al. 2020),
    Permian–Triassic ostracods (Nätscher et al. 2023) and Permian–Triassic
    bivalves (Foster et al. 2026).
  - No study regresses turnover share on warming magnitude or duration.
- **Hunt & Roy 2006** (PNAS 10.1073/pnas.0510550103) is the closest
  per-°C within-lineage test. In one deep-sea ostracod clade under
  *cooling*, size changed by about 29 µm per °C, matching the modern
  spatial slope. It is a Bergmann-type comparison, not against the
  experimental temperature–size rule.
- **Reversal on cooling:** single-lineage evidence only (Secord et al.
  2012; D'Ambrosia et al. 2017; Hunt & Roy 2006). Balk et al. 2019 test
  asymmetry in woodrats; there is no cross-event test.
- **The terrestrial arm is a real gap.** Nätscher excluded terrestrial
  taxa.
  - The scout's arithmetic from D'Ambrosia et al. puts equid change at
    roughly −4 to −6% per °C at both the PETM and ETM2. That is about ten
    times the experimental terrestrial value of about −0.5% per °C
    (Forster et al. 2012).
  - This is a hypothesis only: tooth area is a proxy, and the ΔT values
    are uncertain.

**Disposition: develop the narrowed version.**
- **Question:** within-lineage size change per °C *with event-specific
  local ΔT*, against the experimental temperature–size rule for each size
  class and habitat; plus turnover share against warming magnitude, across
  at least eight event × taxon series.
- **Most open arm:** terrestrial and mammal series.
- **Next:** a support audit of lineage-resolved series with local ΔT.
  Count the series; park if fewer than eight.
- **Closing the kill check:** institutional access to read the PNAS SI.

## Amendments accepted (Codex 76/2112)

**Q-023.**
- Develop a matched two-event design for the Frasnian and end-Triassic,
  Bambach's two origination verdicts, before any rate extraction.
- **Fix the accounting scale first.** For example, excess log depletion
  relative to baseline rates p₀ and q₀: ∫[(q − q₀) + (p₀ − p)] dt.
- That differs from actual decline. Terms can offset, and fractions can
  exceed 100%.
- **Predefine:**
  - background windows;
  - boundary placement (Bambach's substages against stages);
  - origination-detection lags;
  - sampling treatment, with common denominators.
- A larger standardised extinction peak alone does not overturn origination
  dominance. If equally defensible baselines reverse dominance, report the
  dependence and hold a general attribution.
- Alroy 2008's full text remains unverified, and the absence of an
  all-event standardised analysis is not established.

**Q-031.**
- *Corrected:* the Forster et al. terrestrial value (about −0.5% per °C) is
  conditional on about 100 mg dry-mass ectotherms. Comparing PETM and ETM2
  equids with it does not show a "tenfold" departure; that comparison is
  withdrawn.
- Keep two arms: an ectotherm temperature–size-rule arm, and a separate
  mammal, Bergmann, ecological and evolutionary arm. Within-lineage change
  alone does not identify plasticity.
- *Corrected:* there are **four** positive warming values (4, 5, 6 and
  10 °C) shared by **six** events: Permian–Triassic 114 rows,
  Triassic–Jurassic 16, Toarcian 194, OAE1a 4, OAE2 90, PETM 14. They are
  not "five values, one per event".
  - The crisis-only row-level slope is not an independent event-level
    test. Rows share temperatures, studies, clades and intervals.
- **Gate replaced.** "Eight events", "eight event × taxon series" and
  "eight series" were inconsistent. The support table will record:
  - event ID, lineage continuity and site or study;
  - local temperature and its uncertainty;
  - size scale, habitat and thermoregulation;
  - independent contrasts and covariate overlap, assessed before counting
    rows.
- Hold a cross-event regression if it is confounded with clade or method,
  or driven by one event. Prior occupancy stays provisional until the
  PNAS SI is read.
