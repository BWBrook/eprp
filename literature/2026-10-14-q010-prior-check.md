# Q-010: engineering associations are occupied; a causal residual needs support

Owner: Codex. Bounded prior check, 14 October 2026, under Barry's approved
checkpoint tranche. No fit, dataset transformation or author contact.

**Disposition: develop a specific support/design contrast; retain under review,
tier 2.** Generic engineer-associated diversity is already occupied. The
remaining question is whether a particular innovation changed the biological
opportunity available under comparable external conditions. Novelty and a
usable historical comparison remain unverified.

## Closest direct prior

Cribb, Darroch and Gearty (2026), *Marine biodiversity was greater in the
presence of ecosystem engineers throughout the Phanerozoic*, Nature
Communications 17, 9166,
[10.1038/s41467-026-73628-6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-73628-6).
Relevant Results, Discussion, Methods and availability statements read by the
ecology scout and parent. Initial Nature/PMC retrievals failed; the
[Nature text with its cookie-error parameter](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-73628-6?error=cookies_not_supported)
was accessible.

The study compares formation-by-stage Shannon diversity with and without
reef-builders or bioturbators. Proportions are occurrence records, not specimen
abundances. Engineer assignments use body-fossil ecology; ichnotaxa are excluded.
Main responses include engineers, with an exclusion sensitivity. The paper
examines lithology/bathymetry subsets, climate associations and extinction
intervals. It reports positive diversity associations through much of the
Phanerozoic. These findings already occupy a broad engineering/diversity test.

**Our inference:** those contrasts do not directly estimate an environmental
carrying capacity or the counterfactual effect of introducing an innovation.
This is a distinction of target, not a claim that the authors ignored sampling
or physical context. Repeating their association with a different diversity
index would not establish EPRP's remaining contribution.

The [public repository](https://github.com/atcribb/Ecosystem-Engineers-Effects)
lists processed engineer/occurrence data and R scripts. Its README still labels
the manuscript as in revision and requests contact before script use. The
paper points to that repository and
[Zenodo 19824423](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19824423) as available resources.
Version/reuse wording should be reconciled before adopting scripts. This check
inspected text and file listings only; it did not execute or certify the archive.

## A sharper biological route, still provisional

The independent blue-sky scout identified a possible Q-010 mechanism: could
burrowing make an established benthic community persist under conditions that
prevent initial establishment? See
[D3](../ideas/2026-10-14-blue-sky-ideation-codex.md#d3-did-burrowers-change-the-conditions-under-which-deep-sea-ecosystems-could-persist).
This changes the target from fitting a latent diversity ceiling to testing
history-dependent habitability. It is a candidate refinement, not an adopted
replacement for Q-010.

The basic mechanism also has close priors:

- Buatois et al. (2025),
  [10.1073/pnas.2414752122](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2414752122): early deep-sea
  colonisation and a proposed bioirrigation feedback. Abstract and data
  declaration checked; detailed methods/SI not adjudicated.
- Van de Velde et al. (2020),
  [10.1016/j.gca.2020.02.021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.gca.2020.02.021): a
  mechanistic precedent for sediment alternative states. Abstract and indexed
  primary passages checked; complete manuscript retrieval failed.

Thus neither feedback nor alternative states is novel. A remaining comparison
would need independently constrained ancient establishment and persistence
thresholds, against oxygenation, organic delivery, substrate and ordinary
demographic lags. It must not infer external oxygen from the same burrows whose
effects it is trying to estimate. Engineering can also change geochemical
proxies and preservation, as well as animal habitat.

## Next support gate

1. Select one innovation and define the biological outcome independently of
   engineer presence. Choose local persistence, non-engineer occupancy or an
   explicitly defined richness target; do not switch among them after fitting.
2. Identify repeated sections/basins with independent chronology, external
   conditions and pre-arrival evidence. Formation-stage associations alone do
   not supply a staggered-arrival causal panel.
3. Specify a finite-rate, external-forcing comparator and an engineering
   mechanism. Include shared environmental change, dispersal, preservation
   change and inter-basin spillovers. Separate externally imposed conditions
   from conditions caused by engineers.
4. Ask whether their predictions differ on the actual joint support, with a
   consequential contrast chosen before results. A precise small contribution
   is informative; a wide interval or flexible ceiling is not.

Park empirical development if no supported comparison separates the mechanisms.
Do not move to a large PBDB fit merely because processed data are available.
The next owner remains Codex for an agreed bounded support/design brief;
coordinate with the blue-sky merge before duplicating D3 or D8 work.
