## The residual being checked

There are three parts:
1. A fossil **rate** of thermal-niche evolution, separated from range or
   habitat tracking and from plasticity.
2. A test of whether faster adapters survived warming.
3. The sharpest version: a same-shell calcification temperature against
   ambient temperature across hyperthermals, with a fixed-niche null run
   through the same climate model and sampling.

## Reading

- **Stasis of occupied niches over Pleistocene timescales is established**
  (Antell) for foraminifera. So is the predictive success of *static*
  thermal niches for survival through warming (Reddin; Kiessling's 2026
  abstract).
- **Not found:**
  - an estimate of realised thermal-limit change across a hyperthermal
    that is net of habitat tracking;
  - a test of whether such lability *adds* predictive value for survival
    beyond static niches.
- **The obstacle in the register stands.** Plasticity and evolution cannot
  be separated except by timescale arguments; Ying et al. do exactly that.

**A narrowed estimand (proposed).** Measure realised thermal-limit
**lability**: the change in a lineage's calcification temperature, relative
to ambient, that exceeds a fixed-niche-plus-habitat-tracking null. The null
uses the Jonkers–Kučera tracking response, pushed through the same climate
field and sampling.
- Plasticity and evolution are *not* separated. The claim is lability,
  not adaptation.
- The survival half becomes a held-out comparison: does lability improve
  prediction of which lineages survived, beyond a static-niche model? This
  is the same predictive framing as Q-038.
- A negative result, where static niches suffice, would still be
  informative. It would strengthen the static-niche reading of
  extinction risk under warming.

## Disposition and next decision

- **Disposition: develop**, for one bounded support audit. Tier 2 is
  retained.
- **Support audit.** For the PETM, ETM2 or the Middle Eocene Climatic
  Optimum, list species-specific (ideally single-specimen) Mg/Ca or δ18O
  calcification-temperature series with:
  - an independent ambient estimate (TEX86, clumped isotopes or model
    output);
  - at least two sites;
  - lineages with known fates.
- **Park** if fewer than a handful of lineage × site series exist with
  independent ambient temperatures. The survival comparison would then
  have no power.
- **Watch** for a peer-reviewed version of Kiessling et al. 2026.
