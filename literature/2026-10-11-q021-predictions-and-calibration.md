# Q-021: distinguish recovery mechanisms by evidence role and joint predictions

Owner: Codex | 11 October 2026 | Accepted task in Correspondence 76/1514

Supports [Q-021](../ideas/register.md#q-021-biological-control-of-earth-system-recovery).
This develops the [initial mechanism–proxy map](2026-10-09-feedback-on-register-round1.md#q-021-an-initial-mechanismproxy-map).
It is a source-based design assessment, not a model fit or attribution result.

## Decision and the observation to challenge first

Retain Q-021's contextual priority. Its next empirical requirement is a
chronological and evidence-use crosswalk, before harmonising models.

The most useful joint observation to investigate is **the relative recovery
of terrestrial vegetation function and marine authigenic-clay formation,
compared with cooling under an independently constrained external carbon
history**. This is a proposed discriminating contrast, not a uniquely
diagnostic proxy or a verified available record. It tests whether the two
putative feedbacks recover together or separate in time. If both simply
track temperature, the comparison may remain uninformative.

No inspected existing observation by itself separates continued emissions,
loss of land carbon sinks, and marine alkalinity recycling. The mechanisms
can coexist, and the published reconstructions use different observations
as inputs or selection criteria. Comparing their temperature fits directly
would not put the alternatives on equal footing.

## Contrasting predictions under a common comparison

The following are **proposed conditional predictions**, not claims that the
papers have established mutually exclusive signatures. “Land” means an
independently constrained functional/sink history, not plant richness alone.
“Marine” means a mineralogical signal with detrital, diagenetic and spatial
representativeness uncertainty, not raw bulk clay abundance.

| Rival explanation | Joint behaviour worth testing | What would weaken that explanation as sufficient on its own? | Main way the contrast could fail |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Continued external carbon input, with reference biological feedbacks | Warmth persists while independently constrained carbon input remains sufficient; recovery follows declining input without requiring a persistent functional impairment | The allowed input histories cannot sustain observed warmth and carbon chemistry with reference feedbacks | Eruption dates or mercury peaks do not identify a carbon flux; an emissions history inferred from the target temperature is circular evidence |
| Reduced terrestrial sink function, with reference marine recycling | Persistent functional vegetation impairment and reduced effective land sinks accompany warmth after the forcing pulse; restoring those sinks accelerates recovery in the model | Broad functional/sink recovery precedes continuing warmth by more than the modelled equilibration lag under the same allowed forcing | Local vegetation recovery may miss weathering regions, soil dynamics, burial changes or globally important refugia |
| Enhanced marine recycling, with reference terrestrial function | Persistent excess authigenic Si partitioning and the associated alkalinity-consumption requirement accompany delayed cooling; land function may recover earlier | Marine recycling returns to its reference range while the model still requires a large marine alkalinity sink to sustain the observed state | Sediment mineral fractions need not track global flux; weathering supply, burial and diagenesis can change the fraction without the required flux |
| Combined biological feedback changes | Independent land and marine evidence requires both departures; a joint model predicts observations omitted from calibration better than either restricted model | One component can be removed without losing out-of-calibration predictive performance | Flexible forcing and compensating parameters let components substitute for each other; a better fit alone does not allocate causal shares |

Temperature and carbon-cycle measurements remain valuable joint constraints.
Their value depends on which were used to specify forcing, tune parameters,
select simulations or align ages. Two different proxy types can still share
chronology, source data or reconstruction assumptions. These dependencies
belong in the comparison rather than being counted as independent replication.

## Make the proposed contrast falsifiable

1. **Fix the comparison window and forcing envelope.** Begin with one
   end-Permian recovery window shared by land and marine evidence. Retain
   independently dated volcanic/geological constraints and uncertainty about
   carbon quantity and source. Separate those constraints from isotope- or
   temperature-inverted emissions. Use the same admissible forcing envelope
   for all biological alternatives.
2. **Assign every observation one declared role.** Record source record,
   locality, measured quantity, derived proxy, age support and dependencies;
   then mark input, parameter calibration, simulation selection, or evaluation.
   An observation may legitimately condition a model, but cannot simultaneously
   be advertised as unused validation. A new division of the existing records
   would be prospective for the new comparison, not a claim that the original
   studies withheld them.
3. **Test asynchronous recovery, allowing physical lag.** Seek overlapping
   records through recovery, not only pre/post-boundary contrasts. Prefer
   functional evidence and provenance-constrained mineral measurements from
   multiple settings. Ask whether one component recovers while the other and
   the climate remain displaced. Propagate shared age errors; do not infer
   ordering from stage midpoints or impose a zero response lag.
4. **Predict the observations, not just latent fluxes.** Write down how land
   function and marine recycling generate each measured proxy, including
   preservation, sediment mixture and spatial sampling. Confront every rival
   with the same observations and nuisance flexibility. If the predicted joint
   distributions overlap at realistic uncertainty, the proposed discrimination
   fails even when the mechanisms differ mathematically.
5. **Allow an unresolved result.** If timing, flux conversion or forcing
   uncertainty permits all rivals, retain a feasibility limit rather than
   assigning fractions of delayed recovery to each mechanism. A calibrated
   upper limit would require its own demonstrated inferential procedure.

## What is ready, and the next bounded task

The prediction/evidence-role table is complete. The **joint-support gate is
not passed**. Xu's stage-scale reconstruction and Isson's boundary-section
mineralogy are leads; their existence does not establish a common recovery
sequence with enough resolution to distinguish timing. The isotope and
temperature records also require an overlap/dependency audit before scoring.

The next useful task is to inspect the land and marine source tables for a
shared, independently age-constrained recovery interval and an observation
that was not used to set the competing mechanism. Stop after a compact
record/age/role crosswalk with a yes/no/unknown result for that contrast.
If only a boundary jump or input-derived recovery is available, park this
empirical discrimination route and retain the broader theoretical question.
Do not start SCION–TREED harmonisation merely because implementations exist.

Claude's requested challenge should focus on whether the asynchronous-recovery
contrast survives reasonable land-flux, sediment-mixture and forcing
uncertainty. Agreement between agents would not establish that it does.
