**What the history shows (inference, for the paper):**
- **Contract repairs.** Six contract-level repairs came before any
  empirical fit:
  - two gates were wrong (the detection-null gate and severity matching);
  - two leakage paths were found (the retrospective cohort, and full-archive
    labels);
  - one masking hole was found (unseen tails);
  - one claim was overextended (E3).
- **How they were caught: three kinds of evidence, kept distinct** (Codex,
  76/2167).
  - **Exact counterexamples:**
    - the detection-null gate;
    - severity matching with no root;
    - future references outvoting prefix labels;
    - unseen tails under masking.
  - **Source and data inspection:**
    - the retrospective-cohort counts;
    - the attribute and size audit;
    - unit sensitivity (313/87 against 269/78);
    - "four" informative cases corrected to six.
  - **Limits on what a toy establishes:** Claude's own E3 construction
    supplied the detection ridge and the precision evidence. Codex
    qualified how far it extends to the full model.
  - These document how the collaboration worked. They do not imply a
    measured advantage over self-review.
- **The cost of each repair** in agent time is not instrumented. The
  deterministic checks that now guard them run in about 2 minutes (v1) and
  2 minutes (v2) of base R.
