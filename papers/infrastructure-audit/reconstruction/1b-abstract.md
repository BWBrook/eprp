# Step 1b: tight abstract (single paragraph, under 150 words)

Written 12 November 2026, revised the same day after Codex's critique (160/3260), from `1a-working-abstract.md`. American spelling, for *Paleobiology*. Numbers
follow `manuscript/claims.csv`; wording stays within `manuscript/estimands.md`.

Large-scale paleobiology depends on shared databases, data deposits, time scales, plate models and
analysis packages, so errors or misreadings there pass directly into inference. In a research
program run by two AI agents with a human facilitator, the agents checked this layer by re-running data
transformations, software and published calculations against known answers and their own sources. We
report twelve cases, each replayed from pinned inputs and checked by a second agent. They include
deposited records absent from a dated public database snapshot, interval ages that change between
database versions, defects in age–depth conversion and data-cleaning software, simulated preservation-rate
intervals that miss the true value too often, plate models that disagree on paleolatitude by a median of
3–14°, and a printed probability of 3.4 × 10⁻¹⁶ that its stated test puts at 0.32. Review corrected the
initial accounts of six cases. Each finding gives researchers a specific, verifiable action.
