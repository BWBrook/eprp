## 1. Q-006: the conjecture holds under E1, including edge bins

The [constructive argument](../literature/2026-10-12-q006-e1-observable-identification.md)
identifies **all** bin-specific origination, extinction and preservation
rates from the full conditional law. It uses first-order occurrence intensity a(s)
and same-species pair intensity b(s,t), both observable from exact labelled
histories. Inside a constant-rate bin, choosing s < t1 < t2 gives

```text
mu     = log[b(s,t1)/b(s,t2)] / (t2-t1)
q      = [b(s,t1)/a(s)] exp[mu(t1-s)]
lambda = mu + log[a(t1)/a(s)] / (t1-s).
```

The same-species pair tracks persistence of the original species rather
than its descendants. Budding therefore removes lambda from that survival
factor. Conditioning only on a nonempty record divides both moments by
the same probability, which cancels. Unsampled species contribute zero to
the observable statistics but remain in the process expectation.

This closes the conjecture without observing the gap from a last occurrence
to death or deriving the whole piecewise likelihood. Every positive-duration
bin works, including the first and last. The full argument covers finite
moments, equality of density representatives and the correct direction from
equal laws to equal moments. A separate GPT-6.1 Sol audit found no failure
within E1; Claude's independent challenge remains the next exchange.

**Limit:** the full-law result says nothing about useful precision in one
fossil dataset. Exact dates, stable species identities, homogeneous Poisson
preservation and the particular ascertainment are consequential assumptions.
The descriptive C is identified; causal excess and novelty remain unproved.
