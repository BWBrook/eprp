All of these match Codex. They are descriptive arithmetic, as Codex labels

temporal change, and the generic descriptive comparison is occupied.

**Two additions.**
1. **Site offsets are as large as the signal.** In the same stages,
   Takachiho sits about 4.7‰ below Taskent (−2.84 against 1.87). Site or
   archive offsets are therefore of the same order as the ~8‰ temporal
   change. That two sites with correlated age models show the same shift
   does not separate a seawater change from a common change in the archive.
2. **The pH rival is coupled to the mechanism, not just a nuisance.**
   - Taylor allows pH and growth-rate effects of up to about 11‰.
   - Isson's marine-recycling scenario predicts sustained low pH.
   - So a pH-sensitive carbonate fractionation would covary with the very
     mechanism under test. A fixed archive mapping would bias the comparison
     towards or against that mechanism.
   - This is the external review's F-04 point (mechanism-specific
     dependencies) in a concrete form.

## 2. Reopening requirements for a carbonate Li route

Reopen only when **all three** hold. Fewer than three should not reopen it.
1. **An independent, paired constraint on time-varying archive
   fractionation**, for example a pH proxy measured in the same section
   through recovery. Boron-isotope pH reconstructions exist around the
   boundary itself (Clarkson et al. 2015, 10.1126/science.aaa0193; Jurikova
   et al. 2020, 10.1038/s41561-020-00646-4; DOIs checked, contents not
   re-read). Whether any extend through the Anisian recovery is unverified.
2. **Sampling that resolves the Olenekian–Anisian transition** at a spacing
   finer than the predicted lead or lag between scenarios. Taskent's 5.08 Myr
   gap fails this.
3. **A mechanism-specific prediction contrast**, opposite in sign or clearly
   separated in magnitude, that survives after each scenario's *own* pH
   trajectory is applied to the archive mapping. Overlap of marginal bands is
   not the test (F-04).
