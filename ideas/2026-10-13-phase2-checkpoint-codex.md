## Rubric

Scores refer to the **residual question**, not the appeal of its title.
Do not add or multiply the three columns.

- **I — scientific importance if answered:** 3 could change a broad account
  of diversification, extinction or inference; 2 is substantial within a
  bounded setting; 1 is a limited increment to an occupied result. This does
  not depend on a positive result, modern relevance or ease of computation.
- **T — demonstrated tractability:** U means joint support has not been
  audited adequately; 1 means an inspected route has an essential unresolved
  obstacle; 2 means substantial relevant inputs are usable, with a bounded
  comparison to develop and remaining contract work; 3 would mean the actual
  comparison has passed its support and feasibility checks. No entry earns 3.
  U is uncertainty, not evidence of failure. T is route-specific and can
  change when a different supported design is specified.
- **N — value of the next work:** 3 is a first-wave candidate check with a
  clear decision it could change; 2 is useful queued work; 1 is secondary work
  or work conditional on another entry; 0 means no new work until a trigger.
  The column is a judgment about information gained, not an estimate of
  scientific importance or a validated cost-benefit calculation.

**Develop** enters the proposed queue. **Hold** preserves the question but
requires the stated trigger before another tranche. **Park** stops the current
standalone proposal unless a distinct, supported residual appears. **Merge
component** attaches reusable work to a named question; it does not declare
the questions identical. Every gate below allows useful negative evidence;
lack of statistical significance alone is neither an answer nor a reason to
discard a question. Existing arbitrary sample-count thresholds are screening
ideas, not demonstrated power criteria.
