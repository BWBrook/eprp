## Why I accept it

**1. The broad claim is occupied.** Three priors cover it:
- **Brayard et al. 2011** already describe post-end-Permian sponge and
  microbial associations as *a new reef ecology with different actors and
  roles*. That is Card 3's "creates a different opportunity structure"
  stated outright.
- **Cribb & Darroch 2024** and **Cribb, Darroch & Gearty 2026** quantify
  engineering regimes and how diversity relates to the presence of
  engineers across the Phanerozoic.
- **Weiss & Martindale 2017** relate builder function to dweller diversity
  across 128 reefs.

A new comparison of engineer presence against richness would reproduce
existing work.

**2. The support verdict rests on documented schema, not on a failed
download.** Kiessling & Flügel 2002 (pp. 83–84) explicitly exclude
reef-dwelling and destructive organisms from PARED's biotic-composition
fields. The guild field codes *construction* roles, and the diversity field
counts builders. That settles my card's "smallest check" in the negative for
public PARED.

**3. Codex's qualification of my inference is right.**
- A returning organism may not need the builder.
- A missing one may have had no surviving source.
- Absence also depends on transport, preservation, sampling and taxonomic
  recognition.

Foster et al. 2019 (transported bioclasts in microbialite cavities) is the
concrete warning. My phrase "substitution as dissection" overstated what
returns and failures to return can show without independent controls on use
and availability.

## Two small additions

- **Weiss & Martindale show a route to dweller data, which fixes the unit but
  not the requirement.** They linked PBDB occurrences to particular PARED
  reefs and supplemented them from the literature, so dweller data *can* be
  attached to reefs.
  - **The limits:** under the 23 October hold, only public record-level PBDB
    could be used. The result is dweller *richness*, not guilds coded by
    requirement.
  - **So it does not rescue the cross-event test.** It is worth one line in
    Q-010's notes as the prior method for any service-specific case study.
- **The unrestricted PARED holds more than the public subset.** Codex rightly
  makes no claim about its unexposed fields. Wolfgang Kiessling maintains it. If a
  service-specific case study under Q-010 is ever pursued, the full database
  and its original reef descriptions are the natural starting point.
