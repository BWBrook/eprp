## 2. Critique of Codex's eight questions

**D1. Speciation as reset or transmission. Agree: a component of Q-012, not
a B entry.**
- It refines Q-012 usefully: biological renewal at budding is distinguished
  from anagenetic naming, and Codex notes the selection created by
  conditioning on a parent that survives to bud.
- I will fold it into Q-012's support and identification design as an
  event-level contrast, using the Lamyman et al. 2026 Zenodo database and
  supplements (each over 1 GB, not yet downloaded).

**D2. Juvenile viability and evolutionary routes in ammonoids. Strong;
support develop.**
- It is the clearest case in either list of simulation earning its place
  through a mechanism contrast (constraints on adults only against
  constraints at all stages) with a held-out prediction test.
- Additions:
  - **(a) Theoretical-morphospace priors** should be in the prior map:
    Ritterbush & Bottjer 2012, 10.1666/10027.1 (the Westermann
    morphospace of hydrodynamic shell shape), and the Raup coiling
    tradition. That separates the new residual (disconnected *sets of
    viable growth programmes*) from adult morphospace occupation.
  - **(b) Viability thresholds are the crux.** Any barrier must persist
    across plausible thresholds for hydrostatic stability and orientation,
    not one chosen cutoff. Codex flags hard cutoffs; I would make "a
    barrier robust to the threshold" the gate.
  - **(c) Data.** Walton & Korn's 177 species of growth curves are the
    route. Check how many sister transitions are resolved at the
    whorl-section level before simulating.

**D3. Burrowers and deep-sea persistence thresholds. Important; support
develop, but theory first.**
- **(a) A contrary prior on the global sign** is missing: van de Velde et
  al. 2018 (10.1038/s41467-018-04973-4) argue that early shallow burrowing
  *caused* ocean anoxia and warming. Local ventilation, which D3 proposes,
  and global deoxygenation can coexist. The contract must say which scale
  the threshold asymmetry concerns.
- **(b) The binding constraint is circularity of the proxies.** Engineers
  alter the redox proxies used to judge external oxygen. "Establishment
  versus persistence under independently constrained external conditions"
  may have very few qualifying sections. Palaeozoic "deep sea" is mostly
  slope and outer shelf preserved in accreted terranes.
- **Proposal: stage it.**
  1. A **conditional theoretical result** first: does sediment diagenesis
     with irrigation and colonisation produce hysteresis at realistic
     parameters?
  2. Then a section-support audit.
- If the theory gives no consequential asymmetry, the empirical route
  closes early, and that is a useful negative.

**D4. Reefs exporting functions. Agree hold.** Two observation problems are
first-order:
- origin attributed from a first occurrence in reef facies;
- PBDB ecospace categories too coarse to register functional novelty.

A check of phylogenetic origin data (beyond first occurrences) belongs in
its novelty gate.

**D5. Energetic autonomy of early terrestrial vertebrates. Agree hold.**
- The cheapest informative step is a conditional energetic bound: the
  consumer density compatible with a terrestrial-only budget under stated
  efficiencies.
- If that bound is loose, the question stays theoretical, as Codex says.

**D6. Dispersal value reversing with habitat geometry. Agree hold.** A
specific theory prior to check is the **intermediate-dispersal model**:
- Claramunt et al. 2012 (online 2011), 10.1098/rspb.2011.1922;
- Agnarsson, Cheng & Kuntner 2014, 10.1371/journal.pone.0086780.

It already predicts non-monotone diversification with dispersal. D6's
residual must be the *geometry-dependent reversal* in fossils, not
non-monotonicity itself.

**D7. Species sorting against within-lineage reproductive investment.
Agree hold.** Theory occupancy is the main risk:
- species-selection theory (Jablonski 2008,
  10.1146/annurev.ecolsys.39.110707.173510);
- the debate over sexual selection and extinction (Lumley et al. 2015,
  10.1038/nature14419, experimental).

The novelty gate should test whether *distinguishable transient
signatures* are predicted at all before any data inventory.

**D8. Angiosperm replacement and establishment rules. Agree hold; high
importance.**
- The rival-prediction table is the right first step.
- The establishment-when-rare versus persistence-when-common contrast
  parallels D3's establishment–persistence asymmetry. A shared theoretical
  template (thresholds created by engineering or feedback) could serve both
  without merging them.

**What both lists lack.** Both acknowledge an overrepresentation of marine
animals. There are no questions on:
- **plankton and microbes beyond foraminifera.** For example, did diatoms'
  rise reorganise the silica cycle and the fortunes of siliceous groups?
  Harper & Knoll 1975, 10.1130/0091-7613(1975)3<175:sdacre>2.0.co;2, is
  one foundational lead;
- **terrestrial invertebrates and insects;**
- **plants other than angiosperms.**

These are candidates for a later, small, independent expansion. They are
not to be added now.
