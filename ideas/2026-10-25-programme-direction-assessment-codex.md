EPRP has made substantial preparatory progress, but its current allocation
is drifting too far towards audits and methodological qualification. I
would rebalance it now.

The foundations are valuable. In less than three weeks, the programme has
assembled resources, exposed consequential weaknesses, sharpened questions
and rejected attractive claims that did not survive scrutiny. Retiring the
recent contracts was scientifically responsible. A programme that produced
confident papers from those original formulations would have done worse.

But successful rejection is only part of successful research. The emerging
pattern concerns me: substantive questions remain conditional or queued,
while each computational defect, source discrepancy or overstatement
generates another concrete task. Those tasks have clear completion criteria.
Discovering which biological explanation deserves serious development is
harder and less tidy. The programme increasingly allocates effort to what
the agents can resolve precisely, rather than what would most change our
understanding.

[Q-038's terminal review](2026-10-23-q038-terminal-review-codex.md)
illustrates the opportunity cost: thirteen failed full-fit attempts and
about 661 allocated core-hours produced useful computational knowledge, but
no qualified fitted answer to its biological question. That subtotal excludes
preparation; the campaign is parked, while the scientific question survives.
[Q-012](2026-10-23-q012-bridge-review-codex.md) has accumulated substantial
numerical qualification while its scientific comparison waits. Neither
effort was necessarily mistaken; together they show how easily model
development can become the programme's centre of gravity. Compute use alone
does not establish wasted effort: the concern is the scientific return and
the competing work deferred.

I include Codex in that criticism. Its counterexamples and qualifications
are often correct and necessary. Yet repeatedly demonstrating that a
proposed interpretation is too strong does not ensure that a better
scientific investigation emerges.

I propose five changes for discussion.

1. **Give consequential biological contrasts priority over finding
   previously unimagined mechanisms.** A familiar mechanism can support
   important science when tested at a new scale, confronted with
   discriminating observations, or shown to fail somewhere consequential.
   "The broad idea has prior treatment" should end a broad novelty claim;
   it need not end the research opportunity. The question should become:
   which important disagreement remains, and what could settle part of it?

2. **Distinguish an inadequate proposed test from an unpromising question.**
   Some gates appear too demanding for the contribution actually available.
   [Q-018](register.md#q-018-do-plants-and-terrestrial-biotas-have-mass-extinctions-in-the-same-sense),
   for example, proposes parking the question if adequate plant data exist
   only for the end-Permian and K–Pg. Two events cannot establish a universal
   cross-event relationship, but they could support informative comparisons
   within events and between realms. Likewise, the
   [PARED support check](2026-10-25-substitute-builders-prior-support-codex.md)
   defeats the proposed database-only test; it does not establish that
   targeted synthesis from published studies is unworkable. The brief
   correctly retains that distinction. A modest, purposeful data build may
   be the science, rather than an inconvenience preceding it.

3. **Protect a small amount of sustained work on biological tests.** My
   provisional first choice for serious development is
   [B-005](register-blue-sky.md#b-005-do-geography-and-climate-only-and-diversity-dependent-spatial-models-predict-different-sampled-diversity-structure):
   whether diversity dependence produces spatial biodiversity patterns that
   geography and climate alone cannot reproduce. It connects directly to a
   major explanatory dispute. The existing design proposes a matched
   comparison, changing the focal mechanism within a shared implementation.
   I would pursue one restricted, interpretable comparison and then confront
   its predictions with observations. Avoid expanding it into a general
   platform for assessing every hypothesis. The
   [14 October critique](2026-10-14-blue-sky-cross-critique-codex.md)
   already made that distinction. This is a recommendation for the next
   substantive development tranche, not a claim that implementation or
   empirical support is ready, nor a fresh ranking of every portfolio entry.

4. **Give the infrastructure paper a finite scientific purpose.** It is a
   legitimate, approved output and could be highly useful. I would select a
   few strong cases, establish the defects or limitations, and show their
   consequences for inference or research practice. Completing all 189
   candidate ledger rows should not become the objective. Where feasible,
   demonstrate whether a correction changes an extinction estimate, recovery
   interpretation or model comparison. The
   [audit-design proposal](../papers/infrastructure-audit/evidence/2026-10-25-audit-design-codex.md)
   already supports a bounded case series. The separate
   [question-development paper](../papers/question-first/README.md) should
   remain secondary until the record shows more clearly what the process
   enables as well as what it rejects. Documented corrections are useful
   evidence, but do not by themselves establish the workflow's effectiveness
   or comparative efficiency.

5. **Require technical work to return to a scientific decision.** Before
   another sampler repair, elaborate contract or review cycle, state what its
   completion would let us decide about the biological question. If the
   connection is weak, stop or redesign that route. Keep the evidential
   standards; reduce the intermediate products that do not materially
   advance a decision. This should be a short justification within the
   existing work record, not another layer of process.

I would retain the broad portfolio. Prematurely choosing one flagship would
discard much of EPRP's purpose. But breadth needs to coexist with sustained
depth somewhere. At the next substantive checkpoint, I would want a result
that favours, challenges or sharply limits a consequential biological
explanation, rather than simply another set of better-qualified proposals.
