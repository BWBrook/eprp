## Why the dense-metric explanation is insufficient

This is an analytical inference checked against saved draws, not a new
sampler experiment. Let U=-lp__ in Stan's unconstrained coordinates and
d=299. For any fixed positive-definite Gaussian mass matrix, kinetic energy
K has distribution chi-square(d)/2 independently of position. Thus
Var(H)=Var(U)+d/2. Stan saves energy at the selected NUTS endpoint. In the
stationary, exact-Hamiltonian limit, successive saved energy changes equal
the fresh kinetic energy minus the previous kinetic energy, with expected
squared difference d. The population counterpart of **Stan's actual
successive-difference diagnostic** is therefore

    E-BFMI = d / (Var(U) + d/2).

This follows from the version-pinned [NUTS endpoint implementation](https://github.com/stan-dev/stan/blob/v2.38.0/src/stan/mcmc/hmc/nuts/base_nuts.hpp#L75-L78),
[Gaussian dense kinetic energy](https://github.com/stan-dev/stan/blob/v2.38.0/src/stan/mcmc/hmc/hamiltonians/dense_e_metric.hpp#L18-L19)
and [CmdStan diagnostic](https://github.com/stan-dev/cmdstan/blob/v2.38.0/src/cmdstan/diagnose.cpp#L128-L145).
It is consistent with the energy-difference calculation in
[Betancourt 2016, section 3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/1604.00695#S3.SS2).
The paper's conditional-variance BFMI definition has a different factor-two
normalisation; the numerical comparison here uses the CmdStan convention.

| 1 | 883.30 | 149.46 | 149.07 | 294.68 | 0.290 |

The expected kinetic mean/variance are both 149.5 and the expected
energy-difference numerator is 299. Observed moments already closely match
these values. Broad potential-energy variation accounts for the observed
ratio. A constant dense metric, or an affine coordinate change, leaves the
ideal ratio unchanged. Removing the reported linear correlations therefore
does not supply the proposed BFMI-repair mechanism.

Finite integration error, finite-chain variability and incomplete exploration
can change the realised diagnostic. A dense fit might consequently report
a different value; it is not mathematically prohibited from crossing 0.3.
Quarter-chain values here range from 0.237 to 0.397. The present calculation
does not establish stationarity or a true limiting value below 0.3.
Likewise, the sigma/k/kappa correlations are consistent with a trade-off,
but do not prove the biological source of the sampling problem. Replace
the causal certainty in 76/1807 with that qualified interpretation.

## One candidate that preserves the scientific target

Fix a=0.2 and replace the innovation parameter eps with

    w_i = sigma^a * eps_i,
    w ~ normal(0, sigma^a),
    eps = w / sigma^a.

Keep the current x recurrence, logz, likelihood, selection integrals and
all other priors unchanged. The normal density includes the inverse-map
Jacobian -a*N*log(sigma); do not add it twice or give w a standard-normal
prior. Sigma and rho keep their existing constraints and priors.

At matched states, U_new=U_old+a*N*log(sigma). A saved-draw calculation gives:

| Chain | Var(U) current | Var(U) candidate | Ideal energy-refresh proxy, candidate |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 883.08 | 263.18 | 0.725 |
| 2 | 937.98 | 266.11 | 0.719 |
| 3 | 788.63 | 233.07 | 0.782 |
| 4 | 869.46 | 249.18 | 0.750 |

This table uses population-denominator empirical variances. The proxy is
**not a measured new-fit E-BFMI**. The candidate was chosen from a simple
conditional innovation-scale check before this energy-variance calculation:
the high/low-sigma variance ratio becomes approximately one at a=.2.
All four chains informed that initial check; splitting chains 1/3 and 2/4
subsequently provides a consistency check, not independent held-out evidence.
A descriptive a-grid does not authorize a fit search. In this family a=1
substantially worsens the energy proxy; this is not a result about every
possible centred parameterisation.

NumPy reconstruction across all 16,000 saved states agrees in x within
3.6e-15 and in the innovation-prior/Jacobian identity within 7.2e-14.
These checks support the algebra only. Partial scaling also reintroduces
some narrowing near sigma=0, outside the observed 0.145–3.237 range; that
is the strongest immediate counterargument and must be tested.
