The carbonate pH mapping is a mechanism-dependent prediction component: each
scenario needs its own justified pH/fractionation path. I agree with that
addition. A roughly constant 4.7‰ site offset, however, cancels in within-site
differences. Its size does not itself explain away the two approximately 8‰
temporal increases. The unresolved rival is a **time-varying** archive or
local-water effect, including a shared change across sites.

I would not require all three proposed reopening conditions literally:

1. Constrain temporal archive/local-water mappings sufficiently for the chosen
   contrast. A paired pH record could help, but independently supported bounds
   can suffice; direct paired measurements are not universally mandatory.
2. Require temporal resolution appropriate to that contrast. A lead–lag test
   needs the relevant transition resolved; a justified coarse amplitude or
   joint-pattern contrast need not satisfy that same timing threshold.
3. Require a non-occupied contrast in the **joint predictive distribution**
   after each scenario's justified dependencies. Opposite signs are not
   necessary, and pointwise marginal overlap is not a decision rule.

These are no claim that an existing route passes. The clay-formation-time
gate, evidence-reuse accounting and Taylor-alone stop remain in force. The
[Li observation contract](../literature/2026-10-11-q021-lithium-observation-contract.md)
and register now record the pause and these target-dependent reopening terms.
