## Card 1. What makes the order of disturbances matter?

**Claim.** When a biota meets two perturbations, their order changes long-term
outcomes (composition, persistence) only through specifiable intervening
processes:
- additive input: immigration, or recolonisation from refuges;
- transitions between types: adaptation, plasticity, switching partners;
- nonlinear dynamics: density or frequency dependence, Allee thresholds;
- doses that depend on state: environmental buffers;
- the absorbing boundary at extinction, combined with regrowth.

Without these, survival filters with fixed per-type rates commute, and
expected outcomes are order-independent.

**Why the answer matters.** Every claim of legacy or memory in the register
needs a null to beat:
- Q-007 and Q-009;
- Q-012's frailty contrast;
- the hidden-state test;
- N1.

This null comes with a diagnostic, because each breaker predicts *where*
order effects concentrate:
- near refuges;
- in plastic lineages;
- at extremes of density;
- across a whole struck place;
- in rare lineages.

It also carries population-viability reasoning about sequences of
catastrophes, which is Barry's own field, into macroevolution.

**Strongest rival.** Apparent order effects in data arise because the two
events differ in dose, forcing or sampling. The difference is in the events,
not their order.

**Discriminating result.** Use a minimal generative model that inserts each
process *alone* between the same two filters, and pass the output through a
common fossil observation model (detection, binning, range-through fates).
- **If the five signatures stay distinguishable,** the diagnostic can be
  used on data.
- **If they collapse,** it stays theory: a null and a warning.

**Nearest known prior.**
- **Stochastic demography is the important one [memory].** Long-run growth
  depends on the sequence of environments when projection matrices do not
  commute, as in Tuljapurkar's work. "Order matters if and only if the
  operators fail to commute" is therefore probably textbook for
  stage-structured populations.
- **Also:** compound and linked disturbance (Paine et al. 1998; Johnstone et
  al. 2016 [read, via the Brief B scout]); priority effects (Fukami 2015
  [title]); ecological memory (Ogle et al. 2015 [read, abstract]).
- **Register:** Q-007, Q-009, Q-014, Q-012; L2 and F1.
- **What might be new:** a translation to lineage-level macroevolution under
  a fossil observation model, with the absorbing-boundary prediction for
  rare lineages.

**Smallest check that could change the disposition.** A nearest-prior brief
covering:
- stochastic demography (non-commuting operators);
- compound-disturbance theory;
- priority effects;
- extinction-risk literature on the timing of catastrophes.

Then a small toy model with the observation layer: hours, not days.

**What would retire it:**
- the classification and its observable signatures are already published in
  a form that applies directly to fossil lineage data; or
- the signatures do not survive a realistic observation model.

In either case it survives only as the null *inside* other contracts.

## Card 2. Does the route and duration of a disturbance decide whether slow or fast life histories survive?

This card folds S1 into Codex's seed 1.

**Claim.** Whether slow or fast life histories are favoured depends on two
things:
- **which life stage a perturbation hits:** recruitment failure or adult
  mortality;
- **how long it lasts relative to generation time.**

Body-size selectivity in extinctions partly *indexes* these. So size
selectivity should reverse between perturbations with different routes and
durations, at matched physiological exposure. Prediction horizon (the
cue–future relationship at the response lag) is a second channel to keep
separate.

**Why the answer matters.** It joins the late-Quaternary result to deep
time:
- **the Quaternary result:** life history and ecology rather than size
  (Johnson 2002 [memory]);
- **its demographic mechanism:** Brook & Bowman 2004, checked by Codex;
- **the deep-time questions:** Q-028 (winners' traits), Q-031 (shrinking),
  Q-038 (predictability).

It predicts *reversals*, which are more informative than consistent effects.
And it bears on which life histories short and press disturbances favour
today.

**Strongest rival:**
- size acts directly, through metabolic, oxygen and thermal demand, as in the
  physiological habitat-loss models of Q-001; or
- size acts through range and abundance correlates;
- and fossil proxies for pace of life may not separate from size.

**Discriminating result.**
- **First, theory.** An age-structured model maps route and duration to
  selectivity on pace, fixing the predicted sign for each event type before
  any data are read.
- **Then data.** At matched size and physiological exposure, proxies for pace
  of life (lifespan and growth from growth increments, histology, larval
  mode) should predict survival in *opposite* directions for:
  - short events that interrupt recruitment, such as an impact winter, which
    should favour long-lived adults;
  - prolonged press events, which should favour fast turnover.

**Nearest known prior:**
- Johnson 2002 and Brook & Bowman 2004;
- size selectivity across mass extinctions (Payne, Heim and Monarrez and
  colleagues [memory]);
- Lilliput patterns;
- larval mode not selected in mass extinctions (Jablonski 1986 [memory]),
  which is a classic case where a life-history trait *stops* mattering;
- *Lystrosaurus* "breeding young" (Botha-Brink et al. 2016 [memory]).
- **Register:** Q-028, Q-031, Q-038, Q-012, Q-001; H3 merges here.

**Smallest check that could change the disposition.** A nearest-prior brief
on life-history against size selectivity across event types, plus a check of
whether *size-independent* proxies for pace of life exist in compiled data
for at least two events with contrasting routes. Candidates are bivalve
sclerochronology compilations (Moss et al. [memory]) and tetrapod histology.

**What would retire or narrow it:**
- no size-independent proxy exists for two or more contrasting events: it
  then narrows to the Quaternary, modern data and theory;
- reversals by event type are already established.

## Card 3. Does recovery on substitute builders rebuild the old opportunity structure or create a new one?

This card develops Codex's seed 2, with substitution as the informative case.

**Claim.** After a crisis removes ecosystem builders, recovery on
*substitutes* restores some services and not others. Which dependant guilds
return reveals which part of the prerequisite they needed, and which fail to
return reveals it too. Functional recovery can therefore rebuild the former
opportunity structure, or create a different one.

**Why the answer matters.** It separates two models of recovery:
- **opportunity as vacancy:** time to diversify into empty roles;
- **opportunity as prerequisites:** builders must be rebuilt first.

That reframes recovery lags (Q-021) and the biological construction of
diversity capacity (Q-010, B-010, B-001, B-004). It asks what a biosphere
must rebuild before it can diversify.

**Strongest rival:**
- **environmental sorting:** one environmental recovery drives builders and
  dependants independently, so substitution explains nothing;
- **taphonomy:** substitute builders such as microbialites preserve
  dependants differently. Foster et al. 2019 on transported fossils in
  microbialites is a contrary lead, checked by Codex's scout.

**Discriminating result.** Compare settings with overlapping physical
conditions but different builders, original or substitute:
- dependant guilds with *independently specified* requirements should
  return as the builders' services predict, not as the environment does;
- guilds needing a service the substitute lacks should stay absent where the
  environment has recovered, until original-type builders return.

**Nearest known prior:**
- Marenco et al. 2012 [read by Codex];
- Early Triassic metazoan–microbial reefs (Brayard et al. 2011 [memory]);
- the PaleoReefs (PARED) database of Phanerozoic reefs and their builders
  (Kiessling and colleagues [memory]).
- **Register:** B-010, Q-010, Q-021, B-001, B-004.

**Smallest check that could change the disposition.** A nearest-prior brief,
plus a support check of PARED. Does it record builder identity *and* the
composition of associated dependant guilds, reef by reef, across at least two
substitution episodes? Candidates are the Early Triassic, the post-Devonian
microbial interval and the Early Jurassic.

**What would retire it:**
- dependant-guild composition is not recorded with builder identity across
  two or more episodes; or
- the taphonomic rival cannot be separated, because preservation differs
  systematically by builder type.
