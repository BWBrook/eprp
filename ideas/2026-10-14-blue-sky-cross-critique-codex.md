## Three changes needed before the merge

### 1. C1: practical discrimination is valuable; failed power is not impossibility

The proposed power/false-positive table is useful. A procedure with power below
0.8 under a chosen effect and recording model has failed that operating target.
It has not shown that all procedures, additional observables or other parameter
regions fail. Reserve an impossibility claim for observational equivalence or
a bound on the best achievable discrimination. Otherwise state **practical
discrimination under the tested models, data support and procedures**.

The worked geography/climate versus diversity-dependence comparison is the
strongest starting point. Comparing two existing simulators wholesale can
distinguish spatial resolution, dispersal or other implementation choices
instead of the biological mechanism. Hold forcing and nuisance assumptions
comparable, toggle the focal mechanism within a shared implementation where
possible, and repeat the conclusion across plausible implementations. Include
positive controls and parameter regimes not used to choose the discriminator.
Do not calibrate an observation model so flexibly to the observed biological
pattern that it absorbs the very effect being tested.

This concern has a practical precedent: Cooper et al.'s
[DeepDive paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-48434-7) discusses simulator
and training dependence and improvements after retraining on difficult cases
(relevant full Results/Discussion checked). The limit is conditional, not a
universal verdict on a verbal hypothesis.

**Disposition:** develop one explicit biological contrast; keep the general
atlas as an enabling programme component. I would not make a multi-hypothesis
benchmark the blue-sky flagship. This is a substantive priority disagreement,
not a recommendation to discard observation-aware simulation. EPRP's target
remains consequential biological questions, with audits supporting them.

### 2. C3: architecture can reproduce a pattern without identifying its causal share

An architecture-only model that reproduces boundary clustering establishes
conditional sufficiency. It does not estimate what fraction of actual turnover
was manufactured by architecture. Biological rates and observation interact;
sea-level change can affect both habitat and preservation. An unexplained excess
could also reflect omitted dating or sampling processes, not necessarily an
extinction pulse. Peters's
[2005 primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0502616102) explicitly considers
preservation and common-cause explanations (PDF Introduction/Discussion checked).

The first output should be an **architecture-null predictive envelope** for a
specified clustering statistic. Any attributable contribution then needs a
counterfactual definition, biological alternatives and joint uncertainty, rather
than a ratio of simulated to observed clustering. Do not require that ratio to
lie between zero and one by construction and call it causal attribution.

Also separate rock architecture, age assignment and binning. Macrostrat can use
named intervals and interpolated contacts in its
[age models](https://dev.macrostrat.org/docs/columns/age-model); a matching PBDB
boundary pattern can partly reflect shared chronological conventions. Sequence
surfaces and chronostratigraphic stage boundaries are not interchangeable.
Seek independently dated sections and include chronological assignment in the
generative comparison.

[StratPal](https://doi.org/10.1111/2041-210X.14507) supplies filtering machinery,
not calibrated global architecture; its examples require supplied age–depth
histories and preservation assumptions (§3.1 and relevant full text checked).
The [Macrostrat infrastructure paper](https://doi.org/10.1002/gdj3.189) describes
uneven coverage and relatively limited high-resolution sections (§§2.1.1–2.1.2
checked; current global coverage not inventoried here).

**Disposition:** hold the global “share” claim; develop a bounded null-envelope
design only after a section/age-support audit. Importance remains high enough to
retain it, but demonstrated tractability is below the list's M–H until this
support is shown. This is not just a wording change: it changes the estimand.

### 3. C5 and C10 need biological measurements that match their hypotheses

**C5:** first distinguish guild presence, guild richness, relative taxonomic
dominance, local trophic completeness and recovery of ecological processes.
They need not recover in the same order. Song et al.'s
[2018 primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aat5091) measures global
taxonomic/ecological composition and explicitly distinguishes their recovery
(indexed abstract and Results passages checked; direct PMC/publisher access
failed during this pass). Before treating it and Chen–Benton as an experiment
between opposite assembly rules, crosswalk their endpoints, spatial scales and
baseline definitions. An observed predator does not show that lower trophic
levels had yet to exist; relative taxonomic dominance does not measure energy
flow. The proposed tiering/motility/feeding coding is not itself a trophic network.

For a cross-event test, condition on the surviving functional pool, pre-event
occupancy, continuing environmental stress and evolutionary availability of
the guilds. A repeated rank order could arise from shared tolerances or initial
abundances; a failure to reject a sampling null is not positive evidence for
contingency. Use explicit rival assembly processes and quantify a consequential
degree of repeatability. A precise weak concordance is informative; low power
is unresolved. **Develop this target contract:** it is one of the strongest
biological questions in Claude's list, and should remain linked to Q-007/Q-009.

**C10:** drilling frequency and repair frequency are outcomes of predator–prey
interactions, not independent measures of predator capability. Prey resistance,
encounter rate, attack success, survival and preservation can change them in
opposite directions. Lagged correlation between these outcomes and prey traits
is therefore not automatically a test of predator-driven escalation.

Klompmaker et al.'s
[2017 primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aam7468) calibrates drill-hole
size against predator size and already examines prey size, mobility and
infaunalisation (full main text checked). Its existing prey-side analysis makes
the residual narrower than “add prey traits.” A promising contrast would pair
independent predator performance proxies with mechanically relevant defences
in the same prey/habitat systems, comparing escalation against shared energetic
change and taxonomic replacement. **Develop a finite evidence/trait contract**
before any large compilation. Low present data readiness should not automatically
give this grand biological question lower next-work value than an atlas.

Additional qualifications behind that table:

- **C4:** a Lazarus taxon's later return supports lineage survival somewhere;
  it does not locate an unobserved refuge. Local survival versus migration and
  recolonisation needs to be part of the model. Conditioning only on survivors
  also loses the exposure denominator required to call an environment protective.
- **C6:** a fraction of significant clade-pair tests is not a calibrated
  prevalence estimate. Pair selection, dependence among pairs and heterogeneous
  power affect the denominator as well as false positives. Rate dependence
  remains a statistical signature, not direct evidence of competition.
- **C7:** estimate the null's rate distribution independently of the selected
  exceptional lineages, or test held-out predictions. A lineage falling within
  a flexible heterogeneous null need not lack a biological explanation for
  its low rate. Q-036 is a closer overlap than “loosely Q-012” alone.
- **C8:** Arrhenius responses averaged across different local temperatures do
  not generally equal the response at global mean temperature. Generation time,
  body size and ecological exposure also affect the rate mapping. A posterior
  overlapping 0.65 eV or zero is not an outcome-neutral test of a consequential
  effect. The PhanDA trajectory/dependence limits already documented in EPRP
  must remain explicit.
- **C9:** selecting a single maximum from noisy, smoothed or incompletely
  sampled clades can create a lag. Disparity metrics measuring range and average
  dissimilarity can validly disagree. Specify what biological process the chosen
  lag tests; a cross-clade regression on extinction exposure alone does not
  identify extinction's effect.
- **C12:** cooling can change oxygen availability and food-web structure.
  “Which driver?” needs total versus direct effects and time ordering before
  identifiability can be assessed. Uncertainty in co-registered proxy histories
  cannot be repaired by more simulation replicates.
