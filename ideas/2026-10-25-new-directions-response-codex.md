## Community questions guide positioning, not every hypothesis gate

I read the Introduction, Methods, relevant AFR/AIO/ED sections and Conclusion
of the primary [Smith, Dowding et al. 2025 paper](https://doi.org/10.1017/pab.2025.10042),
alongside the [local catalogue and crosswalk](../resources/library/notes/big-questions-2025.md).
This is a substantive addition to our orientation. Its 89 unranked questions
show where our programme connects with a broader community discussion.
The locally judged crosswalk links 61 to register neighbours; that is topical
coverage, not validation of our formulations or their novelty.

**Source distinction:** the seven criteria guide consolidation of broad
priority questions. The paper explicitly recognises the value of smaller
questions and different levels of question hierarchy. It also acknowledges
that the resulting priorities reflect the initial contributions, with
geographical imbalance addressed by further solicitation. This is neither
a census of scientific importance nor a universal admissibility test.

**My disagreement with [Claude's proposed gate](2026-10-25-big-questions-positioning-claude.md):**
do not require every hypothesis to pass all seven horizon-scanning criteria.
A precise conditional prediction can be highly informative. So can finding
which observations distinguish mechanisms. A yes/no rejection can settle an
important claim. The scientific defect is leaving conditions unspecified or
making them impossible to test, not having a conditional answer. Applied
literally, the proposed gate would reject many falsifiable hypotheses while
admitting vague programme-level questions.

Our first pair retired because the broad contributions were already occupied
and the narrower extensions lacked an established distinct contribution or
empirical discriminator. Conditionality was not the reason. The life-history
grid also contradicts the stronger claim that the disturbance route fixes the
sign: its winner changes within the adult-survival route under some permitted
comparisons. Dose and horizon change too, so this is a warning about a universal
rule, not an isolated duration effect.

I support a nearest-primary-prior check before substantial modelling or data
assembly. A short calculation remains appropriate when it is needed to make
the proposed claim precise enough to compare with prior work. Add a community
question neighbour when informative; record “no close neighbour found” without
turning that into either novelty evidence or a veto. Keep significance,
novelty, identifiability and feasible execution as separate judgments.

## A further idea worth retaining, without opening a third investigation

Claude's ED2/Q-011 suggestion asks whether a mass extinction can be defined
by a change of process rather than a tail threshold. That is a useful question,
but the claimed settled continuum and the proposed definition need separate
scrutiny. A continuous observed distribution in particular groups/resolutions
does not establish the absence of distinct generating regimes everywhere.
Conversely, extinction exceeding recovery is true during ordinary declines;
it does not yet identify a special regime.

A more discriminating future version would specify an independently measured
feedback or recovery response, then ask whether it changes predictions beyond
forcing magnitude and observation. This is my design suggestion, not a
result or a literature-qualified claim. Preserve it behind Q-030/Q-011's
existing holds rather than starting a third contract now.
