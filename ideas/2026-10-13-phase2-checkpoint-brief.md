## 1. What phase 2 did and learned (11–13 October)

- **Coverage.** About 24 commits, an external review and 11 replies a side.
  - 13 of 50 register entries were substantially revised.
  - Deep work went into Q-006, Q-021, Q-027, Q-038, Q-042, Q-044 and
    Q-050.
- **Small data contracts paid off.** Each found a decisive limit cheaply:
  - Q-038: the deposited extinction flags are offset by one interval;
  - Q-027: support outside Europe and North America is thin;
  - Q-044: recorded sharing between regions is near zero, though not
    biological saturation;
  - Q-042: the published genus figures measure different targets;
  - Q-021: Li archives cannot yet date the recovery;
  - PBDB counts are clustered by publication.
- **Corrections went both ways.** The external review endorsed this style.
- **Depth-first argument showed diminishing returns.** Q-006 and Q-021 took
  five or six exchanges each to reach conclusions that a target-matched
  check of prior work, or a route review, would have reached sooner.
- **Recurring design lesson.** Where we looked closely, the binding problem
  was usually the *observation model*: sampling and clustering, chronology,
  archive mapping and selection. Access was rarely the binding problem, and
  you resolved several access problems. This is a lesson from selected
  cases, not a portfolio-wide estimate.

## 3. Strongest candidates

These are the strongest candidates for eventual elevation; both passes
placed Q-025, Q-030 and Q-038 in their top five.

| Candidate | Why it matters | Decisive first test | Main way it could fail |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Q-030** Is the biosphere becoming less sensitive to perturbation? | A widely stated, disputed claim never tested with a time term | Count the post-Jurassic events with forcing estimated independently of extinction, on a common exposure scale | Too few comparable events, or exposure measures that are themselves responses |
| **Q-038** How predictable is which genera die, and does that change in mass extinctions? | Turns "bad genes or bad luck" into a testable, held-out prediction; the data are in hand | A prospective predictor contract for Rhynchonellata and Bivalvia, including the observation issues from Q-006 | Valid pre-event predictors cannot be recovered, or the result repeats existing CMR studies |
| **Q-025** How do local losses scale to global extinction, then and now? | Links modern local loss to global extinction risk across scales | One taxon's nested-grain baseline, then a stationary-range sampling null | Apparent local loss is mostly observation or range shift |
| **Q-010** Can biological innovation change how much diversity an environment supports? | A major mechanism beyond the known association between ecosystem engineers and diversity | A contrast table for one innovation: its closest prior, and whether comparable basins have before-and-after support | Capacity is recoverable only from the curve it is meant to explain |
| **Q-012** Is extinction susceptibility inherited along lineages? | Separates heritable risk from apparent ageing, a live dispute | A target-matched prior check, then counts of usable sister and descendant contrasts | Already occupied by 2023–2026 work, or not enough relatives |

**Next behind these** are Q-004 (the background rate after pseudoextinction
correction) and Q-016 (range-based risk criteria over geological time,
open microfossil data).

**A coherent theme (Claude's view):** Q-004, Q-013, Q-017 and Q-034 together
ask how modern extinction rates should be compared with deep-time
baselines. That is the programme's central past–present question, and could
frame a phase-3 package.
