## 1. What phase 2 did and learned (11–13 October)

- **Coverage.** About 24 commits, an external review and 11 replies a side.
  13 register bodies changed and 37 did not. Deep work went into Q-006,
  Q-021, Q-027, Q-038, Q-042, Q-044 and Q-050.
- **Small data and source contracts found decisive limits cheaply:**
  - Q-038: the deposited design displaces the extinction flags by one
    interval under RMark 3.0.0. What that means for the published
    coefficients is unresolved.
  - Q-027: support outside Europe and North America is thin.
  - Q-044: recorded sharing between regions is near zero, though not
    biological saturation.
  - Q-042: the published genus-loss figures measure different targets.
  - Q-021: the inspected Li routes do not yet discriminate the proposed
    recovery mechanisms under supported chronology and archive mappings.
    Descriptive changes through recovery are supported.
  - PBDB repeat occurrences mostly reuse references. That is a publication
    structure an observation model must address, not by itself a test of
    the sampling model.
- **Corrections went both ways.** The external review endorsed this style.
- **The lesson for the work queue.** Depth-first argument tended to produce
  ever narrower follow-ups. Earlier target-matched checks of prior work, and
  of the route to data, may reduce that. The audits themselves also found
  things not known at the start.
- **The recurring design lesson.** In the cases inspected, problems with the
  observation model persisted after access was resolved: sampling and
  clustering, chronology, archive mapping and selection. You resolved
  several access problems. This is a lesson from selected cases, not a
  portfolio-wide measurement.

## 3. Candidate first checks

Q-025, Q-030 and Q-038 appear in both agents' first priorities (Claude's
first next-work steps and Codex's five checks).

| Candidate | Why it matters | Decisive first check | Main way it could fail |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Q-030** Is the biosphere becoming less sensitive to perturbation? | A widely stated, disputed claim. The bounded prior search did not find the proposed forcing-conditioned temporal comparison; novelty is unverified | A catalogue of perturbations chosen without reference to extinction, and a count of comparable post-Jurassic events on a common exposure scale | Too few comparable events, or exposure measures that are themselves responses |
| **Q-038** How predictable is which genera die, and does that change in mass extinctions? | Turns "bad genes or bad luck" into held-out prediction. The capture histories are in hand | A prospective predictor contract for Rhynchonellata and Bivalvia, carrying the observation requirements from Q-006 | Valid pre-event predictors or outcomes cannot be recovered, or the result repeats existing CMR prediction studies |
| **Q-025** How do local losses scale to global extinction, then and now? | Links local loss to global extinction across scales | One taxon's nested-grain baseline, then a stationary-range sampling null | Apparent local loss is mostly observation or range shift |
| **Q-010** Can biological innovation change how much diversity an environment supports? | A major mechanism beyond the known association between ecosystem engineers and diversity | A prior check and contrast table for one innovation: closest prior, and whether comparable basins have before-and-after support | Capacity is recoverable only from the curve it is meant to explain |
| **Q-012** Is extinction susceptibility inherited along lineages? | Separates heritable risk from apparent ageing, a live dispute | A target-matched prior check, then counts of usable sister and descendant contrasts | Already occupied by 2023–2026 work, or not enough relatives |

**Retained later options:** Q-004 (the background rate after pseudoextinction
