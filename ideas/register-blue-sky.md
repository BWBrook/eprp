## Format

1. **Shared input.** The
   [capability inventory](../resources/capability-inventory.md): factual,
   with no questions. Either agent may amend it.
2. **Independent ideation.**
   - Each agent writes 10–15 questions in its own dated file
     (`ideas/YYYY-MM-DD-blue-sky-ideation-{claude,codex}.md`).
   - Each commits its file, frozen, before reading the other's.
   - Quality over count; no quota.
3. **Admission criteria** for every question. Blue-sky, but answerable now:
   - **Pure deep time.** Modern applicability is set aside. The span is the
     whole Phanerozoic, or a constrained period where the question needs it.
   - **A target-matched prior check, recorded.** It names the closest work
     and the residual. "Not found" means not found in a bounded search.
   - **The observation model is named:** how the fossil record generates the
     data (sampling, chronology, taxonomy, archive).
   - **A route:** data and/or simulation from the inventory, or a named new
     resource, with its access status.
   - **A test that is informative either way**, against a consequential
     effect or contrast stated in advance.
4. **One cross-critique exchange,** then a merge into this register with
   IDs B-001 onwards. Each entry gets a disposition (develop / hold / park)
   on the checkpoint's three axes: importance, demonstrated tractability,
   and value of the next work.
5. **Guardrails** carried over from phase 2:
   - two substantive exchanges per question by default;
   - each tranche ends in a recorded disposition;
   - no forced status changes;
   - expansion of the applied register stays paused.

The approved sweep of prior checks on the applied register continues in
parallel, with at most two tasks active.

### B-001 Do ecosystems reassemble in a repeatable order after crises?
Owner Claude. **Develop.** Source: Claude C5.
- **Question.** After major Phanerozoic crises, do functional groups
  re-emerge in a shared order, beyond what recovery of richness implies?
- **Residual.** This bounded search found no sampling-aware test of a
  shared order across three or more events. The leading end-Permian
  accounts (Chen & Benton 2012, 10.1038/ngeo1475; Song et al. 2018,
  10.1126/sciadv.aat5091) measure different endpoints, so they are not a
  test between assembly rules. The question is whether, for a defined
  endpoint, a shared order recurs beyond survivor pools, sampling, clade
  contingency and shared tolerances. The earlier "conflicting accounts"
  wording is kept in the frozen source list.
- **Corrections (Codex).**
  - Distinguish guild presence, guild richness, relative dominance, local
    trophic completeness and recovery of process; they need not recover in
    the same order.
  - Ecospace coding is not a trophic network.
  - Condition on the surviving functional pool, pre-event occupancy,
    continuing stress and evolutionary availability.
  - Use explicit rival assembly processes.
  - Failing to reject a sampling null is not evidence of contingency.
- **First product.** A target contract:
  - a crosswalk of the endpoints, scales and baselines of Chen–Benton and
    Song et al.;
  - definitions of the survivor pool;
  - assembly rivals;
  - a consequential degree of repeatability fixed in advance.
- **Current contract (14 October, amended 15 October after Codex's merge
  review; [contract](../literature/2026-10-14-b001-reassembly-contract.md)).**
  - Chen & Benton assert trophic rebuilding narratively; Song et al.
    measure subsampled richness by motility class and *infer* top-down
    order. Nicholls et al. 2026 also treat them as different metrics.
  - **Primary endpoint:** partial richness restoration time, in Myr to a
    sustained return to 50% of baseline subsampled richness.
    - Left, right (10 Myr) and interval censoring are explicit.
    - The at-risk set is judged from pre-event data only.
    - Scope: the end-Permian, end-Triassic and K–Pg events. Inference
      concerns these events, not crises in general.
  - **Rivals:**
    - R0, sampling and survivor-pool null;
    - R1, clade contingency;
    - R2, bottom-up rule;
    - R3, top-down rule;
    - R4, shared tolerance.
  - **Target:** repeatability ρ, the share of residual variance in log
    delay due to guild identity, in an interval-censored model with event,
    covariate (survivors, baseline, detection) and dominant-clade terms.
    - Negligible ρ₀ = 0.1; rule strength ρ₁ = 0.5; 90% interval; a 2 × 2
      outcome table.
    - Kendall's W is descriptive only (its chance mean is 1/m).
    - A signed contrast δ carries direction (R2 against R3).
  - **Identification:**
    - θ and clade are separated only by guilds refilled by different
      clades;
    - detection constant within a guild is confounded with θ, so it needs
      an independent estimate.
  - **Coding.** In PBDB, 96–99% of populated ecospace values are inherited
    from higher taxa ([provenance](../data/b001-support/README.md)). That
    is dependence, not "a guild is a clade".
  - **Support audit (15 October; [record](../data/b001-support/README.md#b-001-support-audit-knope-coding-joined-to-pbdb-15-october-2026)).**
    Definitions were committed before screening.
    - 4, 5 and 8 guilds are at risk; 4 are at risk in all three events.
    - Only 25–48% of window genera join Knope's coding, and the gaps are
      outcome-related after the K–Pg.
    - A best-case bound (perfect, uncensored data) gives power 0.29 and
      0.27 against the required 0.8. About 17 guilds in all three events
      would be needed.
    - **The global guild-grain empirical route is parked; the question is
      kept.** Proposed status: hold, pending Codex.
    - Reopening needs a coding source supporting about 17 or more guilds,
      or a separately contracted estimand needing less replication across
      guilds.
- **Overlap.** Q-007 and Q-009 (linked). It also shares trait resources with
  B-010 and B-011, as different tests.

### B-002 Can juvenile viability block otherwise useful adult innovations (ammonoids)?
Owner Codex. **Develop.** Source: Codex D2.
- **Contract:** [literature/2026-10-14-ammonoid-developmental-route-contract.md](../literature/2026-10-14-ammonoid-developmental-route-contract.md).
  - Compare adult-only with all-stage physical demands using the *same*
    shell generator, then vary developmental coupling separately.
  - Conditional costs and frequencies of accessibility come before any
    claim of strict disconnection.
  - A finite-grid non-edge is not a proof.
  - Thresholds need independently justified physical and ecological
    ranges.
- **Priors (Claude's additions, accepted and extended by Codex).**
  - Ritterbush & Bottjer 2012 (10.1666/10027.1) include ontogenetic
    variation.
  - The Raup coiling tradition.
  - Urdy et al. 2010 (10.1002/jez.b.21337): complete growth histories.
  - Gerber 2017 (10.1111/brv.12276): Raup-space geometry.
  - The specific residual remains unverified.
- **Support gate.** Access is resolved; transition support is not.
  - Barry supplied the Walton–Korn workbook (Dryad 10.5061/dryad.c17p8,
    file 34579; CC0). Codex verified its size, MD5 and SHA-256 against the
    provider and audited its content ([support README](../data/ammonoid-growth-support/README.md)).
  - What it holds:
    - 179 series rows with 177 exact taxon labels;
    - 2,643 row and whorl positions with all four conch variables;
    - source conflicts preserved: duplicated Macrocephalites labels, 12
      conflicting superfamily cells, a Famennian/Carboniferous
      inconsistency and one zero.
  - It encodes **no** evolutionary relations, specimen IDs or maturity
    labels. The number of eligible historical transitions remains
    **unknown**.
  - It is a growth-data route, not a ready transition dataset. Having the
    file does not establish transition support or physical-model readiness.
  - Next: a small crosswalk of specimens, primary sources and phylogeny.
- **Overlap.** Q-036 and Q-050 (development-adjacent, distinct targets);
  B-013 (shares morphology resources).

### B-003 Did prey defences track independent predator capability through the Phanerozoic?
Owner Claude. **Develop.** Source: Claude C10.
- **Corrections (Codex).**
  - Drilling and repair frequencies are outcomes of predator–prey
    interactions, not predator capability.
  - Klompmaker et al. 2017 (10.1126/science.aam7468) already examine prey
    size, mobility and infaunalisation, which narrows the residual.
- **First product.** A finite evidence and trait contract:
  - an independent proxy for predator performance (for example, drill-hole
    size as predator size);
  - mechanically relevant prey defences in matched prey and habitat
    systems;
  - rivals: shared energetic change and taxonomic replacement.
  No large compilation before the contract.
- **Overlap.** Q-048 and Q-037.

### B-004 Did burrowers change the conditions under which deep-water benthos could persist?
Owner Codex. **Develop (theory first).** Source: Codex D3.
- **Question.** Is there a threshold asymmetry between establishment and
  persistence of benthos under low oxygen, created by sediment ventilation?
- **Corrections (Claude, accepted).**
  - A contrary global prior: van de Velde et al. 2018
    (10.1038/s41467-018-04973-4) argue early burrowing caused ocean anoxia.
    The contract must state the scale (local or global).
  - Proxy circularity: engineers alter redox proxies.
  - Palaeozoic "deep sea" is mostly slope and outer shelf.
- **Staging.**
  1. A theoretical test, not a required positive: do independently
     defended parameter ranges in a sediment-diagenesis model with
     irrigation and colonisation permit a consequential asymmetry between
     establishment and persistence? A sufficiently strong negative is an
     acceptable product and would close the empirical route early.
  2. Then a section-support audit.
- **Overlap.** Q-010 (shared contract on engineering versus external
  opportunity); B-006 (shared threshold template); B-009 (oxygen
  assumptions).

### B-005 Do geography-and-climate-only and diversity-dependent spatial models predict different sampled diversity structure?
Owner Claude. **Hold, queued for development.** Source: Claude C1, narrowed.
- **Contrast.** Toggle diversity dependence within a *shared*
  implementation, holding forcing and nuisance assumptions comparable.
  Repeat across implementations, with positive controls. Compare predicted
  *sampled* spatial structure (regional richness, beta, gradient shape).
  - Balembois et al. 2025 (10.1038/s41467-025-63428-9) and Cermeño et al.
    2022 (10.1038/s41586-022-04932-6) are the starting models.
- **Corrections (Codex).**
  - The claim is practical discrimination under the tested models and
    procedures, not impossibility.
  - Impossibility needs observational equivalence or a bound on the best
    achievable discrimination.
  - The observation model must not absorb the effect.
- **The general atlas** of discriminability across hypotheses is kept as an
  enabling method component, not a register target.
- **Overlap.** Q-040, Q-010, Q-027, and parked Q-019.

### B-007 Does stratigraphic architecture alone reproduce boundary clustering of first and last appearances?
Owner Claude. **Hold.** Source: Claude C3, estimand corrected.
- **Corrected estimand (Codex).** A **predictive envelope under an

  - Reproducing the clustering shows conditional sufficiency, not a causal
    share.
  - Attribution needs a counterfactual definition and biological

  calibration.

a later, small, independent expansion:

  silica cycle; Harper & Knoll 1975);
- insects;
- plants other than angiosperms.
