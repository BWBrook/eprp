## B-001: retain the biological target, repair the inference contract

The endpoint crosswalk is a useful advance. It distinguishes genus-richness
recovery from dominance, trophic completeness and process, and avoids treating
motility classes as directly observed trophic levels. The following issues
matter before even a small empirical analysis.

### 1. High priority: define the repeatability effect before its negative threshold

For m independent, exchangeable, complete untied rankings of the same n guilds,
the ordinary finite-sample Kendall statistic has

`E(W) = 1/m`.

For three events this is one-third, not zero. The corresponding untied identity
is

`W = [1 + (m - 1) mean_pairwise_Spearman] / m`.

At m = 3, W = 0.2 corresponds to mean pairwise Spearman = −0.2. A raw-W interval
below 0.2 therefore is not a general criterion for negligible positive shared
order. The survivor/detection null can have a different, potentially higher
baseline, which makes an explicit effect definition even more necessary.

The proposed positive rule also requires exceeding the simulated null's 95th
percentile; that extra condition is useful and is not being overlooked here.
It does not fix the separate negative-effect interpretation. For illustration,
an exact enumeration of three independent rankings of six guilds gives mean
W = 1/3 and a 95th percentile about 0.6444; W ≥ 0.5 alone is not a 5% test.
This is a mathematical diagnostic, not fossil-data power calibration.

**Repair:** distinguish the observed statistic, its observation/survivor null,
the biological repeatability parameter and the uncertainty interval being
reported. Choose a justified excess-concordance or model-based target, and
calibrate both consequential and negligible effects on that target. The
illustrative adjustment `(W - E_R0[W]) / (1 - E_R0[W])` is not automatically
an identified biological parameter, especially with ties or near-unit null
concordance. It should not be adopted just to retain the existing numbers.
Specify whether inference concerns these three events or a population of
other crises. Guild resampling alone does not quantify uncertainty about the
latter.

The agent independently derived the identity and enumerated the six-guild
case. Parent verification used exact rational arithmetic over all 36 relative
rank combinations for three guilds/three events, confirming mean W = 1/3 and
the −0.2 pairwise-correlation implication. No primary dataset was used.

### 2. High priority: the threshold-crossing endpoint creates ties, censoring and selection

The first bin regaining 50% of pre-event richness needs explicit rules for:

- guilds already above 50% immediately after the event: no observed recovery
  delay, or a left-censored time, rather than an arbitrary later crossing;
- guilds never reaching the threshold within a fixed follow-up: right-censored,
  not deleted or assigned an exact common last rank;
- crossings within broad bins: interval-censored, with uncertain ordering;
- zero/poorly supported pre-event baselines and guilds absent before an event:
  the fractional-baseline target is undefined or unstable;
- noisy temporary crossings: define the first crossing versus a sustained
  return, and propagate uncertainty in baseline and subsampled richness.

Use a common, predeclared guild/event support, or a justified partial-ranking
model. Restricting the analysis after seeing which guilds recover or are sampled
in every event selects on the outcome. Different stage durations and coarse
bins can create apparent common ranks or differences through ties alone.
Simulate these recording and selection rules in the null and positive controls.

The primary quantity is partial richness restoration, not necessarily guild
re-establishment: a guild retaining 80% of baseline throughout never lost its
presence. Name the endpoint accordingly. Region/realm and taxonomic support
must remain consistent, since global loss and local recolonisation differ.

### 3. High priority: concordance does not identify the assembly mechanism

High W is invariant to a shared reversal of all event rankings. It therefore
cannot alone distinguish a bottom-up from a top-down direction. Nor can high
concordance plus different dominant clades exclude shared tolerance or another
unmodelled cause. Specify signed functional-order predictions, or compare rival
generative models on common observables with equal nuisance information.

Tiering × motility × feeding categories do not automatically determine trophic
level or energy flow. Different clades filling the same guild is useful support
for separating clade identity from functional coding, but it is not sufficient
causal identification. Conditioning on the environmental trajectory must have
a stated scale, uncertainty and target; an environmental change caused by the
community itself is not simply an external nuisance variable.

**Repair:** report residual cross-event regularity conditional on the tested
nulls unless specific models and empirical support distinguish mechanisms.
A precise absence of that regularity constrains the proposed shared-order
claim; it does not prove ecological contingency in general.

### 4. Medium priority: inherited coding does not make guild and clade synonymous

Higher-taxon inheritance of ecological annotations is a serious dependence and
validation issue. But “about 99% inherited” does not imply “a guild largely
equals a clade”: many clades can inherit the same guild, while sufficiently
broad clades can contain several guilds. What matters is the actual joint
guild × clade distribution and annotation provenance at a declared rank.

The 99%/1–1.5% figures need the exact PBDB request, retrieval snapshot, fields,
denominator, treatment of missing values and calculation. None is supplied in
the contract or linked source record. Until recoverable, label the percentages
as an unverified agent query and retain the qualitative dependence warning.
Do not use them as an established fraction for the eventual recovery extract.
Likewise, “no open occurrence-level matrix spans three recoveries” should be
bounded-search wording, not a universal data-absence claim.

### 5. Medium priority: six guilds and three events are planning choices, not information limits

The support gate is sensible in purpose, but “fewer than about six … is
uninformative” is not established. The informative sample depends on rank
uncertainty, ties, effect size, shared clade structure and recording windows.
Define “adequate support” before cell counting: coverage/count rule, baseline,
follow-up horizon, censoring and trait confidence. Then use the actual support
in a finite discrimination check against consequential alternatives. A smaller
well-resolved contrast may be useful; a larger tied/confounded table may not be.
Retain six/three as a provisional planning target if useful, with no claim that
it proves power or a general inability below it.
