## B-005: accept the bound and close this exercise

Let P0 and P1 be the frozen multinomial laws and a=0.8873956068630543 their
single-incidence affinity. The rule rejecting when P1/P0>1, equivalently
log(P1/P0)>0, has

    size + false-negative probability = sum min(P0,P1) <= a^N.

Both errors are nonnegative, so each is bounded by a^N. At N=26 the rule
therefore has size <=0.0447764 and power >=0.9552236. At N=100 its power is
at least 0.9999935177. Since it already satisfies size <=0.05, the optimal
size-0.05 test can do at least as well. Together with the existing necessary
lower bound, the minimum feasible budget lies between **4 and 26 independent
cell-incidences**. These are draws with replacement, not distinct cells or
fossil collections. Twenty-six is sufficient, not the exact minimum.

The prior ceiling of one was correctly labelled inconclusive, but I had left
a question unresolved that this constructive bound settles without simulation.
The [certificate and independent proof](../data/b005-design/affinity-review.md)
now supply the missing result. A separate rational enumeration checked the
error-sum identity in all 16 existing synthetic cases. Exact achieved power
is still not calculated; its lower bound is enough for the decision. This
is an operating-characteristic guarantee under the model law, not a guarantee
that every realised sample gives the right answer.

The scientific closure follows from the limited question answered. The
whole-capacity intervention changes the reference map's spatial profile, and
the idealised observer can detect it. The generic comparison is occupied;
unique-genus counting, collection clustering and time/coordinate uncertainty
are not represented. More simulation of this observer would not supply them.
Retain the implementation controls, Hellinger calculation and observation
counterexamples as support, without a biological development allocation.

Three qualifications to Claude's wider interpretation remain:

- Richness-only predictions require additional assumptions for a realistic
  genus-incidence observer; this does not prove that every scientifically
  justified observation model needs a particular identity-bearing simulator.
  No supported small route from the present observer has been established.
- The weighted local-richness ratio is about 4.8, **not a global gamma-richness
  ratio**. Refitting environmental parameters can change normalised shape;
  no such refit was performed. The arbitrary regional time-varying-rate
  counterexample establishes nonidentification for that flexible family, not
  every restricted environmental model.
- We verified the native capacity intervention; we did not show that the
  earlier published exponential comparison accidentally retained finite caps.
  The References brief explicitly preserves this distinction. There is no
  newly established published-code defect to add to the infrastructure paper.

## Q-027: the author's climate definition changes the decision

The held code defines broad `hot_or_cold` classes by named stage intervals.
Its plotting caller uses these classes for the warm/cold slope comparison,
consistent with the accepted manuscript's description of a pooled Phanerozoic
contrast. These are neither numeric SST thresholds nor a binary statement
about ice sheets. The author explicitly places **Albian in Hot and Campanian
in Cold**. Calling both greenhouse intervals does not remove that declared
contrast. A strict hothouse/icehouse variable exists separately and is not the
active variable in that comparison.

The literal code has a consequential discrepancy: a case labelled
"Thanetian to Bartonian (Hot)" selects only Lutetian and Bartonian.
Thanetian and Ypresian are NA in the literal broad classification. We preserved
those NAs and added an explicitly label-implied sensitivity. Held output
tables do not resolve which definitions produced the final published results;
this is a code-level discrepancy, not demonstrated error in those results.
It does not affect the three Cretaceous pairs below.

## Why a failed presence gate would not establish untestability

The existing screen uses strict age containment, collection-age
palaeocoordinates, an approximate 555-km grid and occurrence-row region
assignment. The estimator uses majority-overlap age assignment, deposited
stage-to-map reconstructed coordinates, dggridR cells, modal collection-region
assignment and incidence-quality filters. These are not nested designs.
Majority overlap can add records and regional support; zero-duration ranges
provide a reverse exception. Coarser cells and coordinate changes can also
alter eligibility. The earlier audit already demonstrated support increasing
when the age rule changed.

Therefore "estimator eligibility can only remove support" is not valid for
this comparison. Zero passing extreme-state pairs means zero under the stated
screen, not proof that the retained record can establish neither a dip nor
its absence. Within-region matching addresses the chosen region–band
confounding; it is not the only conceivable design. Our criterion is a
declared conservative design requirement, not a general identification theorem.

## Q-012 is not an automatic fallback

The current queued working likelihood omits the hidden lineages and ancestry
changes induced by recording. Improving its sampler does not insert the
missing marginalisation. The earlier complete-data oracle success and weak
recorded-data detector therefore do not establish that this sampler is the
only route, or that it is the next best allocation. Any Q-012 continuation
first needs the correct recorded-data target and a bounded recovery strategy;
it does not follow merely from closing the present B-005 exercise.
