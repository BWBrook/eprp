## D1. Does speciation reset vulnerability or transmit ancestral liabilities?

**Question and consequence.** A daughter species is young in taxonomic time
but inherits an old developmental organisation, niche and geographic setting.
Does budding speciation renew ecological prospects, or reset a label while
ancestral liabilities persist? Start with Cenozoic planktonic foraminifera,
comparing individual parent–daughter events. The result would change whether
species age is interpreted as an autonomous biological clock.

**Closest priors and residual.** Doran et al. already studied foram age-dependent
extinction ([10.2110/palo.2006.p06-055r](https://doi.org/10.2110/palo.2006.p06-055r),
official abstract). Ezard et al. show that species concepts change inferred
diversification ([10.1098/rsbl.2011.0699](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsbl.2011.0699),
author-repository abstract). Clade Replacement Theory already connects age
selectivity with incumbency and environmental change
([10.1093/jeb/voae012](https://doi.org/10.1093/jeb/voae012), abstract and indexed
theory sections). The fresh scout independently proposed inheritance at
speciation, but the parent's register check shows that **Q-012 already owns
most of this target**, including relatives' survival under matched marginal
distributions. Its [new prior audit](../literature/2026-10-14-q012-prior-check.md)
also includes rate-inheritance and frailty precedents. Do not count D1 as an
independent discovery. Its useful refinement is to distinguish biological
renewal at budding from anagenetic naming and persistent-parent histories.

**Route and capability.** Lamyman et al.'s new ancestor/range/ecology compilation
([10.1080/14772019.2026.2669255](https://doi.org/10.1080/14772019.2026.2669255),
UCL abstract/metadata and indexed data description) makes an event-level audit
possible. Zenodo metadata for the [supplements](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.18854731)
and [database](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.18854730) were accessible; each ZIP
exceeds 1 GB and neither was downloaded. Exact species counts differ between
accessible descriptions and should await version reconciliation. Triton is
held in EPRP, but joint event support remains unchecked.

**Observation and test.** Simulate alternative inheritance fractions with
matched marginal species durations. Compare daughters from different parental
histories at similar geological times, allowing shared environment and
geography. Taxonomic splitting, uncertain ancestry, dissolution, size selection
and uneven core coverage generate the recorded events. Compiled ancestry is
not independent proof of biological speciation. Conditioning on a parent that
survives to bud creates selection that the comparison must represent.
Residual parent–daughter dependence supports inherited state only if shared
exposure and naming cannot explain it; a sufficiently precise absence supports
renewal under the tested conditions. Stop if event assignment is definition-driven
or the rivals remain observationally equivalent. Next owner: Claude's Q-012
design, with this event contrast offered as a component.

## D2. Can juvenile viability block otherwise useful adult innovations?

**Question and consequence.** Ammonoid evolution repeatedly explored similar
adult designs. Were alternatives absent because they performed poorly as
adults, or because evolutionary transitions between complete growth histories
passed through nonviable juveniles? This asks whether convergence reflects
adult adaptation or restrictions on the routes evolution can take. Start with
Devonian–Jurassic ammonoid growth, not a universal law for all body plans.

**Closest priors and residual.** Adult functional trade-offs are established
territory: Tendler et al.
([10.1186/s12918-015-0149-z](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12918-015-0149-z), accessible
abstract/discussion). Klug et al. already compare juvenile and adult morphospace
([10.4202/app.00220.2015](https://doi.org/10.4202/app.00220.2015), full PDF sections).
Walton and Korn document repeated growth programmes across 177 species
([10.1111/pala.12308](https://doi.org/10.1111/pala.12308), deposit abstract and
inventory). Peterman et al. evaluate hydrostatics across a complex ontogeny
([10.1371/journal.pone.0235180](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0235180),
relevant full methods). General developmental accessibility is also occupied
by Gerber's trilobite work
([10.1111/ede.12098](https://doi.org/10.1111/ede.12098), publisher summary).
The provisional residual is whether **sets of viable complete growth programmes
are evolutionarily disconnected**, with barriers that predict actual innovations
and reversals. Including juveniles or rediscovering recurrent ontogenies alone
would not supply it.

**Route and capability.** The [Walton–Korn workbook manifest](https://doi.org/10.5061/dryad.c17p8)
is accessible and lists size-resolved shell measurements; contents remain
uninspected. Existing shell-growth mechanics
([10.1002/jez.b.22716](https://doi.org/10.1002/jez.b.22716), repository abstract)
and virtual hydrostatics permit ensembles of complete developing shells. Begin
with construction and hydrostatic bounds. Vary growth controls between
generations and compare adult-only with all-stage constraints. A fossil growth
curve supplies phenotypic geometry, not ancestral genetics or mutation kernels.

**Observation, rival and test.** Missing inner whorls/body chambers, compaction,
section-plane errors, dimorphism and hydrodynamic sorting can manufacture growth
differences. Size is not age. The strongest rival is ordinary stage-specific
selection and scaling with no additional barrier between growth programmes.
Require barriers to survive several generators, soft-body reconstructions,
evolutionary step sizes and observation models, and predict held-out transitions.
Do not conflate an individual's ontogenetic path with a path between generations.
Allow drift and changing selection; a barrier created solely by an arbitrary
hard cutoff is uninformative. Plentiful viable routes, or equal prediction from
the scaling model, would weaken the developmental-route account. Park historical
inference if juveniles cannot be linked to adults or uncertain tissues erase
discrimination. Next: one physically bounded contrast and a small data audit;
links to Q-036/Q-050 concern development but do not duplicate their targets.

## D3. Did burrowers change the conditions under which deep-sea ecosystems could persist?

**Question and consequence.** Could sediment ventilation let established
Palaeozoic benthos survive oxygen conditions under which uncolonised sediment
could not first be invaded? Different establishment and collapse thresholds
would make deep-sea habitability depend on biological history. Animals would
partly construct their ecological opportunity, without producing oxygen or
overcoming completely anoxic bottom water. Start with Ordovician–Devonian
deep-water sections within the broader early Palaeozoic transition.

**Closest priors and residual.** Buatois et al. already propose bioirrigation
feedback in their large trace-fossil compilation
([10.1073/pnas.2414752122](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2414752122), abstract and
data statement). Bubphamanee et al. link deeper-water expansion to Devonian
oxygenation ([10.1073/pnas.2501342122](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2501342122),
indexed primary redox sections; full SI uninspected). Sediment alternative
states already have a mechanistic precedent
([10.1016/j.gca.2020.02.021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.gca.2020.02.021), abstract
and indexed passages). Cribb et al. already quantify engineer-associated
Phanerozoic diversity
([10.1038/s41467-026-73628-6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-73628-6), relevant
full text). The residual is a historically constrained test of **establishment
versus persistence**, not generic engineering, positive feedback or higher
richness near engineers. This is a narrower biological route related to Q-010;
it need not estimate a diversity ceiling.

**Route and capability.** The Buatois supplement is declared available but was
not downloaded; its section compilation is a discovery map, not verified joint
support. Locate independently dated sections with burrow architecture,
associated benthos, sedimentology and water-column redox evidence. Simulate
oxygen, organic matter and sulphide with animal irrigation and colonisation;
compare feedback and external-forcing-only models under the same forcing.
Aggregated ichnology and geochemistry make local historical comparisons
plausible; ensembles can expose the counterfactual without fitting an arbitrary
capacity curve. No ready ancient-system simulator was verified.

**Observation, rival and test.** Preserved burrows measure behaviour rather than
animal biomass. Erosion, event beds and later mixing distort sequences; engineers
may alter the redox proxy itself. Independent water-column and porewater
conditions must not be conflated. External oxygenation, organic delivery and
grain size may explain both burrowing and associated fauna; enhanced respiration
could offset irrigation. Support needs a robust threshold asymmetry plus
observed persistence under independently constrained external conditions.
If realistic feedback never creates a consequential difference, or forcing alone
predicts the history, the negative answer matters. Stop empirical development
if proxy response and assembly order cannot separate the rivals. Next: a small
section-support and mechanistic-prior brief, coordinated with Q-010.

## D4. Did reefs export new ecological capabilities to surrounding habitats?

**Question and consequence.** Did reef-origin lineages establish previously
absent roles in recipient shelf communities, or mostly add taxa to roles already
present? The alternatives distinguish reefs as sources of taxonomic diversity
from engines of wider ecosystem assembly. Use several Phanerozoic intervals
with strong reef/nonreef records before considering a whole-era analysis.

**Closest priors and residual.** Kiessling, Simpson and Foote already establish
taxonomic cradle/export patterns
([10.1126/science.1182241](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1182241), full report).
Knope et al. already quantify broad functional differentiation
([10.1038/ncomms7455](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms7455), relevant full methods
and results). The residual is **recipient-region assembly by exported functional
novelty**, rather than global functional richness or taxonomic export alone.
Novelty remains unverified, especially in the onshore–offshore literature.

**Route and capability.** Link PBDB habitat histories to feeding, tiering and
mobility traits, while distinguishing innovations from dispersal. EPRP has used
PBDB extracts; this joint extract is unbuilt. Knope's supplementary-data links
were visible but retrieval failed. Simulate passive exchange from a shared
trait pool against reef-associated innovation and export, matching regional
taxon supply, habitat area, substrate and dispersal opportunities. The combined
resources could test what exported taxa changed, beyond counting arrivals.

**Observation, rival and test.** A reef first occurrence does not establish
origin. Facies shifts, reef-derived transported shells, uneven sampling and
genus-level ecological heterogeneity can invent directionality. The main rival
is that many exported taxa automatically include more traits, while recipient
environmental change independently opens roles. Test whether reef-origin
lineages first establish recipient capabilities beyond that taxonomic-supply
null. Define the consequential role contrast before looking at arrival results;
coarse feeding categories may record redundancy while missing real innovations.
Failure to exceed the null would limit the ecosystem-assembly claim without
denying reef export. Park if arrival order or useful trait resolution is
unrecoverable. Next: targeted novelty and one-region trait-linkage audit;
Q-009/Q-033 share assembly themes but not this source-to-recipient contrast.

## D5. When did terrestrial vertebrate communities become energetically independent of water?

**Question and consequence.** Did Carboniferous–early Permian large faunivores
need aquatic prey subsidies long after locomotion permitted life on land?
An energetic shoreline constraint would separate anatomical terrestrialisation
from autonomy of terrestrial food webs. Start with a few assemblages around
330–280 Ma, retaining detrital, fungal, microfaunal and arthropod pathways.
The absence of large vertebrate herbivores never implies absent herbivory.

**Closest priors and residual.** Direct evidence already identifies early
terrestrial feeding: Edwards et al.
([10.2110/palo.2011.p11-094r](https://doi.org/10.2110/palo.2011.p11-094r), selected
full methods/results) and Young et al.
([10.1038/s41598-026-38183-6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-38183-6), relevant
full text; predation versus scavenging unresolved). Qvarnström et al. reconstruct
connected aquatic/terrestrial Triassic webs
([10.1038/s41586-024-08265-4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08265-4), relevant
full text/data statement). Boyce's stoichiometric proposal
([10.3389/fevo.2023.1304831](https://doi.org/10.3389/fevo.2023.1304831)) is a
conceptual lead, not independent evidence for necessity. The residual is an
explicit test of **energetic necessity and its spatial consequences**, not
discovery of aquatic feeding or delayed terrestrial food-web assembly.

**Route and capability.** Use body sizes, alternative diet assignments, direct
digestive evidence and flora in spatial carbon/nitrogen budgets. Set aquatic
subsidy to zero or allow it; vary feeding area, metabolism and trophic efficiency.
Qvarnström's [public supplements](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.26103031)
and image-stack declarations were checked, not downloaded. Young's data require
a request; none was made. No adequate Paleozoic standing-biomass panel was found
in this bounded search. Dietary imaging and spatial simulation enable a
counterfactual, but do not supply missing ancient fluxes.

**Observation, rival and test.** Aquatic deposition, transport and selective
preservation of fish-rich digestive remains can make terrestrial animals seem
water-dependent. Diet links constrain possible consumption, not fractions or
biomass. A terrestrial-only budget accommodating credible consumer densities
would reject subsidy necessity while allowing piscivory. A robust lower bound
on required aquatic energy would predict a shoreline-distance/body-size pattern.
But mere occurrence permits arbitrarily sparse consumers: fossil frequencies
cannot silently become density. Stop historical necessity claims if feeding
area and density remain unconstrained. Retain only a conditional theory of the
boundary in that case. Next: determine whether any independent evidence can
reject the budgets. Q-046's density warning applies, with a different biological
target.

## D6. Does a dispersal strategy's evolutionary value reverse with habitat geometry?

**Question and consequence.** Restricted larval dispersal may promote divergence
while preventing colonisation. Does its effect on durable lineage production
reverse with habitat spacing and persistence? Target late Mesozoic–Palaeogene
gastropods and bryozoans as separate initial systems. The prize is a mechanism
for why similar life histories have different macroevolutionary consequences.

**Closest priors and residual.** Duda and Palumbi show that repeated trait
transitions can mimic species selection
([10.1073/pnas.96.18.10272](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.96.18.10272), abstract/author
PDF). Jablonski and Hunt distinguish larval mode from species-level range effects
([10.1086/507994](https://doi.org/10.1086/507994), abstract/intro). Friend et al.
already favour geographic contingency in Volutospina
([10.1017/pab.2020.60](https://doi.org/10.1017/pab.2020.60), relevant full sections).
Leventhal and Samuels-Fair find a time-restricted brooding/origination association
([10.1017/pab.2024.63](https://doi.org/10.1017/pab.2024.63), abstract/intro).
The residual is a **predicted spatial crossover**, not another trait/rate
coefficient or a new claim that geography matters. General spatial-speciation
theory is a serious occupancy risk and needs a closer check.

**Route and capability.** Build metapopulation simulations where successful
establishment, gene flow and differentiation respond to patch geometry. Begin
with generic landscapes; fossil distributions do not recover precise currents
or dispersal kernels. Friend's tables/phylogeny are available in the article.
The bryozoan [deposit manifest](https://doi.org/10.5061/dryad.1vhhmgr44) lists
occurrence, brooding and taxonomic files; contents and reuse terms are unchecked.
Do not pool genus-level brooding with species-level gastropod transitions.
Combined trait/occurrence compilations could test predicted reversals across
independent origins, provided suitable palaeohabitat geometry can be recovered.

**Observation, rival and test.** Missing protoconchs, undetected brood chambers,
taxonomic averaging and outcrop-limited ranges bias state and geography.
Non-planktotrophy is not identical to direct development; rafting also matters.
Intrinsic trait effects, transition supply and extinction bottlenecks are the
main rivals. Predefine contrasting establishment/gene-flow regimes and require
repeatable changes in sign, rather than choosing a scale after seeing the data.
Stable effects across well-supported geometries would weaken the crossover
account. Stop if transitions or relevant spatial contrasts cannot be resolved.
Next: theory-prior and geometry-support checks. Q-020/Q-029 are nearby, but
restricted dispersal interacting with geometry is a distinct proposed mechanism.

## D7. Can species sorting restrain reproductive liabilities continually regenerated within lineages?

**Question and consequence.** Can differential species loss counteract
individual-level selection that repeatedly increases costly reproductive
investment? Or does the apparent balance simply track changing environmental
optima? Late Cretaceous–Palaeogene cytheroid ostracods offer an initial fossil
anchor for a multilevel evolutionary question, not a universal proxy for
sexual selection.

**Closest priors and residual.** Martins et al.
([10.1038/s41586-018-0020-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-018-0020-7), relevant
full methods/text) already test **both extinction and speciation** against male
investment. The extinction–origination trade-off cannot be claimed as new.
Their later paper documents post-K/Pg dimorphism changes and discusses
environmental explanations
([10.1098/rspb.2020.0730](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2020.0730), relevant full
sections). The residual is whether opposing **within-lineage generation and
between-lineage removal** produce distinguishable transient signatures. Its
novelty against multilevel-selection theory remains unverified.

**Route and capability.** Simulate trait change, inheritance at speciation,
ordinary and pulse extinction, and moving optima. Ask which mechanisms yield
the same aggregate dimorphism but different within-lineage trajectories. The
[2020 supplement](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.c.5082855) has accessible
metadata listing small trait tables; the raw
[Dryad deposit](https://doi.org/10.5061/dryad.tx95x69vr) was identified but not
retrieved. New simulation capacity helps map equivalent mechanisms; it does not
make pre/post distributions into longitudinal observations.

**Observation, rival and test.** Sex assignment, juvenile mixtures, valve
orientation, preservation, immigration and formation aggregation affect the
dimorphism signal. Carapace geometry measures neither mating competition nor
selection directly. Repeated positive within-lineage changes together with
preferential loss of high-investment species would support opposing fluxes;
synchronous shifts with environmental change favour moving optima. Fossil
covariation alone cannot establish the individual-level cause. Park empirical
promotion if repeated populations/phylogenetic links are inadequate; park theory
if models differ only in freely fitted rates and have no distinct prediction.
Next: one theory novelty check and a repeated-measurement inventory. Q-050
concerns evolvability, not this between-level opposition.

## D8. Did flowering plants replace forests by changing the rules of establishment?

**Question and consequence.** Were Cretaceous transitions to angiosperm
dominance mainly responses to changing climate and physiology, or did growing
angiosperm abundance alter recruitment conditions enough to make replacement
self-maintaining? The distinction concerns external opportunity versus a
historically contingent transformation of terrestrial ecosystems. Dominance
means vegetation abundance/structure here, not numbers of described taxa.

**Closest priors and residual.** This is emphatically not a new feedback
hypothesis. Berendse and Scheffer explicitly proposed nutrient-driven alternative
states ([10.1111/j.1461-0248.2009.01342.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1461-0248.2009.01342.x),
abstract and indexed model/discussion passages; repeated PMC access failed).
Bond's regeneration-niche explanation is older still
([10.1111/j.1095-8312.1989.tb00492.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1095-8312.1989.tb00492.x),
abstract). Bres et al. already model CO2-dependent hydraulic/photosynthetic
advantages and explicitly propose dynamic vegetation and nitrogen extensions
([10.5194/bg-18-5729-2021](https://doi.org/10.5194/bg-18-5729-2021), relevant full
methods/discussion). Coiffard et al. document phased environmental expansion
([10.1073/pnas.1218633110](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1218633110), abstract).
The proposed residual is **discriminating old explanations through their
replacement and reversal predictions**, rather than claiming a new mechanism
or just building a more detailed vegetation model. Novelty remains unverified.

**Route and capability.** Combine fossil leaf traits, vegetation composition
and climate reconstructions in demographic competition models with feedback
switched on/off. Compare establishment when rare with persistence when common;
include ordinary demographic lags and disturbance. The Bres full article and
supplement links are accessible; EPRP has no assembled Cretaceous demographic,
soil-nutrient or vegetation-abundance panel. Leaf-vein compilations and published
model descriptions are starting resources, not that missing panel. Ensembles
could identify contrasting regional and temporal predictions before attempting
a continental reconstruction.

**Observation, rival and test.** Pollen production/dispersal, leaf transport,
facies and organ taxonomy mean fossil counts do not directly measure canopy
dominance. Trait-based CO2 estimates must not be reused as independent forcing
without their shared uncertainty. Climatic filtering, fast regeneration and
demographic lag can all mimic persistent replacement. Require a predicted
asymmetry or regional history contrast that survives those alternatives and
is supported by independent forcing; persistence alone does not establish
hysteresis. If climate/physiology models explain establishment and reversal
without feedback, that constrains its necessary historical role. Stop empirical
development if nutrient and recruitment freedom can fit either history.
Next: a rival-prediction/evidence table. This connects to Q-010/Q-033 but adds
an explicitly terrestrial replacement problem; it should not become a second
generic competition question.
