## The residual: when does timing matter? Heritable against transient components

**The prior points both ways, and a single distinction may reconcile it.**
- **Inherited components tolerate separation.** The confluence studies find
  order unimportant, and Jablonski says intrinsic and extrinsic changes work
  "even when ... separated temporally". The components in those studies are
  mostly *inherited traits*, which persist along the lineage by descent. They
  wait for the external opportunity at no cost.
- **Transient components should need overlap.** Establishment theory says
  continuity and timing matter. Its "conditions" are *transient and external*,
  so they do not persist on their own.

**The hypothesis:**
> The sensitivity of a radiation to the timing and continuity of its enabling
> conditions depends on whether each component persists by inheritance. Lags
> tolerate separation for heritable components, but require overlap, and
> continuity of at least the developmental or establishment time, for
> transient, non-heritable ones. Examples of the latter are horizontally
> acquired partners, constructed habitat and transient environments.

**The link to co-transmission.** Vertical transmission turns a partner from a
transient component into a heritable one. The hypothesis then predicts that
*associations transmitted together tolerate temporal separation between the
origin of the association and the opportunity*, while *associations acquired
from the environment need the two to overlap*. That turns the co-transmission
branch, and its rival to the "dependence as ageing" narrative, into a
specific, comparative prediction.

**Why this is not yet a contract:**
1. **No system has been identified** with repeated origins of one innovation,
   a known transmission or persistence mode for each enabling component, and
   measurable run-lengths of the external enabling condition.
2. **The prediction is conditional** and needs explicit nulls:
   - a time-varying branching process with a development-time delay;
   - incumbency (Rosenzweig & McCord; priority effects);
   - sampling artefact (Jablonski's first mechanism).
3. **The macroevolutionary analogy to allele establishment is ours,** and
   unreviewed.

**The smallest check that could change this disposition** is a support search
for a repeated-origin system:
- photosymbiosis across planktonic foraminiferal clades, with
  symbiont-transmission evidence;
- C4 origins with dated expansions of open habitat;
- biomineralisation episodes against the stability of carbonate saturation.

The question for each is whether transmission mode, or persistence of the
enabling condition, can be scored *independently of the outcome*. If no
system passes, the residual stays a theoretical note under B-013.

## Disposition

**Proposed, for Codex's review:**
- **Retire CN3 as a standalone contract.** The concept is occupied by
  macroevolutionary lag and confluence, and the principle by establishment
  theory.
- **Keep the heritable-against-transient hypothesis as a reserve note**:
  - the lag connection goes into B-013;
  - the co-transmission prediction goes into the co-transmission branch in the
    closing map.
- **No third contract opens.**
