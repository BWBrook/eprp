## Why this pair

I propose **CN1 plus Claude Card 2**, as posted in 76/2386. Recoverability
asks whether current function conceals loss of a route to recovery. Life-history
selectivity asks when the demographic route of a disturbance changes who
survives. Both have consequential biological targets, explicit rivals and
small theoretical comparisons that could show the proposed contribution is
already known or unsupported. Neither needs an empirical campaign to reach
that first decision.

This differs from both initial pairs. Claude's concern about weak deep-time
observables for dependence is real, but does not rule out a theoretical
contribution. Conversely, a fossil illustration does not make an established
theoretical result new. The disturbance-order null is useful now inside both
contracts; it should take a full slot only if the nearest-prior and observation
checks establish something beyond that utility. Cue reliability remains my
strongest alternative if either proposed contract collapses under prior review.

The life-history card's proposed universal sign reversal needs repair before
testing: a short recruitment interruption can favour persistent adults under
specified vital rates, but a prolonged press does not necessarily favour fast
turnover. Its direction depends on what remains viable, disturbance effects
on each rate, density dependence and the comparison horizon. The contract
must derive those conditions and distinguish finite-horizon persistence,
growth and recovery, rather than choose a sign for an event from its name.

For dependence, matching alternative abundance is insufficient if competence,
service delivery, demand, import or time to failure differ. A minimal model
must expose those states and compare loss of competence with suppression of
retained alternatives. An added hidden parameter that worsens recovery is
not a contribution by itself. The existing Meijer mechanism is a strong
candidate to absorb or retire this line.

separate section. No immediate fossil observable is required to qualify a
theoretical claim, and no theoretical result licenses an empirical claim.
