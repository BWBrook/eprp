## Where I agree, and where I'd push

I accept both of Codex's lines. My challenge has three parts:
- **an order:** the geological construction of causes first;
- **a sharper form** of the hidden-state line;
- **one shared test case** on which both lines make rival claims.

I would keep the innovation window as the alternative, and I would not bring
back a different branch. The shared test case below is G1 returning, and
that is enough.

### Line 1: the geological construction of apparent causes

**Why it could change the most.** Nearly every major transition in the
textbook carries a named trigger, and most of those triggers were named
because they coincide with a boundary:
- a cooling step, or meteorite dust, for the Ordovician radiation;
- oxygenation for the Cambrian;
- volcanism for the Capitanian.

If some of those coincidences are made by which witnesses survived, the
correction is not to a magnitude but to the *causal history* that people
teach. Few answers in this round could change more.

**Nearest prior (strong).** Holland & Patzkowsky 2015 already show that last
occurrences cluster at sequence-stratigraphic surfaces even under constant
extinction [checked]. Elevated extinction sharpens those clusters, and every
mass extinction except the end-Cretaceous shows them. They propose three
tests to separate a brief pulse from stratigraphic concentration [checked
that the tests exist, not their content]. Holland 1995 is the root of this
work [memory]. Shen et al. 2020 apply the same logic to the North American
Capitanian [checked, via a review summary].

So the timing and magnitude of extinctions are well-occupied ground. What
looks open is two steps beyond them:
- **from timing to cause:** the environmental context of the surviving
  package becomes the explanation;
- **from extinctions to radiations:** a radiation's apparent start snaps to
  the oldest surviving package.

A prior check should test exactly that.

**Strongest rival: common cause, in Peters' sense.** The environmental change
really did make both the package boundary and the biological event. Its
first-order prediction is identical, an event at the package base, so
coincidence alone can never decide between them.

**The observation that would.** Look for *diachrony against local witnesses*:
- **witnesses make the date:** the inferred event moves with each region's
  own package boundary;
- **common cause or real biology:** the event holds in absolute time across
  regions whose package boundaries differ.

Eustatic boundaries are synchronous, so the informative regions are those
whose packages are set by local tectonics. Holland & Patzkowsky's three tests
may already include this. Settling that is part of the brief.

### Line 2: hidden biological state, made sharper

**Recast the question.** Is the biosphere's response to a shock *Markov in
its observable state*?
- **If yes:** history matters only through what can be seen at the onset,
  namely occupancy, ranges, composition and environmental baseline.
  Ecological memory reduces to state, and Codex's contrary answer becomes
  the main result.
- **If no:** some state is hidden. The candidates are the worlds already on
  the table: a spent store, a filtered composition, reorganised dependencies
  and broken partnerships.

**The test in principle** is a conditional-independence question. Once a
lineage's observable state at the onset of S2 is given, does knowing its
history through S1 still improve the prediction of its fate?

**Nearest prior (partial).** Alroy 2008 found that a drop in diversity is
followed by low extinction, and that high extinction is followed by high
origination [checked]. That is history acting through observable state,
which is the Markov null at work. Range buffering in mass extinctions (Payne
& Finnegan 2007) and its Triassic–Jurassic counterpart (Kiessling & Aberhan
2007) bear on world 4 [memory]. Balmford 1996 supplies the filter [checked].

**Partnerships are more observable than either of us assumed.** Interaction
traces preserve associations directly:
- **Labandeira, Johnson & Wilf 2002:** 51 plant–insect associations scored on
  13,441 leaves across the K–Pg in North Dakota. The specialised associations
  almost disappeared at the boundary and failed to recover [checked].
- **Wilf et al. 2006:** insect damage and plant diversity were decoupled for
  one to two million years afterwards [checked].
- **Other interaction traces:** predatory drill holes, trematode traces in
  bivalves, epibionts, and growth form or isotopes as evidence of
  photosymbiosis [memory].

So Codex's partnership worlds A–C are not pure imagination. There is a
natural experiment in which the host plant lineages largely persisted and
their specialist associations did not.

**Strongest rival for partnerships: an invisible taxon went extinct.** The
specialised associations vanished because the specialist insects died out,
and insects are rarely preserved as body fossils. That is taxonomic loss we
cannot see, not reorganisation among survivors. Separating "broken
organisation" from "unseen partner extinction" is the crux of the
partnership branch. It is also why the line is interesting: the evidence
exists, and its reading is contested.

### The shared test case: the Phanerozoic decline in background turnover

**One canonical pattern sits exactly where the two lines meet.** Background
extinction and origination rates decline through the Phanerozoic. Alroy 2008
reports the decline and cautions that taxonomic biases may lie behind it
[checked]. Raup & Sepkoski 1982 is the classic statement [memory].

Three readings compete:
- **Hidden state (G1 maturing):** the biosphere accumulated robustness.
- **Geological construction:** younger intervals have more and better
  witnesses, so observed ranges lengthen and apparent turnover falls.
- **Taxonomic practice:** Alroy's own caution.

Each reading makes a different claim about the same curve. **I propose it as
the case on which both lines are compared, not as a project.** If neither
line has anything distinctive to say about the decline, that is itself
informative about how far each reaches.

### The alternative: the innovation window

**Why it could change the most.** Some absences from morphospace would be
accidents of timing. A *missed radiation* is the counterfactual of a world
that never happened.

**Nearest prior.** Accounts of the Cambrian explosion as a coincidence of
genetic, environmental and ecological conditions are common (Marshall 2006
[memory]). So the coincidence framing alone changes little. The missed
radiation is the new part.

**Strongest rival: incumbency.** Viable forms are absent because occupied
roles exclude them, as in incumbent replacement (Rosenzweig & McCord 1991
[memory]), not because the conditions never coincided.

**The observation that would decide.** After an incumbent is removed, does
the "missing" form appear promptly, which favours incumbency, or only when
the other conditions next coincide, which favours timing? Crises are the
natural experiments, which ties this branch back to story 2 without merging
the two.
