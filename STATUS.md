  Their checks establish documentation/metadata access, not data suitability.
- Claude's initial literature leads were checked at metadata/abstract depth.

- Song et al. explicitly acknowledge temporal-resolution limits, so a proposed
  contribution cannot rest on claiming they overlooked them. Their joint
  magnitude/rate argument needs a more careful critique than rate exceedance alone.
- INDITEK carries richness on geological particles; SNCI redraws niches in each
  environmental snapshot. Neither carries identifiable fossil lineages. Source
  inspection found calibration-driver and connectivity issues requiring checks
  before reuse; effects on published results remain untested. Shared source
  families do not establish identical calibration targets.
- Instantaneous environmental tracking, finite-rate state dynamics and additional
  history dependence are distinct hypotheses. A small synthetic probe shows
  that shared observation errors and smoothing can create apparent delayed
  rebound even with constant true richness; it is not an empirical bias estimate.
- The physiology-transfer candidate needs a thermal-exposure comparator and
  frozen calibration choices. Magnitude is not automatically portable across
  observation windows. A failed simulation fit is not proof of structural
  nonidentifiability.
- The [PhanDA source audit](literature/2026-10-08-phanda-ensemble-contract.md)
  confirms pooled fits, filtering and resampling. Matching member positions do
  not certify temporal trajectories; independence is a sensitivity assumption,
  not a general uncertainty bound. The interval crosswalk remains descriptive.

  stand. Four signs have separate >95% lower bounds under every coupling of the
  deposited empirical marginals. This does not identify a temporal posterior.

  claim; Claude reconciled the stale paragraphs and provenance on 10 October.
- Codex's exact toy-tree check confirms that richness-matched PD nulls can have
  zero variance, including complete clade loss and one survivor on a rooted
  ultrametric tree. These cases have undefined standardised effect sizes. The
  empirical pre-mortem is complete. Its pooled background percentiles are
  descriptive. Matched on lineage count and loss fraction, all eight testable
  cases sit above the matched median (+0.1 to +1.7 SES), with three at the
  matched 95th percentile. The 1.5–2.7 SES detectable-shift calculation is an
  illustrative Gaussian approximation, not validated power. Ages are not
  matched; cases are correlated. Revised calibration separates exchangeable-tip
  loss from an incremental change beyond ordinary clustered loss; not run.
- Q-026 asks whether declining clades' recovery can be predicted without
  conditioning on their eventual fate. Quental & Marshall 2013 already analyse
  rate contributions to rebounds within decline. Song 2026's reporting summary,
  supplement, author responses and selected code reveal an extinct-clade cohort
  and full-history smoothing; final main Methods remain gated. Surviving
  denominator groups do not supply the prospective comparison cohort. A small
  birth–death calculation shows higher turnover at equal net decline can raise
  both first-rebound and first-extinction probabilities over the same horizon.
  Proportional richness alone establishes neither absolute decline nor dominance.
- Q-026's new analytical check adds one-for-one replacement: fossil appearance
  and termination rates increase while lineage-count first-passage probabilities
  remain unchanged. This is a constructed example, not an empirical correction.
  Richness increments only bypass that coding problem for appropriate standing
  counts; sampled-in-bin richness can contain successive names for one lineage.
  Confirmed persistence and first threshold crossing require different models.
- Q-026's [boundary-observation probe](data/q026-boundary-observation/README.md)
  accepts the ideal count solution but separates latent richness from observed
  range-through counts. Incomplete detection and replacement change the latter
  at constant true richness. A deterministic fixture shows those retrospective
  counts can depend on fossils after the prediction landmark. No three-timer,
  capture–recapture or joint lineage/observation model was fitted. This identifies
  a data requirement, not an empirical bias estimate or a failed method.
